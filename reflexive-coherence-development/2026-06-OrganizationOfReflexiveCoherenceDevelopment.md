# Organization of Reflexive Coherence Development
## From Task Execution to Condition-Oriented Practice

**Date:** 2026-06-24

Copyright © 2026 Uroš Jovanovič, CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Abstract

*Attunement of Becoming* changed the unit of sight. A cultivation loop is not an isolated episode but a local aperture into a continuous, path-dependent, scale-coupled field. Loops integrate into trajectories; trajectories integrate into regimes; regimes interact as ecologies; and the observer is a scale-calibrated participant whose probes, supports, withdrawals, classifications, and integrations become part of the field they observe.

*Aims of Reflexive Coherence Development* changed the unit of direction. Development is still oriented, but it is not oriented primarily toward terminal configurations. It is condition-teleological: it moves toward regimes of possibility in which desired emergent properties can arise, stabilize, vary, recover, transfer, be revised, and become native. Arrival remains real, but arrival means entering a condition envelope rather than occupying a final snapshot.

This paper asks what happens to **organization** after those two shifts.

If development is understood as movement toward a predefined end-state, organization can be understood as objective decomposition: define the target, break it into tasks, assign roles, sequence work, monitor metrics, and declare completion. This remains powerful and often appropriate when the relevant objects, success criteria, roles, outputs, and evaluation surfaces are already well formed. Reflexive Coherence Development does not reject task-objective organization. It names its domain.

The gap appears when the aim is not merely to produce an output but to cultivate conditions under which a desired property can become native. In that domain, task completion may produce the sign without forming the capacity. A role assignment may produce coordination without role differentiation becoming a basin. A meeting may produce agreement while degrading the shared medium. A scaffold may produce performance while increasing naturalization debt. An agent may appear goal-directed while the producer state supplies the missing agency. An organization may reach the objective while weakening the conditions needed for future coherence.

This paper develops **Organization of Reflexive Coherence Development** as the discipline of arranging activity, support, boundary, rhythm, role, shared medium, withdrawal, and integration around condition formation.

The central claim is:

```text
Once an aim is understood as a condition regime, organization cannot be reduced
only to task execution toward an endpoint. It must become the arrangement of
conditions through which desired emergent properties can become native.
```

From this shift, several consequences follow. Tasks become local instruments inside cultivation moves. Roles become basin functions rather than only responsibility labels. Coordination occurs through shared media as much as through messages. Explicit scaffolds and producer states become naturalization debt when the aim requires native expression. Rhythm becomes a developmental condition rather than a scheduling convenience. Agency becomes something organization either cultivates, suppresses, simulates, extracts, or distributes.

This paper forms the third ground of Reflexive Coherence Development. Attunement changes sight. Aims change direction. Organization changes practice. Once practice is reorganized around condition formation, measurement itself must change; otherwise the practice will be pulled back into endpoint scoring. That is the work of the next paper.

## 1. Why Organization Must Change After Attunement and Aims

Attunement changed where the observer stands.

The observer is not outside a system, choosing a future state and applying neutral operations to move the system there. The observer is embedded in the field they claim to develop. Their orientation affects what becomes visible. Their classifications affect what becomes sayable. Their probes supply supports. Their withdrawals alter trust, boundary elasticity, and expression. Their integrations become part of the trajectory that later loops inherit.

Aims changed what direction means inside that field.

The aim is not merely a desired terminal configuration. It is a directional commitment toward a condition regime. The work is not only to make a state appear, but to cultivate the conditions under which a desired emergent property can arise, stabilize, vary, recover, transfer, be revised, and become native without constant external forcing.

Once these two shifts are accepted, organization cannot remain unchanged.

A task-oriented plan assumes that the desired result can be decomposed into known parts. The work is divided. Responsibilities are assigned. Dependencies are sequenced. Outputs are delivered. Metrics track progress. This is a valid and often excellent form when the world is sufficiently predefined. If the bridge must meet a known specification, if the software feature has stable acceptance criteria, if the manufacturing process has known tolerances, if the legal filing has a defined structure, if the emergency protocol has a clear safe state, then task-objective organization is not a mistake. It is a disciplined way of arranging action around known closure.

Reflexive Coherence Development begins where that closure is not yet the right object.

The difficulty appears when the desired result is not merely an output but a capacity, relation, pattern, identity, agency, practice, or regime that must become native. Under these conditions, a task can be completed while development has not occurred. The visible output may appear, but the condition that would let it reappear without the scaffold remains absent.

A team can hold a retrospective and still not develop repair capacity.

A student can complete a set of exercises and still not develop representation capacity.

A person can feel calm in a protected session and still not develop recovery capacity under perturbation.

A research group can publish results and still not develop a generative inquiry regime.

An artificial agent can produce role-specialized behavior and still not develop native role differentiation.

A community can adopt a governance process and still not develop the shared medium through which disagreement can be metabolized.

In each case, the task was not meaningless. The task may have been necessary. The problem is not that the task was done. The problem is that the task was mistaken for the development.

This is the organizational gap.

When the aim is a condition regime, the central organizational question is not only:

```text
What tasks must be completed?
```

It becomes:

```text
What arrangement of activity, support, boundary, rhythm, role, shared medium,
withdrawal, and integration would allow the desired property to become native?
```

This question changes the object of organization.

Organization is no longer only the arrangement of people, agents, tasks, resources, and deadlines around output production. It becomes the arrangement of conditions under which coherent becoming can continue. It must ask what the activity is doing to the field that receives the next activity. It must track whether supports are becoming native or hidden. It must distinguish role performance from role basin formation. It must care about the medium through which coordination occurs. It must know when to withdraw, when to supply, when to stabilize, when to let a bifurcation proceed, and when an aim must be redescribed.

This does not make organization vague. It makes organization more precise about its developmental object.

A task plan can say:

```text
Run the session.
Assign the role.
Complete the report.
Ship the feature.
Resolve the issue.
Update the policy.
```

A condition-oriented organization asks:

```text
What capacity did the session make more native?
What dependency did the role assignment hide or reduce?
What boundary did the report disclose?
What future becoming did the feature enable or constrain?
What support ecology did the issue reveal?
What regime condition did the policy stabilize or rigidify?
```

The same activity can be organized in either frame. A meeting can be a task, or it can be a cultivation move. A role can be a responsibility label, or it can be a basin function. A metric can be a completion signal, or it can be a claim-ceiling instrument. A plan can be a schedule of outputs, or it can be a choreography of supports, probes, withdrawals, and integrations.

The shift is not from structure to looseness. It is from output-centered structure to condition-centered structure.

This matters because open-ended development is easily captured by the forms of predefined organization. Once an aim is translated into tasks, the task surface becomes visible and manageable. The condition surface becomes background. People can report progress while the desired property remains scaffolded. Agents can satisfy outputs while producer state carries the missing coherence. Institutions can optimize delivery while depleting trust, reserve, or shared-medium readability. A personal practice can complete repetitions while never naturalizing the capacity it was meant to cultivate.

Organization must therefore become capable of seeing what task execution hides.

It must ask what supports are being supplied from outside. It must ask which boundaries are missing. It must ask whether repeated activity is forming a rhythm or merely consuming attention. It must ask whether roles are differentiating functionally or ossifying socially. It must ask whether coordination is carried by the shared medium or by constant intervention. It must ask whether the aim is becoming more native or more dependent on its scaffolds.

The essential proposition of this paper is:

```text
Organization is not only who does what by when.
Organization is the condition field that activity creates, maintains,
withdraws, repairs, and integrates.
```

Everything else follows from this proposition.

If organization is a condition field, we must define the domain in which task-objective organization is sufficient and the domain in which it becomes incomplete.

If the aim is a condition regime, we must define organization as condition formation.

If activities matter by how they change conditions, tasks must be distinguished from cultivation moves.

If roles matter by the functions they stabilize, roles must be understood as basin functions.

If coordination is field-mediated, shared media must become organizational objects.

If explicit scaffolds produce desired signs, naturalization debt must be tracked.

If time itself shapes basins, rhythms must be organized as conditions.

If agency is a desired developmental capacity, organization must ask what kind of agency it cultivates.

And if organization can still distort the field, it needs guardrails and ledgers that preserve claim ceilings until measurement can take over.

Attunement changed sight. Aims changed direction. Organization now changes practice.

## 2. The Domain and Boundary of Task-Objective Organization

Task-objective organization is not the enemy of Reflexive Coherence Development. It is one of its possible local instruments.

A task is a bounded commitment to produce, change, deliver, decide, repair, or verify something under specified conditions. An objective is a coordinated commitment to achieve a defined result. A plan sequences tasks toward objectives. A role assigns responsibility for classes of tasks. A metric tracks whether the expected output, state, or threshold has been reached.

This structure is powerful because it reduces ambiguity. It allows work to be divided. It makes accountability possible. It protects finite attention. It prevents endless discussion. It gives a group a way to close loops. It is especially appropriate when the relevant state space is already formed.

Task-objective organization is strongest when:

```text
the output class is known,
the success criteria are stable,
the dependencies can be sequenced,
the relevant roles are already differentiated,
the environment is sufficiently predictable,
the metric tracks the intended function,
and the cost of reaching the objective does not materially alter the aim.
```

In such cases, developmental language may add little. The work can be organized as execution. The honest question is whether the agreed result was produced under the agreed constraints.

The boundary appears when the produced result is not the same as the developed condition.

A task can close while the condition remains open. An objective can be met while the underlying capacity remains externalized. A role can be assigned while the function is not yet naturalized. A metric can improve while the regime degrades. A plan can succeed while the shared medium becomes less able to carry future coordination.

The boundary can be stated as a distinction between **output closure** and **condition formation**.

Output closure asks:

```text
Was the specified output produced?
```

Condition formation asks:

```text
What changed in the system-observer field such that future expression,
recovery, variation, transfer, or naturalization is now different?
```

These questions are not opposed. They belong to different levels.

A task may be necessary for condition formation. A completed output can disclose a boundary. A local objective can make a probe possible. A schedule can protect rhythm. A deliverable can become an integration surface. A metric can expose a claim ceiling. The issue is promotion. A task becomes misleading when it is promoted from local instrument to developmental proof.

In predefined domains, this promotion may be harmless because the output is the intended object. In open developmental domains, it is dangerous because the output is often only a sign.

The sign may say:

```text
This expression is possible under these conditions.
```

It does not yet say:

```text
This expression is native.
```

Nor does it say:

```text
The regime that produced it is coherent, transferable, generative, or sustainable.
```

Organization must therefore preserve the difference between **task evidence** and **condition evidence**.

A completed task is evidence that an activity occurred or an output was produced. It may also be evidence that a boundary can be reached under support. It is not automatically evidence that the desired property has naturalized. That stronger claim requires withdrawal, variation, recurrence, transfer, and endogenous precondition generation.

This distinction changes how task-objective organization is used inside Reflexive Coherence Development. Tasks remain, but they are subordinated to condition inquiry. Objectives remain, but they are read as local handles. Plans remain, but they must contain withdrawal paths and integration surfaces. Roles remain, but they are tested as basin functions. Metrics remain, but they must state claim ceilings.

The question becomes:

```text
What is this task a task of?
```

If it is a task of output production, ordinary task organization may be enough.

If it is a task of condition formation, the task must be embedded in a larger organizational grammar.

That grammar begins by treating organization itself as a condition field.

## 3. Organization as Condition Formation

An organization is usually imagined as a structured arrangement of people, agents, roles, resources, processes, and decision rights. This remains useful. But for Reflexive Coherence Development, it is incomplete.

A developmental organization is an arrangement of conditions.

It shapes what can be noticed, what can be asked, what can be supported, what can be withdrawn, what can be integrated, what can become native, and what kinds of agency can persist under perturbation.

The organizational field can be written schematically as:

$$
\mathcal{O}_k =
(\mathcal{A}_k,\mathcal{S}_k,\mathcal{B}_k,\mathcal{R}_k,\Theta_k,\mathcal{E}_k,\mathcal{W}_k,\mathcal{I}_k)
$$

where:

```text
A_k = activity moves available at stage k
S_k = supports and scaffolds supplied by the organization
B_k = boundary conditions and selective permeabilities
R_k = role basins and responsibility functions
Theta_k = rhythms, cadences, and temporal architecture
E_k = shared media through which coordination and trace propagate
W_k = withdrawal paths and support-weakening possibilities
I_k = integration surfaces that turn episodes into usable history
```

This tuple is not meant as a complete formal model. It is a reminder that organization is more than a task list. It is the condition field in which activity occurs.

Using the update notation introduced in Attunement, one may say:

$$
M_{k+1} = \Phi(M_k,L_k;\mathcal{O}_k)
$$

where $M_k$ is the current developmental condition, $L_k$ is the loop trace, and $\mathcal{O}_k$ is the organizational field that conditions how the loop can be received, expressed, supported, withdrawn, and integrated. $\Phi$ is not a known deterministic transition rule. It names the irreducible developmental update through which a completed loop changes the condition of future loops.

The important point is not that organization controls $\Phi$. It does not. The important point is that organization participates in the conditions under which the update occurs.

A plan does not determine becoming. It conditions what kinds of becoming are more or less likely to be noticed, supported, repeated, withdrawn, integrated, or suppressed.

A role does not determine agency. It conditions how agency can be expressed, constrained, carried, or hidden.

A rhythm does not determine development. It conditions whether contact, consolidation, withdrawal, and recovery can form basins.

A shared medium does not determine coordination. It conditions whether signals, traces, pressures, reserves, and susceptibilities can propagate without constant explicit control.

This is why organization is not neutral. Even when it claims only to arrange tasks, it arranges conditions.

A daily standup may be organized as status reporting. It may also create a rhythm of mutual visibility, a pressure of performative progress, a channel for early boundary disclosure, or a ritual of shallow compliance. The same meeting type can condition different regimes depending on support, boundary, role, rhythm, medium, and integration.

A role table in an agentic ecology may assign functions explicitly. It may also prevent role basins from forming because differentiation remains producer-held. Or it may serve as a temporary scaffold that later weakens as shared-medium traces begin to carry role-specific affordances.

A school assignment may test performance. It may also reveal whether a student can generate representation before procedure. Or it may reward pattern imitation while concealing dependency on examples.

A therapy session may provide regulation. It may also cultivate the conditions under which recovery can begin before external rescue. Or it may strengthen dependency on the session as the only place where regulation is possible.

In condition-oriented organization, these differences matter.

Organization asks:

```text
What does this arrangement make easier to express?
What does it make harder to express?
What support does it supply?
What support does it hide?
What boundary does it disclose?
What boundary does it blur?
What rhythm does it form?
What agency does it cultivate?
What agency does it simulate?
What future loop begins differently because this arrangement existed?
```

The answer is rarely a single scalar. Condition formation is multi-axis. A useful organizational move may improve boundary clarity while increasing support dependence. A necessary scaffold may enable expression while increasing naturalization debt. A role may stabilize a function while narrowing transfer. A rhythm may deepen practice while excluding necessary disruption. A shared medium may carry coordination while accumulating unprocessed trace.

The purpose of organization is not to maximize every axis. It is to keep the developmental object visible.

Organization becomes condition formation when it stops asking only whether activity was completed and begins asking what kind of field the activity created.

## 4. From Tasks to Cultivation Moves

A task is an activity with an expected output.

A cultivation move is an activity whose primary significance is the condition change it attempts to disclose, support, weaken, stabilize, integrate, or naturalize.

The same concrete action can be either, depending on how it is organized.

A meeting can be a task:

```text
Hold the meeting and record the decisions.
```

The same meeting can be a cultivation move:

```text
Test whether the group can name cross-boundary tension without facilitator rescue,
and integrate what survives into the next coordination rhythm.
```

A model evaluation can be a task:

```text
Run the benchmark and report the score.
```

The same evaluation can be a cultivation move:

```text
Distinguish scaffolded task performance from native representation capacity
under support withdrawal and context variation.
```

A lesson can be a task:

```text
Teach chapter four.
```

The same lesson can be a cultivation move:

```text
Move one support from worked example to learner-generated representation,
then observe where the capacity collapses or stabilizes.
```

The distinction is not motivational. It is structural.

A task is evaluated primarily by output closure. A cultivation move is evaluated by what it reveals or alters about the condition field.

A cultivation move usually contains a local question:

```text
Which boundary is missing?
Which support is still external?
Which role function is not yet basin-like?
Which rhythm is forming or failing?
Which medium trace is carrying coordination?
Which claim ceiling is valid after withdrawal?
```

It also declares its supplied support, its intended withdrawal path, and its blocked stronger claim. This prevents the move from being promoted too quickly into proof.

A cultivation move can be described through a condition delta:

$$
\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}(a_i)
=
(\Delta S,\Delta B,\Delta N,\Delta R,\Delta E,\Delta A,\Delta I)
$$

where $a_i$ is an activity and the components indicate observed changes in support dependence, boundary condition, naturalization depth, role-basin formation, shared-medium condition, agency capacity, and integration. This is not a scalar score. It is a descriptive vector that preserves the multi-axis nature of development.

For example:

```text
Activity: reduce facilitator intervention during repair discussion
Delta S: external support weakened
Delta B: conflict boundary became more explicit
Delta N: repair language partially persisted
Delta R: one participant began taking repair-function role
Delta E: shared medium became temporarily tense but more readable
Delta A: group agency increased locally, not yet transferable
Delta I: integration occurred in next planning cycle
Claim ceiling: evidence of scaffold weakening, not yet native repair regime
```

This is the organizational unit of Reflexive Coherence Development.

Not every activity must be analyzed this way. Over-instrumentation can itself become a distortion. But when the aim is condition formation, at least some activities must be organized as cultivation moves. Otherwise the organization has no way to know whether it is developing conditions or merely completing tasks.

Cultivation moves include:

```text
disclosing a boundary,
testing a support,
weakening a scaffold,
stabilizing a rhythm,
creating an integration surface,
repairing a shared medium,
distributing a role function,
exposing naturalization debt,
protecting reserve,
allowing controlled bifurcation,
retiring a dead form,
redescribing an aim.
```

The move is local. The meaning is trajectory-level.

A single withdrawal may not prove naturalization, but it changes the support map. A single rhythm may not form a basin, but it makes temporal architecture visible. A single role transfer may not create distributed agency, but it reveals where the role remains person-bound, producer-bound, or medium-bound. A single failed probe may not be progress, but it may disclose the missing condition more honestly than a successful task.

The organization that can learn from such moves is different from the organization that can only count completed tasks.

Task completion closes activity. Cultivation moves open condition knowledge.

Both are needed. But in Reflexive Coherence Development, tasks are organized under the larger question of what they cultivate.

## 5. Roles as Basin Functions

In ordinary organization, a role is often treated as a label attached to a person, agent, team, or component. The role defines responsibility, authority, expected behavior, and accountability. This is useful. Without role labels, coordination can dissolve into ambiguity.

But a role label is not yet a role basin.

A role basin exists when a function becomes re-enterable, recognizable, transferable, and sustainable within the organizational field. It is not merely that someone is named facilitator, steward, reviewer, mediator, builder, teacher, maintainer, or agent. It is that the field contains a stable enough pattern for that function to be taken up, enacted, handed off, adapted, and reintegrated without constant external invention.

A role basin is a recurrent condition for function.

Schematically, a role basin may be described as:

$$
\rho_i = (f_i,B_i,S_i,\theta_i,\kappa_i,\chi_i)
$$

where:

```text
f_i = the function carried by the role
B_i = the boundary that distinguishes the role without isolating it
S_i = the supports on which the role depends
Theta_i = the rhythm or timing through which the role appears
kappa_i = coupling to other roles, media, and regimes
chi_i = claim ceiling for how native or transferable the role currently is
```

This expression is not meant to reduce roles to variables. It prevents the organization from mistaking a label for a condition.

A role can be assigned before it is basin-like.

A facilitator can be appointed, but repair still collapses without that person.

A maintainer can be named, but the codebase or practice may not contain the traces needed for maintenance to transfer.

A reviewer can be assigned, but review may remain a gatekeeping ritual rather than a boundary-disclosure function.

A teacher can lead a class, but learning may remain dependent on the teacher's immediate regulation of difficulty.

An artificial agent can be given a role prompt, but role-specific behavior may collapse when the prompt, scheduler, or producer role table is weakened.

In each case, the role exists as assignment. It does not yet exist as basin.

The question is not:

```text
Who has the role?
```

The developmental question is:

```text
What conditions allow this function to be carried, transferred, varied,
and integrated without constant external enforcement?
```

This matters because role labels can conceal hidden support. A group may appear well organized because one person carries several invisible roles: boundary keeper, translator, memory, emotional regulator, quality gate, scheduler, and repair surface. The chart shows distributed responsibility. The regime is actually held by a single overloaded basin. When that person withdraws, the organization discovers that the roles were not naturalized.

The same can happen in agentic systems. A designer may externalize role differentiation into prompts, rules, state tables, or scheduler logic. The agents appear differentiated, but the differentiation is producer-held. If the aim is only to produce a controlled behavior under known conditions, this may be fine. If the aim is native agentic ecology, the explicit role table is naturalization debt.

Role development therefore requires withdrawal and transfer.

A role basin becomes stronger when:

```text
the function survives variation in who or what carries it,
the boundary remains clear without rigid over-definition,
the support ecology becomes visible,
the role can couple to other roles without being absorbed by them,
the shared medium carries enough trace for the role to reappear,
and the organization can state what stronger role claims remain blocked.
```

A mature organization does not eliminate role labels. It uses them as local handles while cultivating the deeper basin conditions that make role functions real.

This also prevents two opposite distortions.

The first distortion is **role ossification**: the label becomes rigid, identity-bound, and insulated from the field. The role persists, but it stops responding to changing conditions.

The second distortion is **role diffusion**: everyone is responsible, so no basin forms. The function is named as shared, but no boundary, support, rhythm, or integration surface allows it to be carried.

A role basin sits between these extremes. It has enough boundary to be re-enterable and enough permeability to remain alive.

## 6. Shared Medium Organization

Coordination is often imagined as message passing.

One unit sends information. Another receives it. Roles define who speaks and who acts. Protocols define allowed exchanges. In predefined domains, this can be sufficient. If the messages are well typed, the roles are stable, and the environment is controlled, coordination can be organized as communication between discrete units.

Developmental organization requires a wider view.

Much coordination is carried by a shared medium rather than by explicit messages. The medium contains traces, gradients, reserves, pressures, affordances, timing, histories, norms, artifacts, expectations, and susceptibilities. It is not only what participants say to one another. It is what the field lets them feel, notice, remember, anticipate, and respond to.

A shared medium can be a codebase, a lab notebook, a workspace, a therapeutic setting, a social norm, a governance ritual, a sensor field, an ecological substrate, a communication platform, a memory structure, a repository, a schedule, a material environment, or an artificial agent's common substrate.

In a shared medium, relation is not only transmitted. It is deposited.

A trace changes what later participants can perceive. A reserve changes what risk can be tolerated. A timing pattern changes whether recovery is possible. A norm changes what can be said without rupture. A codebase structure changes what future changes are easy or hard. A platform's affordances change which interactions become natural. A shared artifact changes what the group can coordinate around without explicit instruction.

This is why message-passing organization is incomplete for Reflexive Coherence Development.

If the shared medium degrades, coordination becomes expensive even when individual agents remain competent. More explicit messages are needed. More control is required. More meetings are scheduled. More rules are added. More reminders are sent. More producer state accumulates. The organization may interpret this as a personnel, motivation, or compliance problem. Often it is a medium problem.

A shared medium has developmental properties:

```text
trace readability,
reserve,
repairability,
susceptibility,
selective permeability,
timing support,
role affordance,
boundary memory,
integration capacity,
and decay rate.
```

These are not decorative. They condition whether desired properties can become native.

A research group with a readable shared medium can let questions accumulate across contributors. A group without one must rely on memory holders.

A software team with a coherent codebase can distribute maintenance. A team without one must rely on specialists who know hidden paths.

A community with a repairable medium can metabolize conflict. A community without one must either suppress tension or repeatedly rupture.

An agentic ecology with field-carried traces can develop role differentiation through local affordance. One without such traces must rely on explicit role control from outside.

Organization therefore includes medium stewardship from the beginning. The shared medium is not only infrastructure. It is a condition of agency.

The organizational question becomes:

```text
What must the medium carry so that the desired function does not have to be
held entirely by explicit instruction, individual memory, external control,
or repeated intervention?
```

This question connects directly to naturalization. A capacity becomes more native when some of its preconditions move from external instruction into the field's own affordances, traces, boundaries, reserves, and rhythms. This does not mean everything should be hidden in the medium. Some explicitness is necessary. But explicitness should be understood as scaffold, interface, or declared support, not mistaken for native capacity.

A healthy shared medium does not eliminate agency. It makes agency less brittle. It allows participants to carry less hidden support. It gives future loops something coherent to inherit.

This is organization as field formation.

## 7. Producer State and Naturalization Debt

In engineered systems, especially agentic systems, organization often begins with producer state.

The designer supplies roles, rules, goals, memories, schedules, permissions, resources, costs, communication channels, evaluation thresholds, and control logic. This is not wrong. Producer state is often the only way to begin. It creates a scaffolded ecology in which desired behavior can appear, be observed, and be tested.

But producer state can hide the difference between expression and naturalization.

If a role appears only because the role table assigns it, the role is scaffolded.

If goal-directed behavior appears only because a prompt declares the goal, the agency is scaffolded.

If coordination appears only because an external scheduler sequences actions, the shared medium is not yet carrying coordination.

If memory appears only because a producer-managed store re-injects context, the ecology may not yet have read-back structure.

If restraint appears only because hard-coded constraints block action, the boundary may not yet be native.

Again, this is not a criticism of scaffolding. Scaffolding is how many developmental processes begin. The issue is claim discipline.

Naturalization debt is the gap between the function the aim wants to become native and the support currently supplied from outside the developing regime.

For a desired property $P^*$, one can define the naturalization-debt set as:

$$
D_N(P^*) =
\{\sigma \in S_{\mathrm{ext}} \mid P^* \text{ degrades, collapses, or changes rung when } \sigma \text{ is withdrawn}\}
$$

where $S_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is the set of external supports relative to the current aim. This definition is intentionally relative. A support is not debt in itself. It becomes naturalization debt only when the aim claims that the function should be native without that support.

This distinction matters.

A wheelchair is not naturalization debt for a mobility regime whose aim includes assistive support as part of the condition envelope. It becomes debt only if the system falsely claims unsupported walking capacity.

A facilitator is not debt for a practice whose aim is facilitated repair. The facilitator becomes debt if the aim is distributed repair capacity and repair collapses whenever the facilitator withdraws.

A prompt is not debt for a prompt-driven tool whose aim is supported performance. It becomes debt if the system claims native reasoning, role differentiation, or agency that actually collapses when the prompt is weakened.

Naturalization debt is not a moral flaw. It is a bookkeeping of support honesty.

When weights are meaningful, a rough debt magnitude can be written as:

$$
|D_N(P^*)|_\omega = \sum_{\sigma \in D_N(P^*)} \omega_\sigma
$$

where $\omega_\sigma$ represents the developmental significance of each external support. But this scalar should be used cautiously. The debt set is often more informative than the number because different supports have different kinds of developmental meaning. A small explicit scaffold may hold a critical boundary. A large visible support may be benign because it is part of the intended regime.

The organizational task is not always to eliminate naturalization debt. It is to know what debt exists, why it exists, whether it is temporary, whether it is declared, and whether the aim requires it to be reduced.

There are several possible relations to debt:

```text
accept it as part of the condition regime,
declare it as permanent support,
weaken it gradually,
translate it into shared-medium affordance,
translate it into role-basin structure,
translate it into rhythm,
translate it into boundary elasticity,
or redescribe the aim so the support is no longer falsely treated as external.
```

This is especially important in agentic ecology. The design question is not simply how to make agents behave. It is how much of the desired behavior must remain in producer state, and how much can become geometry, support, aftereffect, flux routing, role basin, shared-medium trace, or native persistence condition.

What remains explicit is not automatically bad. It is visible scaffolding.

What is hidden is dangerous.

A Reflexive Coherence Development organization therefore keeps a naturalization-debt ledger. It asks:

```text
What desired property are we claiming?
Which supports currently supply it?
Which supports are inside the intended condition envelope?
Which supports are external to the claim?
What happens when each support is weakened?
What can become shared-medium affordance, role basin, rhythm, or native boundary?
What stronger claim remains blocked?
```

This ledger protects organization from mistaking scaffolded expression for developmental arrival.

## 8. Organizational Rhythms

Time is not only a container for activity. In development, time is one of the conditions.

A rhythm is a recurring temporal structure that changes how activity is received, integrated, recovered from, and anticipated. A rhythm can form a basin around a practice. It can reduce friction. It can create safety. It can create pressure. It can protect consolidation. It can produce fatigue. It can invite exploration. It can enforce premature closure.

Schedules allocate time. Rhythms condition becoming.

This distinction matters because an organization can have many scheduled events and no developmental rhythm. Meetings may occur, but nothing integrates. Reviews may happen, but no boundary becomes clearer. Sprints may repeat, but the shared medium degrades. Practice may be frequent, but recovery is absent. Evaluation may be constant, but naturalization is never tested. Intervention may be available, but withdrawal never occurs.

A condition-oriented organization treats rhythm as part of the support ecology.

Important rhythms include:

```text
exploration rhythm: when the system is allowed to encounter novelty,
consolidation rhythm: when what appeared is integrated into usable history,
withdrawal rhythm: when support is weakened to test naturalization,
repair rhythm: when boundary damage or medium degradation is metabolized,
synthesis rhythm: when trajectory-level patterns are read,
rest rhythm: when reserve is restored,
transfer rhythm: when capacity is varied across contexts,
redescription rhythm: when aims are allowed to change shape.
```

These rhythms cannot all be maximized. Development is not constant acceleration. Too much exploration can fragment. Too much consolidation can rigidify. Too much withdrawal can become abandonment. Too much support can create dependency. Too much measurement can collapse practice into performance. Too much repair can become local repair loops. Too much openness can dissolve commitment. Too much closure can suppress emergence.

Rhythm is the art of spacing developmental contact.

A rhythm can be described by:

$$
\theta = (\Delta t, p, r, \varepsilon, \iota)
$$

where:

```text
Delta t = spacing between contacts,
p = phase or sequence relation to other rhythms,
r = recovery allowance,
epsilon = expected perturbation intensity,
iota = integration surface after the contact.
```

Again, this is not a full model. It is a reminder that cadence is not just frequency. A weekly meeting without recovery and integration may be less developmental than a monthly rhythm with deep read-back. A daily practice with careful support decay may naturalize capacity better than an intense burst of unsupported performance. A rapid agent loop may produce output while preventing basin formation if aftereffects are constantly reset.

Rhythms also reveal organizational stance.

A system that constantly probes but never consolidates may be driven by novelty or anxiety.

A system that constantly measures but never withdraws may be trapped in performance evidence.

A system that constantly supports but never weakens support may be protecting expression at the cost of native capacity.

A system that constantly withdraws without repair may confuse naturalization with abandonment.

A system that never allows aim redescription may have converted direction into ideology.

This is why rhythm belongs to organization rather than logistics. Time is one of the ways an organization says what kind of becoming it permits.

The phrase can be stated simply:

```text
Rhythm is how time becomes support.
```

When rhythm is organized well, later loops inherit more than memory. They inherit readiness.

## 9. Agency in Organization

Aims introduced agency as one of the central capacities development may cultivate. Organization determines whether that agency becomes scaffolded, native, proxy, wounded, extractive, or generative.

Agency is not treated here as mere choice. It is the capacity of a coherent pattern to persist, regenerate, transfer, and participate under perturbation. A person, team, practice, institution, agent, or ecology may show signs of agency. But the developmental question is always:

```text
What conditions allow this agency to persist, and what does its persistence cost?
```

Organization can cultivate agency.

It can provide supports that allow a capacity to appear safely. It can create roles through which functions become re-enterable. It can maintain shared media that let participants perceive the consequences of their action. It can protect rhythms of exploration, withdrawal, and repair. It can make claim ceilings explicit so agency does not have to perform maturity before it has naturalized.

Organization can also simulate agency.

A person may appear autonomous because invisible support carries the difficult parts. A team may appear self-managing because one member quietly absorbs coordination debt. An agent may appear decision-capable because producer state chooses the relevant options. A community may appear participatory because the process allows input while the actual boundary is elsewhere.

Organization can suppress agency.

It can over-specify roles until no one can respond to live conditions. It can measure signs so tightly that participants optimize performance rather than capacity. It can remove support before a basin forms. It can keep support so constant that no one learns to generate preconditions. It can degrade the shared medium until all agency becomes local survival.

Organization can extract agency.

An extractive organization persists by consuming the coherence of the people, agents, ecologies, or media that support it. It may deliver outputs. It may appear efficient. It may even show strong internal order. But its persistence weakens the surrounding field's capacity to develop.

Organization can cultivate generative agency.

Generative agency persists in a way that increases the agency-capacity of its environment. It does not merely survive perturbation. It leaves behind better supports, clearer boundaries, more readable media, deeper reserves, more transferable roles, and more honest claim ceilings.

This gives organization a diagnostic question:

```text
Does this arrangement increase or decrease the agency-capacity of the field
that must sustain it?
```

A condition-oriented organization tests agency through several handles:

```text
withdrawal resistance: what persists when support is weakened?
naturalization depth: what preconditions does the system generate itself?
transfer capacity: does agency survive context variation?
proxy collapse rate: does agency disappear when local signs are no longer rewarded?
generative effect: does the agency strengthen or weaken the surrounding field?
```

These handles will be treated more directly in Measures. Here they matter because organization creates the conditions that measurement later reads.

The organizational aim is not to maximize independence. Independence can be another snapshot fantasy. Many forms of agency are relational, assisted, ecological, or medium-dependent. The point is not to remove all support. The point is to distinguish declared support from hidden support, generative support from extractive support, and native participation from performed autonomy.

Agency becomes organizationally mature when it can say:

```text
These supports are part of my regime.
These supports are scaffolds I am still naturalizing.
These boundaries I can maintain.
These boundaries I still borrow.
These effects I have on the field around me.
These stronger claims remain blocked.
```

This is why agency cannot be left to individual intention or agent architecture alone. Agency is organized.

It is organized by supports, boundaries, roles, media, rhythms, withdrawals, and integrations.

## 10. Condition-Oriented Planning and Organizational Distortions

If organization is condition formation, planning must change. A plan is no longer only a sequence of tasks. It is a provisional arrangement of moves that test, support, weaken, integrate, and naturalize the conditions required by an aim.

A condition-oriented plan still contains tasks. It still names responsibilities. It still schedules activity. It still commits to outputs where outputs are needed. But it also records the developmental meaning of the work.

A lightweight planning ledger can be written as:

```text
CONDITION-ORIENTED ORGANIZATION LEDGER

AIM:
    What desired emergent property or condition regime is being cultivated?

CURRENT EXPRESSION:
    Where does the property appear now, and under what support?

CONDITION GAP:
    Which boundary, support, rhythm, role, medium, or integration surface is missing?

TASKS / OUTPUTS:
    What must be produced locally?

CULTIVATION MOVES:
    Which activities are meant to disclose, support, withdraw, repair, or naturalize conditions?

ROLES AS BASIN FUNCTIONS:
    Which functions must become re-enterable, transferable, and sustainable?

SUPPORTS AND SCAFFOLDS:
    Which supports are declared, hidden, temporary, permanent, or contested?

SHARED MEDIUM:
    What trace, reserve, affordance, memory, or timing must the field carry?

RHYTHM:
    What cadence supports exploration, consolidation, withdrawal, repair, and redescription?

WITHDRAWAL PATH:
    Which supports can be weakened safely, and what collapse would mean?

NATURALIZATION DEBT:
    Which parts of the desired property are still held externally?

AGENCY EFFECT:
    Does the arrangement cultivate, simulate, suppress, extract, or generate agency?

INTEGRATION SURFACE:
    Where does the episode become usable history?

CLAIM CEILING:
    What can be claimed after this move, and what stronger claims remain blocked?

REDESCRIPTION TRIGGER:
    What evidence would require the aim or organization to change shape?
```

This ledger is not meant to bureaucratize development. It is a support for attention. It keeps the organization from collapsing condition formation back into task completion.

For example, a research program might record:

```text
AIM: develop generative inquiry around a new phenomenon
CURRENT EXPRESSION: interesting observations appear when one senior researcher frames them
CONDITION GAP: classification vocabulary and shared trace are not yet distributed
TASKS: run three exploratory probes; collect observations
CULTIVATION MOVES: require each participant to classify at lowest valid rung before theory discussion
ROLES: rotate boundary-question role and integration role
SHARED MEDIUM: maintain observation ledger with demotions and blocked claims
RHYTHM: weekly probes, biweekly synthesis, monthly redescription review
WITHDRAWAL PATH: senior researcher reduces framing support after second cycle
NATURALIZATION DEBT: theoretical coherence still senior-held
AGENCY EFFECT: inquiry agency beginning to distribute, not yet transferable
CLAIM CEILING: evidence of shared observation practice, not yet generative research regime
```

An agentic ecology might record:

```text
AIM: native role differentiation in shared medium
CURRENT EXPRESSION: roles appear under explicit role table
CONDITION GAP: medium traces do not yet bias role-specific continuations
TASKS: implement shared trace substrate and local response affordances
CULTIVATION MOVES: weaken explicit role prompts while preserving trace gradients
ROLES: treat role functions as emergent basins, not fixed labels
SHARED MEDIUM: traces must carry local aftereffect and affordance history
RHYTHM: alternate exploration loops with stabilization loops
WITHDRAWAL PATH: reduce producer scheduling and observe role persistence
NATURALIZATION DEBT: role table, scheduler, prompt structure
AGENCY EFFECT: currently scaffolded; possible native differentiation under medium support
CLAIM CEILING: role-like behavior under scaffold, not yet agentic ecology
```

A community practice might record:

```text
AIM: repair capacity under disagreement
CURRENT EXPRESSION: repair occurs only when facilitator intervenes
CONDITION GAP: shared boundary for naming tension without rupture
TASKS: hold repair sessions; document recurring patterns
CULTIVATION MOVES: gradually transfer naming and integration functions to participants
ROLES: repair role, witness role, boundary role must become re-enterable
SHARED MEDIUM: common language for harm, misunderstanding, commitment, and return
RHYTHM: regular repair window plus post-conflict integration
WITHDRAWAL PATH: facilitator reduces rescue while preserving safety boundary
NATURALIZATION DEBT: facilitator-held regulation and memory
AGENCY EFFECT: possible generative agency if repair strengthens surrounding trust
CLAIM CEILING: scaffolded repair practice, not yet native repair regime
```

The ledger also helps name organizational distortions. These distortions are not moral failures. They are common ways organization loses contact with condition formation.

| Distortion | How it appears | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| **Task capture** | Completed tasks substitute for developed conditions | Ask what condition changed and what support remains external |
| **Output inflation** | More deliverables are produced while the regime degrades | Track shared-medium health and support load |
| **Over-scaffolding** | Expression is protected so strongly that native capacity cannot form | Define safe withdrawal paths |
| **Hidden support** | One person, prompt, rule, or infrastructure carries unacknowledged coherence | Maintain naturalization-debt ledger |
| **Role ossification** | Role labels become rigid identities rather than living functions | Test transfer, variation, and boundary permeability |
| **Role diffusion** | Everyone owns a function, so no basin forms | Give functions enough boundary to be re-enterable |
| **Shared-medium degradation** | Coordination requires more explicit control because the field no longer carries trace | Steward trace readability, repairability, reserve, and timing |
| **Rhythm compression** | Activity accelerates until integration and recovery disappear | Protect consolidation, rest, and synthesis rhythms |
| **Local repair loop** | Organization repeatedly fixes symptoms without changing conditions | Reclassify recurring repair as condition evidence |
| **Enforced coherence** | Order is maintained by suppressing difference or bifurcation | Preserve trajectory diversity and boundary honesty |
| **Abandonment as withdrawal** | Support is removed before basin formation | Distinguish support weakening from premature exposure |
| **Agency theater** | Participants or agents perform autonomy while scaffolds decide for them | Test withdrawal, transfer, and proxy collapse |
| **Extractive agency** | The organization persists by weakening the field around it | Track generative effect on surrounding agency-capacity |

The purpose of naming distortions is not to prevent all of them in advance. Some will appear because development is irreducible. The purpose is to keep organization responsive when they do appear.

A condition-oriented organization does not need to know the whole path. It needs to know what kind of evidence would show that its organization is no longer cultivating the conditions it claims to cultivate.

That is why the planning ledger ends with redescription.

When the field discloses that the current arrangement is producing the sign but not the function, strengthening output while degrading the medium, preserving stability while suppressing agency, or sustaining agency through extraction, organization must change shape.

This is not organizational failure. It is organizational learning under irreducibility.

## 11. Boundary and Transition to Measures

Organization of Reflexive Coherence Development depends on the two papers before it.

Attunement changed sight. It showed that development is not a sequence of isolated state transitions. Loops integrate into trajectories, trajectories into regimes, regimes into ecologies, and the observer is a scale-calibrated participant inside the field.

Aims changed direction. They showed that development remains oriented, but not toward fixed snapshots as its highest object. It moves toward condition regimes in which desired emergent properties can arise, stabilize, vary, recover, transfer, be revised, and become native.

This paper has asked what practice becomes once sight and direction have changed.

A **task** is a bounded commitment to produce or change something under specified conditions.

An **objective** is a coordinated commitment to achieve a defined result.

A **cultivation move** is an activity organized around condition change rather than only output closure.

An **organizational field** is the arrangement of activity, support, boundary, role, rhythm, shared medium, withdrawal, and integration through which loops are received and future loops are conditioned.

A **role basin** is a recurrent condition under which a function can be re-entered, transferred, varied, and sustained.

A **shared medium** is the field of trace, affordance, reserve, pressure, timing, susceptibility, and memory-like aftereffect through which coordination becomes more than message passing.

**Naturalization debt** is the set of supports currently external to the regime but still required for a property that the aim wants to become native.

**Rhythm** is how time becomes support.

**Agency in organization** is not merely individual choice or agent output. It is the persistence, regeneration, transfer, and generative participation of coherent patterns under perturbation, as conditioned by the organization's supports, boundaries, roles, media, and rhythms.

The shortest formulation is:

```text
A task produces an output.
A cultivation move changes a condition.
A role label assigns responsibility.
A role basin carries function.
A message transmits information.
A shared medium carries relation.
A schedule allocates time.
A rhythm forms support.
An organization does not merely coordinate work.
It conditions becoming.
```

This paper also reaches its boundary.

Organization can arrange conditions, but arrangement is not proof. A plan can name naturalization debt without reducing it. A role can be described as a basin function without being basin-like. A shared medium can be declared important while still degrading. A rhythm can be scheduled without becoming supportive. Agency can be intended while remaining simulated. A cultivation move can be performed while its claim ceiling is inflated.

Once organization changes practice, evidence must change too.

If measurement remains endpoint scoring, the organization will be pulled back into the predefined-world frame. Tasks will become proof. Outputs will become capacity. Metrics will become aims. Scaffolded expressions will be mistaken for native properties. Producer state will be hidden. Claim ceilings will rise faster than naturalization.

The next paper therefore asks:

```text
How do we measure development without mistaking signs for capacity?
```

The full movement so far is:

```text
Attunement changes sight.
Aims change direction.
Organization changes practice.
```

The next movement follows naturally:

```text
Measures must change evidence.
```
