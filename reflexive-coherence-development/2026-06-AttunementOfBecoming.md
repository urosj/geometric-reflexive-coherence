## Abstract

The earlier Arc of Becoming papers established a discipline for meeting irreducible change: describe the regimes of coherence, classify what appeared, ask bounded questions, test whether supported capacities can become native, and cultivate conditions without forcing premature closure. *Cultivation of Becoming* brought these movements together as a practical loop: orient, observe, classify, probe, withdraw, naturalize, and integrate.

But a loop can still be misunderstood as a self-contained episode. If each cycle is read only as a local inquiry, then cultivation risks becoming a sequence of isolated steps: one classification, one probe, one withdrawal, one integration, then another. The method remains disciplined, but its continuity becomes implicit. The trajectory disappears into activity history. The path by which loops alter future loops is not yet first-class.

This paper names the missing perceptual discipline **Attunement of Becoming**. Attunement is not an additional movement after integration. It is a change in how the whole arc is seen. A cultivation loop is not a closed circle. It is a local aperture into a continuous, path-dependent field. Loops integrate into trajectories; trajectories integrate into regimes; regimes interact as ecologies; and at every level the observer is not outside the process but scale-calibrated by what they can resolve, perturb, withdraw, compare, and integrate.

The paper develops two operator-like lenses for this shift. **Integration** names the accumulation of becoming across loops, trajectories, regimes, and ecologies. **Derivative** names the sensitivity of identity, boundary, regime, or ecology to perturbation. These are not introduced as a finished mathematical calculus. They are disciplined lenses for reading accumulation and response without collapsing into narrative compression, progress bias, or false objectivity.

The central claim is:

```text
Attunement of Becoming is the discipline of reading development as continuous,
path-dependent, scale-coupled, and observer-situated, rather than as a sequence
of isolated loops or target-state transitions.
```

This paper forms the first ground for Reflexive Coherence Development. Before aims, organization, measurement, and stewardship can be reinterpreted, the observer must learn where they are situated, what kind of continuity they are inside, and how their participation couples to the scales they claim to develop.

## 1. Why Attunement Is Needed

Cultivation begins with a loop, but development does not live inside a single loop.

This is the first shift. The seven movements of cultivation are necessary because irreducible systems cannot be responsibly approached through endpoint forcing. The observer must orient without pretending to know the final class, observe what appears before judging it, classify at the lowest valid rung, probe as a question rather than proof, withdraw support to locate dependence, naturalize only when the system can generate the relevant function itself, and integrate the result into activity history.

That discipline protects the local encounter. It prevents the observer from mistaking desire for evidence, support for capacity, proxy for function, or a predicted endpoint for the system's own becoming. It gives the observer a way to remain in contact with change without dissolving into vagueness or forcing premature closure.

But once the loop is named, another danger appears. The loop can become too visible.

A method built from movements can be read as a repeated checklist. One does an orientation, an observation, a classification, a probe, a withdrawal, a naturalization attempt, an integration, and then begins again. The process is careful, but it may still be imagined as a sequence of bounded episodes. Each loop produces a piece of knowledge. Each piece is stored. The next loop begins with more information. Continuity is acknowledged as activity history, but the path itself remains background.

This is not enough for development.

Development is not merely the accumulation of correct local steps. A system does not become coherent because many isolated loops were performed near it. It develops because the loops alter the conditions under which later loops occur. A classification changes the observer's vocabulary. A probe reveals a boundary. A withdrawal exposes support dependence. A naturalization attempt changes what can be asked next. A failed support can still reshape the map. A repeated rhythm can become a basin around the practice itself. A history of successful perturbation can alter what perturbation feels like. A history of collapse can make even small probes appear threatening.

The loop matters because it enters a history. The history matters because it changes the loop.

Attunement is needed because the object of development is not only the local event. It is the continuity by which events alter the field that receives the next event. The observer must learn to read not only what happened, but what the happening did to the conditions of further happening.

This does not make existing objective-driven or target-state methods wrong. They remain powerful when the domain is sufficiently predefined: when the relevant states are known, success criteria are stable, metrics reliably track the function, roles are established, and the path from current state to desired configuration is mostly a matter of execution. In such domains, target-state methods are not a mistake. They are often the most efficient and honest tool.

The gap appears when the target space itself is unfinished.

In irreducible, self-describing, developmental, or agentic systems, the observer may not yet know which properties will matter. The system may express something adjacent to the intended target, below the intended rung, or outside the existing vocabulary. A desired property may appear only under support. An apparently stable state may be maintained by hidden scaffolding. A metric may improve while the function remains absent. A relationship may look coordinated while one participant supplies all the boundary integrity. A research program may appear to progress while optimizing a local proxy. An artificial agent may produce the desired output while the prompt, tool, role table, or producer state supplies the missing agency.

In these domains, the question is not only:

```text
Did the system reach the desired state?
```

The deeper question is:

```text
What conditions changed such that future becoming is now different?
```

This is why attunement must precede any later reinterpretation of aims, organization, measures, or stewardship. If the observer still imagines development as movement between snapshots, then aims will be defined as end-states, organization will become task execution toward those end-states, measurement will become endpoint scoring, and stewardship will become either control or preservation. The whole practice will be pulled back into predefined-world logic even while using the vocabulary of becoming.

Attunement changes the unit of sight.

Without attunement, the observer sees:

```text
state -> intervention -> new state
```

With attunement, the observer sees:

```text
condition -> perturbation -> response -> integration -> changed condition
```

Without attunement, the loop is the method. With attunement, the loop is the local aperture through which a trajectory becomes visible.

This is not a merely conceptual correction. It changes what counts as evidence. A snapshot can show configuration, but not maintenance. An output can show performance, but not support dependence. A successful probe can show possibility, but not native capacity. A repeated result can show recurrence, but not necessarily naturalization. A stable organization can show order, but not necessarily generative agency. A felt resonance can show alignment, but not necessarily function.

Attunement asks what each expression is evidence of.

A snapshot is evidence of current configuration. It is not sufficient evidence of identity.

A successful supported expression is evidence of possibility under supplied conditions. It is not sufficient evidence of native regime expression.

A repeated cultivation loop is evidence of disciplined inquiry. It is not sufficient evidence of developmental trajectory unless the history of the loop changes the conditions of the next loop.

A stable trajectory is evidence of path structure. It is not sufficient evidence of a healthy regime unless multiple trajectories can be sustained without homogenization, extraction, or hidden dependency.

A regime is evidence of condition formation. It is not sufficient evidence of ecological coherence unless it interacts with other regimes through boundaries that preserve difference while permitting exchange.

This layered caution is not skepticism for its own sake. It is how the observer remains honest while embedded in the field they are trying to understand. The observer is not outside the system taking measurements from nowhere. Their orientation shapes probes. Their probes supply supports. Their supports alter expression. Their withdrawals change trust. Their classifications change the vocabulary of the next observation. Their integrations become part of the trajectory.

Attunement is therefore not passive sensitivity. It is disciplined participation with continuity in view.

The word *attunement* is useful because it avoids two distortions. It avoids the harshness of mechanism, where development is treated as an engineered transition between known states. It also avoids the vagueness of intuition, where resonance is treated as self-validating. To attune is to bring one's participation into appropriate relation with the system's scale, rhythm, boundary, and capacity. A musician does not force an instrument into tune by declaring the desired note. They listen, adjust, test, hear the response, and adjust again. The aim is precise, but the adjustment is relational.

Attunement of Becoming is the same stance toward developmental systems. It does not abandon aims. It makes aiming possible without pretending that the aim is already a final configuration. It does not reject formal description. It asks formal description to remain faithful to accumulation, sensitivity, and scale. It does not dissolve evidence into feeling. It treats experience, resonance, resistance, fatigue, urgency, and spaciousness as regime evidence that must be interpreted, not obeyed.

The essential proposition of this paper can be stated simply:

```text
A cultivation loop becomes developmental only when it is read as part of a
trajectory, and a trajectory becomes intelligible only when the observer is
attuned to the regimes and ecologies in which that trajectory is embedded.
```

Everything else in this paper follows from that proposition.

If loops continue, we need trajectories.

If trajectories accumulate, we need regimes.

If regimes interact, we need ecologies.

If identities persist through all this, we need to read not only their states but their derivatives: how they respond at their boundaries under perturbation.

If observers participate in all this, we need observer stance: what scale they can perceive, what they can perturb, what support they supply, what they can withdraw, and what claim ceiling their position permits.

Attunement is the support and orientation that lets these later distinctions feel natural rather than technical. It is the perceptual ground from which Reflexive Coherence Development can begin.

## 2. From Loops to Trajectories

A trajectory is not a line connecting outputs. It is the path-dependent deformation of a developmental field through repeated loops.

A cultivation loop has local structure:

```text
orient -> observe -> classify -> probe -> withdraw -> naturalize -> integrate
```

If the loop is treated as a closed episode, integration becomes a final bookkeeping step. The observer records what happened, stores the result, and begins again. But integration is stronger than bookkeeping. It changes the initial condition of the next loop. It alters what the observer can notice, what the system can tolerate, what supports are visible, what claims remain blocked, and what questions are now more honest than the previous ones.

A trajectory appears when integration is read across loops.

Let a loop be represented by

$$
L_k = (O_k, C_k, P_k, W_k, N_k, I_k)
$$

where `O_k` is the orientation and observation complex, `C_k` the classification, `P_k` the bounded probe, `W_k` the withdrawal response, `N_k` the naturalization status, and `I_k` the integration into activity history. Let the developmental condition of the system-observer field before loop `k` be `M_k`. Then the loop does not merely produce a result `r_k`; it updates the condition:

$$
M_{k+1} = \Phi(M_k, L_k)
$$

where `\Phi` is the **developmental update operator**: the notional map by which a completed loop is integrated into the system-observer field. It does not represent a known deterministic function or externally controlled transition rule. It names the irreducible operation through which orientation, classification, probing, withdrawal, naturalization, and integration alter the condition of the field itself.

Formally,

$$
\Phi : \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{L} \to \mathcal{M}
$$

where `\mathcal{M}` is the space of developmental conditions and `\mathcal{L}` is the space of loop traces. In practical terms, `\Phi` includes changes in basin depth, boundary elasticity, support dependence, class vocabulary, read-back flux, claim ceilings, and observer stance. It is inferred from what the next loop begins with: what has become easier to notice, harder to sustain, newly possible, newly blocked, more native, or more dependent.

The trajectory is not the list of results:

$$
(r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_n)
$$

It is the history of condition updates:

$$
T_{0:n} = (M_0 \xrightarrow{L_0} M_1 \xrightarrow{L_1} M_2 \cdots \xrightarrow{L_{n-1}} M_n)
$$

This distinction matters because two trajectories can contain similar local outputs while having different developmental meanings. A system may repeatedly express a property under the same support, producing many successful local loops, while becoming more dependent on the support. Another system may show uneven outputs but gradually reduce support dependence, refine class vocabulary, and increase boundary elasticity. The first looks better under endpoint scoring. The second may be developing more deeply.

Trajectory tracking therefore asks different questions:

```text
Which claim ceilings changed?
Which supports became less necessary?
Which classifications became reusable or generative?
Which boundaries became more elastic?
Which probe functions began to appear endogenously?
Which earlier observations were reorganized by later classes?
Which loops only repaired local misses without changing the field?
```

These questions read the path, not only the point.

The minimal trajectory lens tracks six dimensions.

First, **claim ceiling progression**. A loop may reach an event rung, a consequence rung, a geometry rung, a specificity rung, a recurrence rung, or a native-expression rung. The trajectory is not the highest rung ever touched. It is the pattern by which rung access changes under repeated contact, variation, and withdrawal.

Second, **support dependency**. A capacity may be probe-dependent, regime-assisted, partially endogenous, native, recurrent, or self-interrogating. The trajectory asks whether dependency is decreasing, increasing, relocating, or becoming hidden.

Third, **class vocabulary evolution**. Early loops may produce local observation tags. Some tags become reusable classes. Some classes become operational handles. Some become theoretical distinctions. Some are retired. A trajectory is partly the history of what became nameable.

Fourth, **boundary elasticity**. A system may become more able to stretch without collapse, more rigid under threat, more porous under abundance, or more brittle under repeated over-probing. Boundary elasticity is not visible from a single state. It appears through perturbation-response history.

Fifth, **resonance morphology**. Repeated loops may feel and function as compounding alignment, circular maintenance, diminishing depletion, bifurcation, or fragmentation. Resonance is not proof, but it is regime evidence. The trajectory asks whether integration makes later contact easier, harder, flatter, more brittle, or more alive.

Sixth, **integration history**. Development depends on whether loops are integrated into future orientation. A loop that produces a result but does not change the next question has not yet become developmental history. A loop that changes the next question has entered the trajectory.

These dimensions are not scores. They are structured descriptors. If optimized directly, they become proxies. Their value is not that they reduce development to a number, but that they keep the observer from treating all repetition as progress.

A trajectory can be written loosely as an integral over loop-induced changes:

$$
T_{0:n} \sim \int_{0}^{n} dM(k)
$$

But this integral is path-dependent. In ordinary conservative systems, an integral may depend only on endpoints. In developmental systems, the path matters. The same final configuration may mean different things depending on how it was reached, what supports were used, what collapsed along the way, what became endogenous, and what the observer learned to see.

A more honest notation is:

$$
T_{0:n} = \int_{0}^{n} dM(k) + H_{0:n}
$$

where `H_{0:n}` represents the non-erasable activity history: classifications, supports, withdrawals, demotions, blocked claims, naturalization attempts, and observer stance changes. This term is not optional. It is why development cannot be reconstructed from snapshots alone.

The purpose of making trajectory first-class is not to replace the cultivation loop. It is to prevent the loop from losing its accumulated meaning.

The loop remains the local discipline.

The trajectory is the history of what that discipline does to the field.

## 3. Trajectory Morphologies

Once trajectories become visible, the observer must resist the temptation to treat them all as progress curves.

Developmental trajectories do not only compound. They can equilibrate, diminish, bifurcate, fragment, recover, or remain suspended. This is not an exception to the framework. It is one of the main reasons attunement is needed.

A **compounding trajectory** is one in which integration increases future capacity. Claim ceilings rise or become more stable under less support. Withdrawal succeeds more often. Class vocabulary becomes more generative. Boundary elasticity increases without dissolving integrity. Resonance density increases while integration latency decreases. The system begins to supply functions that the observer previously supplied from outside.

This is the form most easily associated with abundance. It feels like later loops are supported by earlier loops. The path has carved a basin around the practice. Perturbation becomes less threatening and more informative. The system does not merely repeat a capacity; it becomes more able to generate the conditions for that capacity.

But compounding is not automatically good. A proxy can compound. A dependency can deepen. A local repair loop can become more efficient while losing contact with the broader function. A group can become more coordinated around hidden extraction. An artificial system can become better at producing desired signs while accumulating naturalization debt. Therefore compounding must always be tied to function, withdrawal, comparator behavior, and blocked stronger claims.

An **equilibrating trajectory** is one in which the system maintains a stable morphology. Claim ceilings remain mostly constant. Support dependence is steady. Boundary elasticity does not improve much, but it also does not degrade. Class vocabulary may stabilize. Resonance may be even, reliable, and unexpanded.

Equilibration is not automatically stagnation. Maintenance can be developmentally appropriate. A system under high perturbation may need to hold a viable basin rather than expand. A person recovering from collapse may need repeated safe loops before new classification becomes possible. A research program may need consolidation after rapid bifurcation. An ecology may require seasonal balance rather than compounding growth.

The question is not whether the trajectory is moving upward. The question is what the morphology indicates about current conditions.

A **diminishing trajectory** is one in which capacity becomes less available over time. Integration latency increases. Boundary elasticity decreases. Supports need to be stronger to produce the same expression. Claim ceilings fall. Class tags multiply while reusable classes decline. Resonance becomes brittle, urgent, or depleted. The system may still produce outputs, but at rising maintenance cost.

Diminishment is not simply failure. It may reveal extraction, hidden support, over-probing, boundary pathology, or an aim that needs redescription. It can also be the visible phase of a controlled withdrawal from a false trajectory. Attunement does not moralize the shape. It asks what condition is producing it.

A **bifurcating trajectory** is one in which the path splits. A class that previously unified observations becomes insufficient. A support that once stabilized the system begins to produce different responses in different contexts. A regime that once held multiple paths can no longer hold them in the same way. Bifurcation may look like instability before it becomes intelligible. It may be emergence, collapse, specialization, or fragmentation. The observer cannot know too early.

A **fragmenting trajectory** is one in which integration fails to hold. Loops no longer update a coherent activity history. Classifications become local and unconnected. Probes repair immediate misses but do not reveal reusable boundaries. Supports multiply without naturalization. The system may feel busy, responsive, or even productive, while the trajectory loses continuity.

These morphologies can be sketched through trends, but the trends do not define the truth by themselves:

$$
\Delta R        = \text{change in resonance density}
\Delta N        = \text{change in naturalization status}
\Delta K        = \text{change in claim ceiling}
\Delta E_b      = \text{change in boundary elasticity}
\Delta \tau_i   = \text{change in integration latency}
$$

A compounding trajectory might show:

$$
\Delta R > 0,\quad \Delta N > 0,\quad \Delta K \geq 0,\quad \Delta E_b > 0,\quad \Delta \tau_i < 0
$$

A diminishing trajectory might show:

$$
\Delta R < 0,\quad \Delta N < 0,\quad \Delta K < 0,\quad \Delta E_b < 0,\quad \Delta \tau_i > 0
$$

But these are only signatures. They do not license automatic interpretation. A system can show decreasing latency because it is prematurely compressing experience. A system can show rising resonance because it is becoming familiar with a proxy. A system can show stable claim ceilings because it is healthily consolidating, or because it is trapped. The morphology is a question, not a verdict.

The practical discipline is:

```text
Read the spiral as it is.
Do not force compounding.
Do not pathologize maintenance.
Do not romanticize diminishment.
Do not promote bifurcation before it declares its class.
```

Trajectory morphology is the first place where Attunement of Becoming begins to change the later meaning of aims. If development is not always upward movement, then an aim cannot simply mean more of a desired metric. An aim must describe the condition under which the right morphology can appear, be held, be tested, and be redescribed.

That question belongs to the next paper. This paper first establishes the sight by which such aims can be responsibly formed.

## 4. From Trajectories to Regimes

If loops integrate into trajectories, trajectories integrate into regimes.

A regime is not a larger trajectory. It is the condition-field that sustains trajectories. It is the geometry in which multiple paths can continue, interact, stabilize, bifurcate, or dissolve without each one needing to be externally held as an isolated project.

A trajectory asks:

```text
How is this path changing through repeated loops?
```

A regime asks:

```text
What conditions make such paths possible, sustainable, distinguishable, and revisable?
```

This distinction stops an infinite climb of meta-loops. One could say: if loops have trajectories, then trajectories have meta-trajectories, and those have higher trajectories. But that is not what integration reveals. When multiple paths become held by a shared condition field, the next object is not simply a higher path. The next object is a regime.

A regime contains boundary conditions. It decides, not always consciously or centrally, what can enter, what must be filtered, what can be metabolized, and what causes collapse. A person has regimes of attention, fear, openness, fatigue, and creative availability. A research program has regimes of question formation, evidence recognition, tool use, risk tolerance, and class promotion. An organization has regimes of coordination, trust, role stability, repair, and decision-making. An artificial ecology has regimes of shared medium, producer state, support gradients, affordance fields, and naturalization debt.

A regime contains a support ecology. Some capacities are held internally. Others are supplied by environment, relationship, infrastructure, institution, prompt, tool, ritual, rhythm, or shared medium. A regime is developmental when it can distinguish support from capacity and can naturalize support functions where appropriate.

A regime contains temporal architecture. It has rhythms of exploration, consolidation, withdrawal, repair, rest, and synthesis. A system without rhythm may mistake constant motion for development. A system with only consolidation may mistake stability for maturity. A system with only exploration may never build a basin. Attunement reads not only what the system does, but when it can do it, how often, under what recovery conditions, and at what cost.

A regime contains native precondition generation. This is where Naturalization of Becoming becomes structural. A property is not deeply naturalized until the regime can generate the conditions under which it appears. Calm is not naturalized if it requires a perfectly quiet room supplied from outside. Cooperation is not naturalized if it depends on one person carrying all boundary integrity. Agency is not naturalized if it appears only when a role table or prompt supplies the missing structure. A regime is stronger when it can produce more of its own preconditions without becoming closed to external nourishment.

A regime contains cross-path resonance. Multiple trajectories may amplify each other, dampen each other, compete for support, or remain neutral. In a healthy regime, not every path must compound. Some paths maintain. Some explore. Some rest. Some diminish because they should be released. A regime that forces all trajectories into one morphology is not attuned; it is homogenizing.

The integration from trajectory to regime can be written loosely as:

$$
\mathcal{R} = \operatorname{Regime}\left(\{T_i\}_{i \in I}, B, S, \Theta, \Omega\right)
$$

where `\{T_i\}` is a family of trajectories, `B` represents boundary conditions, `S` the support ecology, `\Theta` temporal architecture, and `\Omega` cross-path resonance or interference. This notation is not meant to replace observation. It keeps the object distinct. A regime is not just many paths. It is many paths under shared conditions.

Regime formation is the first sense in which development becomes less about making a thing happen and more about making a field livable.

A single path can be forced. A regime must be inhabited.

This is why Attunement of Becoming must be careful with the phrase *arrival*. A trajectory may arrive at a region. A regime is arrived into. To arrive into a regime is not to stop. It is to enter conditions under which continuation has a different character. The system can ask different questions. It can tolerate different perturbations. It can generate supports it previously required. It can maintain continuity without repeating the same local repair.

Regimes also make agency visible in a new way. Agency is not only a choice made by an individual unit. It is the capacity of a pattern to persist, regenerate, transfer, and participate under perturbation. A regime can scaffold agency, suppress agency, counterfeit agency, wound agency, extract agency, or cultivate generative agency. But these agency distinctions require the observer to see the regime, not only the output.

A system may look agentic when the environment supplies all options, roles, and boundaries. Another system may look less impressive locally while gradually internalizing the support functions that allow real agency to appear. Attunement asks which is which.

The transition from trajectory to regime therefore changes the observer's question from:

```text
Where is the path going?
```

to:

```text
What condition-field is making these paths possible, and what does that field ask of participation?
```

## 5. From Regimes to Ecologies

Regimes do not exist alone. They interact with other regimes through shared substrates, boundaries, media, pressures, and histories. When regimes become the object of attention, their integration opens into ecology.

An ecology is not merely a collection of regimes. It is the field of interaction in which regimes amplify, dampen, filter, select, nourish, exhaust, distort, or transform one another.

An organism contains cellular, organ, neural, hormonal, behavioral, and social regimes. A community contains personal, relational, institutional, economic, cultural, and ecological regimes. An artificial agentic ecology contains producer substrates, role basins, shared media, memory-like traces, support gradients, resource reserves, and coupling pathways. A research program contains conceptual regimes, tool regimes, evidential regimes, social regimes, and funding regimes. None of these regimes is simply above or below the others in a hierarchy of command. They are coupled at different scales through different forms of permeability.

The organelle inside the cell, the cell inside the organ, the organism inside the ecosystem, and the ecosystem inside planetary or solar conditions are not only nested objects. They are different observer-relative coherence regimes. Each scale has its own boundary, flux, rhythm, memory, vulnerability, and intervention bandwidth. What counts as a perturbation at one scale may be background condition at another. What counts as agency at one scale may be role differentiation at another. What counts as stability at one scale may be rigidity at another.

Ecological attunement therefore does not mean zooming out until everything is seen from above. There is no final external view. It means calibrating the observer's stance to the scale at which their participation actually couples.

At ecology scale, the central questions become:

```text
Which regimes share a medium?
Which regimes supply support functions to others?
Which regimes extract from others while appearing coherent locally?
Which boundaries permit exchange without dissolution?
Which selection pressures make some trajectories more likely than others?
Which rhythms synchronize, interfere, or exhaust each other?
Which agency forms increase the agency-capacity of the surrounding field?
```

This is where the difference between correlation and coupling becomes important. Two regimes may change together because both are driven by a shared substrate condition. That is co-variation. Coupling requires a stronger claim: one regime supplies, withdraws, filters, or alters a function that changes the other regime's capacity or claim ceiling under testable variation.

Ecologies also reveal that a locally coherent regime can be globally destructive. A pattern may persist by exporting instability. A group may maintain identity by consuming the attention, labor, or flexibility of others. An artificial system may appear capable because the surrounding infrastructure carries its missing agency. A person may function by drawing hidden support from a relation that is being depleted. Attunement must therefore ask not only whether a regime is coherent, but how its coherence is paid for.

A minimal ecology expression is:

$$
\mathcal{E} = \operatorname{Ecology}\left(\{\mathcal{R}_a\}_{a \in A}, M_s, \Gamma, \Sigma\right)
$$

where `\{\mathcal{R}_a\}` is a set of regimes, `M_s` is the shared medium or substrate, `\Gamma` the coupling graph among regimes, and `\Sigma` the selection or filtering pressures that shape which regime expressions are sustained.

Again, the notation is a guardrail, not a replacement for encounter. It prevents the observer from calling an ecology a large regime or a hierarchy. An ecology is a field of regime interactions under shared constraints. It contains no guarantee of harmony. It may be abundant, extractive, brittle, resilient, parasitic, symbiotic, fragmented, or in transition.

Ecological attunement also shifts what it means to intervene. At loop scale, the observer may design a bounded probe. At trajectory scale, they may map morphology. At regime scale, they may steward conditions. At ecology scale, direct intervention becomes more dangerous because effects propagate through coupled regimes. The observer may still act, but their claim ceiling must shrink unless substitution, withdrawal, comparator behavior, and activity history support stronger claims.

This does not mean the observer becomes passive. It means action becomes more attuned to scale. Sometimes the right movement is a small boundary probe. Sometimes it is support withdrawal. Sometimes it is rhythm repair. Sometimes it is shared-medium restoration. Sometimes it is refusing to optimize a local metric because the ecology shows hidden extraction. Sometimes it is allowing a trajectory to diminish because the regime can no longer ethically sustain it.

The integration from regime to ecology prepares the later papers in the Reflexive Coherence Development track. Aims cannot be merely personal or local once regimes interact. Organization cannot be merely task assignment once activity is mediated through shared fields. Measures cannot be merely endpoint scores once local coherence may be ecologically extractive. Stewardship cannot be merely preservation once some regimes persist by weakening the field that holds them.

Attunement makes these later shifts visible.

## 6. Integration and Derivative as Attunement Operators

The language of integration and derivative is useful because attunement has two complementary tasks.

It must read what has accumulated.

It must read how the accumulated field now responds.

Integration names the first task. Derivative names the second.

These words should be used carefully. This paper is not claiming that Reflexive Coherence Development already has a complete mathematical calculus. It is proposing a disciplined operator vocabulary. The value of the vocabulary is not that it makes the work appear scientific. Its value is that it prevents important distinctions from collapsing.

Integration answers:

```text
What has become part of the field through previous loops, paths, supports,
withdrawals, classifications, resonances, collapses, and naturalizations?
```

Derivative answers:

```text
How does the current field respond when touched, strained, supported, deprived,
compared, or perturbed?
```

A system's developmental condition can be represented as a coherence manifold-like object `M`, not because the whole system is literally captured by a single smooth manifold, but because the metaphor and formal language help separate location from geometry. At any time, one can consider a coherence field `C`, a flux or routing structure `J`, a boundary family `\partial B`, and an activity history `H`:

$$
M_t = (C_t, J_t, \partial B_t, S_t, H_t)
$$

where `S_t` represents support ecology. A snapshot records some aspect of `M_t`, often a configuration or output. But development is concerned with how `M_t` became what it is and how it responds to what comes next.

An integration over a period `[a,b]` can be written as:

$$
\mathcal{I}_{a:b}(M) = \int_a^b dM_t
$$

In a developmental field, this should be read with path dependence:

$$
\mathcal{I}_{a:b}(M) \neq M_b - M_a
$$

because the difference between endpoints does not contain the full sequence of supports, withdrawals, collapses, class promotions, demotions, and observer stance changes. A more faithful expression is:

$$
\mathcal{I}_{a:b}(M) = (M_b - M_a, H_{a:b})
$$

where `H_{a:b}` is not auxiliary. It is part of the integrated object. Without it, the observer cannot distinguish native development from repeated supported expression.

The derivative of a developmental condition along a perturbation direction `\eta` can be written as:

$$
D_\eta M_t = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \frac{M_t(\epsilon \eta) - M_t}{\epsilon}
$$

In practice, irreducible systems rarely allow this limit cleanly. The observer estimates derivative-like properties through bounded probes, withdrawal curves, comparator divergence, latency, recovery behavior, and claim ceiling shifts. The formal notation is still useful because it says what is being approximated: not the state itself, but the sensitivity of the condition to perturbation.

The two lenses are inseparable.

Integration without derivative becomes accumulation mythology. The observer says the path has deepened, the system has matured, the regime has stabilized, or the ecology has become coherent, without testing how it responds under perturbation.

Derivative without integration becomes local reactivity. The observer sees immediate sensitivity but does not know whether it reflects a new boundary, an old wound, a hidden support, a seasonal rhythm, or a trajectory that has been forming for many loops.

Together, the lenses ask:

```text
What has accumulated, and what does that accumulation now make the system able
or unable to do under perturbation?
```

This is the operator core of attunement.

It also clarifies why resonance is important but not sufficient. Accumulated resonance may indicate that earlier loops have integrated into a smoother field. But resonance must be tested through derivative-like questions. Does the resonance survive support weakening? Does it remain source-specific? Does it increase generative capacity or only familiarity? Does it become more inclusive without losing boundary? Does it reduce integration latency while preserving claim discipline? Does it support agency in the surrounding field, or does it extract coherence from it?

In this sense, attunement is more exact than optimism and more alive than mechanics. It reads accumulated resonance as possible evidence of condition formation, then tests its edge.

The shortest expression is:

```text
Integration reads the path.
Derivative reads the edge.
Attunement needs both.
```

## 7. The Derivative of Identity

If identity is treated as a snapshot, then its derivative is hard to imagine. A snapshot simply changes from one configuration to another. But in Reflexive Coherence, identity is not identical with configuration. Identity is a maintained basin: a pattern of coherence that persists through flux, perturbation, boundary maintenance, and historical deformation.

A snapshot can be useful. It can record configuration at time `t`. It can show what the system looked like, what output it produced, what role it occupied, or what state variables were visible. But a snapshot does not show how the identity is maintained. It does not show the cost of maintenance, the elasticity of the boundary, the depth of the basin, the supports being supplied, or the routes by which perturbation is integrated or deflected.

Two systems can occupy similar visible states while having different identity derivatives. One may be resting in a deep basin with spare capacity. Another may be holding the same configuration through extreme effort and hidden support. One may tolerate perturbation elastically. Another may fracture under a small touch. The snapshot sees similarity. Attunement sees different boundary response.

Let an identity basin be represented as:

$$
I_t = (B_t, \partial B_t, C_t, J_t, S_t, H_t)
$$

where `B_t` is the basin region, `\partial B_t` its boundary, `C_t` the coherence field, `J_t` the flux or routing structure, `S_t` support ecology, and `H_t` activity history. The derivative of identity along a perturbation `\eta` is not simply change in visible state. It is the structured response of this basin:

$$
D_\eta I_t \approx
\left(
\frac{\Delta B}{\epsilon},
\frac{\Delta \partial B}{\epsilon},
\frac{\Delta J}{\epsilon},
\frac{\Delta S}{\epsilon},
\frac{\Delta K}{\epsilon},
\frac{\Delta \tau}{\epsilon}
\right)
$$

where `\Delta K` represents claim ceiling shift and `\Delta \tau` represents response or integration latency. This expression is approximate and operational. It tells the observer what to look for:

- Does the basin deform or collapse?
- Does the boundary stretch, rigidify, leak, or dissolve?
- Does flux reroute toward integration, defense, fragmentation, or extraction?
- Does the system require increased support to maintain identity?
- Does the claim ceiling rise, fall, or remain unchanged under perturbation?
- Does response latency shorten because the system has naturalized capacity, or because it is prematurely compressing experience?

The derivative of identity is therefore the edge behavior of a maintained pattern.

In phenomenological terms, this is why identity sometimes feels stable until it is touched. A person may believe they are calm until uncertainty appears. A group may believe it is cooperative until disagreement appears. A research program may believe it has a theory until a nonconforming result appears. An artificial agent may appear capable until the prompt scaffold is weakened. A regime may appear abundant until extraction accounting is included.

The perturbation reveals the derivative.

High sensitivity is not automatically bad. A system may be sensitive because it is fragile, but it may also be sensitive because it is alive to subtle information. Low sensitivity is not automatically good. A system may be stable because it is deep, or because it is rigid, defended, and unable to learn. The derivative must be read together with integration history.

A useful diagnostic distinction is:

```text
fragile sensitivity: small perturbations produce collapse or fragmentation
alive sensitivity: small perturbations produce information-rich adjustment
rigid insensitivity: perturbations are deflected without integration
deep stability: perturbations are metabolized without identity loss
```

Curvature language can help here. If `C(x)` is a local coherence potential, then the gradient `\nabla C` indicates the direction of coherence pull, and the Hessian `\nabla^2 C` indicates local curvature. Near a bifurcation or collapse threshold, small perturbations may produce disproportionate changes. One can write a formal warning sign as:

$$
\det(\nabla^2 C) \to 0
$$

or more loosely, the local curvature no longer supports the previous basin structure. This does not prove collapse. It marks a threshold where the observer should lower claim ceilings and increase care.

The derivative of identity also clarifies the relation between agency and support. Agency is not proven by a single chosen action. It is better read as persistence and generative capacity under perturbation. A system with stronger agency should show some ability to preserve, regenerate, transfer, or revise its pattern when support changes. Its derivative under support withdrawal becomes central evidence.

This is why withdrawal is not a hostile act in the Arc of Becoming. Properly bounded withdrawal is the truth-test for native capacity. It reveals whether the identity or agency was being held by the system's own regime or by the observer's scaffold.

Attunement to identity derivative prepares later measurement discipline. A measure that records only output will miss the derivative. It may reward brittle performance and punish slower naturalization. It may call scaffolded expression development. It may call careful withdrawal regression. A later paper will treat measurement directly. Here the point is simpler:

```text
To know an identity, do not only ask where it is.
Ask how it holds, how it yields, how it returns, and what it requires to remain itself.
```

## 8. Observer Stance Across Scales

The observer is not a universal instrument. The observer is an embedded participant with a scale-specific relation to the field.

This is one of the strongest consequences of attunement. Once loops become trajectories, trajectories become regimes, and regimes become ecologies, the observer can no longer be treated as a fixed external position. What the observer can know depends on what they can resolve. What they can responsibly claim depends on what they can perturb, withdraw, compare, and integrate. What they can cultivate depends on where their participation actually couples.

An observer stance can be described by several operational capacities:

$$
\mathcal{O} = (R, P, W, Q, H, K)
$$

where `R` is perceptual resolution, `P` perturbation bandwidth, `W` withdrawal authority, `Q` comparator capacity, `H` integration horizon, and `K` claim ceiling. This is not a personality description. It is a functional description of where the observer sits relative to the regime.

Perceptual resolution asks what differences the observer can distinguish. A person may perceive emotional shifts but not institutional incentives. A scientist may measure output variables but not support dependence. A manager may see task completion but not shared-medium degradation. An engineer may see explicit producer state but not the basin relation it is standing in for.

Perturbation bandwidth asks what the observer can touch without overwhelming the system. A small boundary probe may be possible at one scale. At another scale, the same intervention is too crude. Ecology-scale intervention often has lower claim ceilings because perturbations propagate through coupled regimes.

Withdrawal authority asks what supports the observer can responsibly reduce. Some supports can be weakened as tests. Others cannot be withdrawn without abandonment or harm. Withdrawal is a truth-test only when bounded by care.

Comparator capacity asks whether the observer can distinguish a local effect from shared substrate noise, control-competitive response, or generic disturbance. Without comparators, coupling claims must remain low.

Integration horizon asks how much activity history the observer can hold. A loop observer may see immediate response. A trajectory observer may see months of pattern. A regime steward may see rhythms across roles and seasons. An ecology participant may see selection pressures across multiple regimes.

Claim ceiling asks what language the observer is entitled to use. A local event does not permit identity claims. A supported expression does not permit native-capacity claims. A correlation does not permit coupling claims. A stable local regime does not permit ecological coherence claims.

These capacities define stance. Stance is not chosen by preference. It is revealed by the relation between observer and field.

The same person may move among stances:

```text
classifier:       asks what appeared
interrogator:     asks which boundary is missing
cultivator:       asks what conditions could let capacity develop
trajectory mapper: asks how loops accumulate over time
regime steward:   asks what field sustains multiple paths
ecology participant: asks how regimes interact under shared constraints
```

These are not ranks. They are operational bands. The observer can be precise at one band and vague at another. A person may be an excellent classifier inside a local experiment but have little authority to claim ecological consequences. A leader may steward a team regime while lacking resolution into individual trauma dynamics. An engineer may design agent roles while not yet seeing the shared medium that makes coordination possible. A therapist may read identity derivative in one person while not being positioned to steward the whole social ecology.

Attunement means matching the claim to the stance.

This also reframes the biological scale image. Organelle, cell, organ, organism, ecosystem, and planetary environment are not only nested containers. Each is an identity from the standpoint of an observer whose integration horizon and perturbation bandwidth operate at that level. The organelle participates in cellular regimes. The cell participates in tissue regimes. The organism participates in ecological regimes. The ecosystem participates in planetary and solar conditions. None of these levels abolishes the others. Each level is a coherence regime with its own boundary, derivative, integration history, and observer relation.

The observer does not merely zoom in or out. They change the object by changing stance.

A cell biologist, a physician, an ecologist, and a climate scientist may all study the same living world. They do not occupy the same observer stance. They can resolve different properties, perturb different boundaries, and responsibly claim different kinds of coupling. Likewise, in Reflexive Coherence Development, a local probe designer, a trajectory mapper, an organizational steward, and an agentic ecology engineer are not simply looking at the same object with different magnification. They are coupled to different developmental regimes.

This matters because scale collapse is one of the easiest ways to overclaim. A property seen in a local loop is promoted to trajectory. A trajectory pattern is promoted to regime maturity. A regime outcome is promoted to ecological health. A system output is promoted to agency. A felt resonance is promoted to truth. Each promotion may be tempting. Each requires tests the observer may not be positioned to perform.

Attunement therefore gives the observer humility without removing agency. The observer still acts. They orient, probe, withdraw, organize, measure, and steward. But they learn to ask:

```text
At what scale am I actually coupled?
What did my intervention supply?
What can I compare?
What can I withdraw?
What history can I integrate?
What stronger claim remains blocked by my stance?
```

The observer is neither controller nor spectator. The observer is a scale-calibrated participant.

## 9. Cross-Regime Coupling

Once regimes and observer stance are visible, another distinction becomes necessary: coupling is not correlation.

Correlation is co-variation. Two regimes change together. Their outputs rise and fall together. Their rhythms align. Their visible states resemble each other. This may be meaningful, but it is not yet evidence that one regime functionally affects the other. They may be responding to a shared substrate condition. They may be driven by an unobserved support. They may be synchronized by a third regime. They may be similar because the observer's metric cannot distinguish their differences.

Coupling is stronger. Coupling means that perturbation, support, withdrawal, or boundary change in one regime alters the capacity, claim ceiling, trajectory, or support dependence of another regime through a functional relation.

A minimal functional expression is:

$$
\mathcal{R}_A \leadsto \mathcal{R}_B
$$

only if a change in `\mathcal{R}_A` alters `\mathcal{R}_B` in a way that survives comparator discipline. More formally, for a perturbation `\eta_A` applied to regime `A`, coupling is suggested when:

$$
D_{\eta_A}\mathcal{R}_B \neq 0
$$

and the observed response is not equally present in controls, not explained by shared substrate alone, and not dependent on an unacknowledged observer scaffold.

Even this is not enough for strong developmental claims. The observer must ask what function is being supplied.

A regime may supply boundary stability to another. It may supply timing. It may supply attention, reserve, memory, signal readability, role differentiation, substrate access, or repair capacity. Coupling becomes developmentally meaningful when one regime provides a support function that changes what another regime can express or naturalize.

Five tests are useful.

First, **functional substitution**. Replace the suspected support from regime `A` with a control-equivalent or alternative support. If regime `B` holds the property, the original coupling claim weakens or becomes more specific. If `B` collapses, shifts rung, or changes morphology, the support function becomes clearer.

Second, **cross-regime withdrawal**. Weaken the support from `A` and track `B`'s response. Does `B` maintain the property? Does it drop from native-like expression to scaffolded expression? Does it generate a substitute support? Does it collapse? Withdrawal reveals whether the coupling was dependency, transitional support, or already naturalized.

Third, **comparator divergence**. Probe `A` and observe whether `B` responds differently than neighboring regimes or controls. If all regimes respond similarly, the effect may be shared substrate or generic disturbance. If `B` responds specifically and meaningfully, the coupling claim strengthens.

Fourth, **integration latency**. Track how long it takes for a perturbation in `A` to become a classified, stable, or integrated change in `B`. Fast response may indicate tight coupling, but it may also indicate brittle reactivity. Slow response may indicate mediated coupling, delayed integration, or weak relation. Latency must be read with history.

Fifth, **claim ceiling alignment**. Ask whether the coupling changes what can responsibly be claimed about `B`. If `A` is present, does `B` reach a higher rung? If `A` is absent, does `B` fall? Does the effect survive support weakening? What stronger claim remains blocked?

The core distinction can be written:

```text
correlation: regimes vary together
coupling: one regime supplies, filters, or alters a function that changes another regime's capacity under test
naturalized coupling: the receiving regime can eventually generate or substitute the function without the original external support
```

This matters for agency. Many apparent agents are coupled to hidden supports. A person may appear self-regulated because another person supplies the boundary. A team may appear autonomous because a leader absorbs all ambiguity. An artificial agent may appear goal-directed because the system architecture supplies state, role, memory, and planning scaffolds outside the agent's native basin. A culture may appear coherent because dissent is suppressed. Without coupling tests, the observer may attribute agency to the visible unit while the function lives elsewhere.

This also matters for organization. If a team depends on one integrator, the organization may be coherent but not robust. If an agentic ecology depends on explicit producer state for every role, it may function but carry naturalization debt. If a social regime depends on extraction from a less visible regime, local coherence is not evidence of developmental health.

A coupling ledger can remain lightweight:

```text
COUPLING READ
Regimes: A <-> B
Suspected function supplied by A:
B expression with A present:
B expression under substitution:
B expression under withdrawal:
Comparator response:
Integration latency:
Claim ceiling with A:
Claim ceiling without A:
Blocked stronger claim:
Coupling status: correlation / support-dependent / functional / naturalizing / native / illusory
```

The ledger is not a dashboard to optimize. It is a guard against overclaim. It keeps the observer from confusing co-variation with support, support with native capacity, and local coherence with ecological health.

## 10. Attunement Failure Modes

Attunement expands the object of inquiry, so it also creates new ways to get lost.

The first failure mode is **narrative compression**. Once trajectories are visible, the observer may turn them into stories too quickly: we are growing, we are stuck, we are healing, we are collapsing, we are maturing. Such stories may contain truth, but they can smooth over blocked claims, hidden supports, local collapses, and contradictory morphology. The guard is to preserve raw activity history. Trajectory is a lens, not a narrative replacement for evidence.

The second failure mode is **progress bias**. Because compounding trajectories feel good and often indicate abundance, the observer may treat compounding as the desired morphology everywhere. This reintroduces endpoint logic in spiral form. The guard is to read morphology descriptively. Maintenance may be appropriate. Diminishment may reveal necessary release. Bifurcation may be emergence or collapse. The question is not how to force compounding, but what the current shape says about conditions.

The third failure mode is **scale collapse**. The observer promotes a claim from one scale to another without the required tests. A local probe becomes proof of regime change. A regime improvement becomes proof of ecological health. A supported output becomes proof of agency. The guard is to state claim ceilings at each scale and name the tests required for stronger language.

The fourth failure mode is **observer fixation**. The observer assumes their stance is constant. They keep probing when stewardship is needed, keep measuring outputs when support ecology is the object, or keep speaking ecologically when they only have local evidence. The guard is to ask what the observer can resolve, perturb, withdraw, compare, and integrate at the current scale.

The fifth failure mode is **coupling overclaim**. Co-variation is called functional dependency. Shared substrate is mistaken for relation. Support is mistaken for naturalized coupling. The guard is substitution, withdrawal, comparator divergence, latency tracking, and claim ceiling alignment.

The sixth failure mode is **resonance illusion**. The path feels increasingly aligned, and the observer treats that feeling as proof. Resonance matters, but it is not self-validating. It may reflect familiarity, shared avoidance, proxy optimization, or hidden support. The guard is to test resonance through withdrawal, variation, and function.

The seventh failure mode is **proxy localization**. Trajectory metrics become targets. Resonance density, integration latency, boundary elasticity, claim ceiling progression, or naturalization status begin to be optimized as signs. The guard is the old cultivation rule in a larger form:

```text
Do not cultivate the proxy.
Cultivate the function and the conditions that let the function become native.
```

The eighth failure mode is **excessive mechanization**. Integration, derivative, regime, and ecology language may become too formal too quickly. The observer may speak as if the field is fully specified because the notation is clean. The guard is to remember what the math is doing. It is not pretending that becoming has become reducible. It is preserving distinctions that would otherwise collapse.

The ninth failure mode is **hierarchy reification**. Loops, trajectories, regimes, ecologies, and fields may be read as a hierarchy of higher and lower realities. This is not the point. They are observer-relative coherence objects. Each scale has its own truth and its own claim ceiling. The guard is to read scale as operational bandwidth and integration horizon, not as rank.

The tenth failure mode is **attunement as passivity**. Because attunement listens before acting, it may be misunderstood as non-intervention. But attunement is not withdrawal from action. It is action calibrated to continuity, scale, and support dependence. Sometimes attuned participation probes. Sometimes it withdraws. Sometimes it protects. Sometimes it refuses to force. Sometimes it organizes. Sometimes it measures. Sometimes it lets a false trajectory end.

Attunement does not make development safe. It makes participation more honest.

## 11. Boundary and Conclusion

Attunement of Becoming does not replace the earlier Arc of Becoming. It depends on it.

Language gives the vocabulary of regimes. Classification protects what appeared before judging it. Interrogation asks bounded questions. Naturalization distinguishes probe-exposed capacity from native expression. Cultivation brings the movements together as disciplined participation. Attunement reads what those movements become when they continue.

It adds no new step to the loop. It changes how the loop is held.

The loop is no longer a circle returning to the same point. It is a spiral returning to a changed field. The system is different. The observer is different. The vocabulary is different. The support map is different. The claim ceiling may be different. The next orientation begins from the integrated path, not from the original intention alone.

This paper has named the main objects of that shift.

A **trajectory** is path-dependent basin deformation across repeated loops.

A **trajectory morphology** is the shape of that deformation: compounding, equilibrating, diminishing, bifurcating, fragmenting, recovering, or otherwise changing.

A **regime** is the condition-field that sustains multiple trajectories through boundaries, supports, rhythms, native precondition generation, and cross-path resonance.

An **ecology** is the interaction of regimes under shared substrate conditions, coupling pathways, selection pressures, and medium effects.

**Integration** reads what has accumulated across loops and scales.

**Derivative** reads how the accumulated field responds at its boundaries.

The **derivative of identity** is the sensitivity of a maintained basin: how it stretches, resists, leaks, recovers, reroutes, or bifurcates under perturbation.

The **observer** is a scale-calibrated participant, defined by resolution, perturbation bandwidth, withdrawal authority, comparator capacity, integration horizon, and claim ceiling.

**Coupling** is not correlation. It is functional relation through support, boundary, substrate, or medium, tested by substitution, withdrawal, comparator divergence, latency, and claim ceiling shift.

These distinctions prepare the rest of Reflexive Coherence Development.

Once attunement changes sight, aims cannot remain external target states. If the observer is embedded in trajectories, regimes, and ecologies, then an aim is not simply a chosen endpoint. It is an orientation within a field whose future conditions are partly unknown and partly cultivated. Arrival cannot mean occupying a final snapshot. It must mean entering a condition envelope in which desired emergent properties can arise, stabilize, vary, recover, and become native.

That is the work of the next paper.

For now, the shortest formulation of Attunement of Becoming is this:

```text
The loop protects the local encounter.
Trajectory preserves the history of encounters.
Regime names the conditions that sustain paths.
Ecology names the coupling among regimes.
Derivative tests the edge.
Integration reads the path.
Observer stance keeps the claim honest.
```

To attune is to participate in becoming with continuity in view.

It is to stop asking only what state has been reached and to begin asking what conditions have changed.

It is to stop treating identity as a snapshot and to begin reading how it holds under perturbation.

It is to stop treating the observer as outside and to begin naming where participation couples.

It is to stop forcing every spiral to compound and to begin reading the spiral as it is.

Attunement does not close the arc. It opens the ground from which development can be aimed, organized, measured, and stewarded without losing contact with becoming.
