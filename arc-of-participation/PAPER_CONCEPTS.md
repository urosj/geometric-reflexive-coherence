# Arc Of Participation Concepts

This file is a compressed concept map for the Arc of Participation. It is
meant for quick orientation: what each paper asks, which distinctions it
introduces, and where to begin for a particular question about function,
identity, participation, scale, experience, knowledge, meaning, or existence.

For a more detailed chapter-level digest, see
[PAPER_CONTENT.md](PAPER_CONTENT.md).

## Concept Flow

```text
context gives formation its function
  -> the field differentiates into identity
  -> identity participates from partial knowing
  -> participation changes context within and across scale
  -> changed context forms new possibilities of identity and experience
  -> retained experience becomes knowledge
  -> composed participation becomes meaning
  -> locally continuous participation is disclosed as existence
```

The five papers form one cumulative argument. The sequence is linear as a
reading path but reflexive as a theory:

> **Context → identity → participation → changed context → transformed or newly formed identity**

Each paper changes the object inherited by the next. Function becomes
relational; the bearer of function must then be formed; the formed identity
must continue without global foresight; its participation crosses contextual
horizons; and the full relation finally changes what it means for an identity
to exist.

## Paper Concept Notes

### The Contextual Geometry Of Function In Reflexive Coherence

**Core idea:** a local structure does not carry a complete ecological function
in isolation. Its role is mediated, and may be constituted, by the geometry,
flux, boundary, history, support, and scale through which it participates.

**Important concepts:**

- chronological, path-dependent, and semantic progression
- enacted path and retrospectively understood path
- geometric mediation of function
- contextual constitution of function
- geometry–flux regime
- regime coexistence and contextual dominance
- context-typed primitive
- literal copying and functional translation
- bounded functional correspondence
- preservation contract and context-imposition debt
- context-complete truth claims
- Atlas of contextual realizability

**Read this for:** the relational account of function, regimes, portability,
truth, and the first applicative form of the Atlas.

### The Ecological Formation Of Identity In Reflexive Coherence

**Core idea:** the participant cannot be assumed in advance. An identity forms
when the complete field differentiates into a locally continuous closure whose
boundary, susceptibility, support relation, history, and perspective are
ecologically constituted.

**Important concepts:**

- complete causal state
- formation context
- initial identity signature
- seed, nucleation, spark, stabilization, and admission
- spark as attractor reorganization
- candidate basin and admitted identity
- perspective as formed situatedness
- identity as compressed ecological history
- formation as first participation
- reinforcement, splitting, composition, dissolution, and lineage
- identity-relative trace
- immanent unfolding without teleology

**Read this for:** the transition from contextual function to the formation of
the identity that can experience and participate.

### The Ecology Of Participation In Reflexive Coherence

**Core idea:** a formed identity does not need advance semantic knowledge of
the complete trajectory in order to participate meaningfully. It contributes
from its present boundary and history, receives ecological response, and is
changed by the context it helps change.

**Important concepts:**

- determinacy without foresight
- partial knowing as formed position
- local and ecological participation
- immanent unfolding from within identity
- local semantic sufficiency
- fragment as situated contribution
- ecological answerability
- holding geometry
- future composability and release
- temporal vision
- shared-medium meaning
- principles and transformation primitives
- cultivation heuristic

**Read this for:** meaningful action under incomplete knowledge and the
ecological conditions that keep partial contributions coherent, revisable, and
composable.

### The Scale-Recursive Structure Of Participation In Reflexive Coherence

**Core idea:** context, identity, and participation recur at several horizons,
but repetition of the vocabulary does not prove exact scale invariance. The
paper separates descriptive recurrence from mapped preservation and genuine
causal coupling.

**Important concepts:**

- scale as a horizon of closure
- nested and overlapping contextual readings
- function relative to scale
- upward contribution and downward constitution
- higher-order formation context
- coordination versus higher-order identity
- scale recurrence
- scale covariance
- scale invariance
- inter-scale coupling
- scale-preservation contract
- multiscale attractor tube and FRC scale flux
- experience, knowledge, and meaning across scale

**Read this for:** disciplined cross-scale claims and the relation between
ordinary RC's nested closures and FRC's explicit scale coordinate.

### Existence As Participation In Reflexive Coherence

**Core idea:** an RC identity does not first exist as an independent object and
then optionally enter relation. Its basin, boundary, persistence, withdrawal,
and dissolution already change the field, so locally continuous existence is a
form of participation.

**Important concepts:**

- existence versus occupancy
- locally continuous involvement
- persistence, withdrawal, and dissolution as participation
- structural identity-relative experience
- phenomenal-consciousness claim boundary
- knowledge as experience retained in changed possibility
- operative, experiential, semantic, inherited, and shared knowledge
- meaning as composed participation
- reflexive continuation
- history-bearing participation
- life as a bounded RC interpretive category
- participatory ecology across scale

**Read this for:** the ontological synthesis of participation, experience,
knowledge, meaning, life, and the limits of those interpretations.
