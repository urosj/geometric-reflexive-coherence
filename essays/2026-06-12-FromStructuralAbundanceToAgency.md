# From Structural Abundance to Agency

## Interpreting RC Through the Arc of Becoming

**Subtitle:** How a dynamical claim about basin proliferation opened the question of agency as persistence  

**Copyright © 2026 Uroš Jovanovič, CC BY-SA 4.0.**

---

## Abstract

Reflexive Coherence already contains abundance as a structural claim. A
coherence-conserving field whose geometry is shaped by its own coherence does
not naturally terminate in flatness or final uniformity. Gradients can become
directions, directions can become flux paths, flux paths can deepen into
identity basins, and basins under tension can collapse, split, or spark new
forms. In this register, abundance means differentiated identity geometry:
basin proliferation under conserved coherence.

But structural abundance is not yet the meaning of abundance for an embedded
observer. From within the system, abundance is encountered as novelty,
uncertainty, surprise, surplus capacity, instability, cost, and the pressure of
possibilities that cannot yet be classified. The Arc of Becoming gives this
inside view a discipline. It prevents novelty from being promoted too quickly,
support from being mistaken for native capacity, and proliferation from being
confused with development.

This paper does not add a new theorem to RC and does not yet give a theory of
agency. It follows the interpretive movement from structural abundance to the
question abundance leaves behind. If coherence continually differentiates,
collapses, redistributes, and renews, then the deeper question becomes: what can
hold through that process? Abundance opens the question of agency, but does not
answer it.

---

## 1. Abundance Has Two Registers

Abundance first appears in Reflexive Coherence as a structural property. The
field is conserved, but its forms are not fixed. Coherence can move, gather,
differentiate, and return. The geometry that guides this motion is not external
to the field; it is induced by the field's own coherence. This means that a
local difference is never merely local. A deviation in coherence can change the
curvature through which later coherence will pass.

That is the formal beginning of abundance. Not plenty. Not optimism. Not a
guarantee of flourishing. Abundance begins as the refusal of the field to reduce
itself to inert sameness.

At the same time, no embedded observer encounters abundance first in that
language. The observer does not live as an integral over the field. It lives
inside partial access. It encounters a new continuation before it knows whether
that continuation is a basin, a spark, a distraction, or a collapse beginning.
It feels surplus before it can classify the structure of surplus. It feels
choice before it can describe the collapse that will select a basin.

This gives abundance two registers.

In the structural register, abundance is basin proliferation under conserved
coherence. It is the tendency of a non-uniform, self-referential coherence
system to produce differentiated identity geometry.

In the interpretive register, abundance is what that structural tendency feels
like and requires from inside becoming. It is novelty that has not yet found a
class, possibility that has not yet become commitment, and surplus capacity that
may become growth, leakage, proxy, or renewal depending on how the system meets
it.

Interpretive here means both phenomenological and epistemic. Abundance is felt
as an inside condition before it is understood, but the feeling is not yet an
interpretation. The system still has to ask what the feeling is evidence of.

The two registers should not be collapsed. If abundance is treated only
structurally, it misses the embedded problem of interpretation. If it is treated
only experientially, it becomes inflated. Every opening begins to look like
wisdom. Every expansion begins to look like development. Every increase begins
to look like life. RC does not support that inflation. It gives abundance a
mechanism. The Arc of Becoming gives abundance a discipline.

This paper moves between those two registers. It asks how a dynamical claim
about basin proliferation becomes, from inside the Arc, a question about what
can persist through becoming.

## 2. How Coherence Becomes More Than Uniformity

The simplest picture of conserved coherence would be misleading. If conservation
only meant preservation of the same distribution, there would be no abundance.
The field would keep what it already had. But RC conservation is not the
freezing of form. It is a constraint on total coherence while the field
continues to redistribute through its own induced geometry.

The loop is circular:

```text
C -> K[C] -> g[K] -> J_C -> update C
```

The coherence field contributes to a tensor. The tensor shapes the geometry.
The geometry guides flux. Flux updates the coherence field. The updated field
then rewrites the next geometry. The loop is self-referential because the field
is transported by a geometry it helps produce.

This changes the status of a gradient. In a fixed geometry, a local gradient
might be treated as a difference to be smoothed out. In RC, the gradient also
participates in shaping the geometry that governs smoothing, concentration, and
future return. A difference can therefore become a path. A path can become a
recurrent flow. Recurrent flow can deepen the region toward which it returns.
That region can become an identity basin.

Identity, in this frame, is not a thing hidden behind the dynamics. It is a
stable mode of return inside the dynamics. A basin persists because coherence
can re-enter it, reinforce it, and find its own previous history in the
geometry that now guides later flow.

This is already more than uniformity. The system has acquired local history.
Certain regions matter because previous coherence has made them matter. The
field is no longer only distributed; it is organized around return.

But the same process that forms basins prevents a basin from becoming final. A
basin that expands too far begins to lose the internal differentiation that made
identity meaningful. It carries coherence, but also tension. It stabilizes, but
also compresses. If it cannot articulate its own internal pressures, it
approaches degeneracy. At that point, the geometry must change. A collapse can
select one continuation from several compatible basins. A spark can introduce a
new attractor. A previous unity can split.

This is the core of structural abundance. Coherence is conserved, but the
structures that carry coherence are not eternal. They can deepen, dissolve,
split, and reappear in altered form. Abundance is not an infinite supply added
from outside. It is conserved coherence forced through self-referential
geometry until form can no longer remain flat, monolithic, or complete.

That is why structural abundance is narrower than many of the meanings the word
abundance invites. It does not say that everything improves. It does not say
that every novelty is valuable. It does not say that every system becomes more
ethical or more alive. It says that under the right RC conditions, non-uniform
coherence tends toward differentiated basin geometry rather than final
uniformity.

The rest of the paper asks what this means from inside.

## 3. The Inside of Structural Abundance

An embedded observer cannot occupy the full outside view. It cannot see all
gradients, all coupling, all future curvature, all possible basins, and the
eventual collapse outcome. It lives inside the completeness asymmetry of RC:
globally determined, locally unpredictable.

This local unpredictability is not a defect added later. It is the condition
under which abundance appears as experience. If the local system could already
contain the whole future, nothing genuinely new would appear to it. It would
execute a known continuation. Novelty begins where the system generates more
than its current local description can access.

That is why abundance can feel like surprise. Something appears that the system
did not know how to expect. It can feel like possibility, because multiple
basins may be locally accessible before collapse. It can feel like creativity,
because a spark becomes articulable as a new form. It can feel like anxiety,
because the same surplus that opens possibility also exceeds control. It can
feel like spaciousness, because the system has more coherence than the current
demand requires. It can feel like distraction, because not every opening has
the structure of a basin.

The phenomenology is therefore not simple. There is no one feeling of
abundance. There are regimes in which structural abundance becomes livable,
usable, overwhelming, seductive, or false. The same gap between generation and
access can appear as wonder or threat depending on the basin structure of the
system that encounters it.

This is why experience must be read, not obeyed. A feeling of expansion is not
proof that development is occurring. A feeling of pressure is not proof that the
system should close. A feeling of novelty is not proof that a new identity mode
has stabilized. Experience is evidence of regime. It tells the system that some
relation to perturbation, surplus, collapse, or spark is active. It does not by
itself say what should be trusted.

The inside of abundance is therefore a methodological problem. A system that
generates more than it can represent must learn how to receive that excess
without either suppressing it or worshiping it. This is where the Arc becomes
necessary. It gives the observer a way to stay with abundance long enough to
find out what kind of thing has appeared.

## 4. Why Abundance Cannot Stay Open

The most tempting misunderstanding is that abundance means remaining open. If
abundance is many basins, many possibilities, and surplus capacity, then it can
seem that the most abundant system is the one that refuses closure.

But permanent openness is not abundance. It is unresolved plurality.

A system with many possible continuations has not yet written a continuation
into its geometry. It may be rich in potential, but it has not learned. Learning
requires that something collapse, that one continuation become actual enough to
change the field. Without collapse, abundance remains suspended. It produces no
history.

Collapse is therefore not the enemy of abundance. It is how abundance becomes
consequential. When a multi-basin configuration resolves, some possibilities
lose their current structure and one continuation becomes stable enough to be
inhabited. The field narrows. But the narrowing is not merely loss. It is the
formation of a path that can now be reinforced, questioned, strained, and later
transformed.

Stability is the next misunderstood phase. If openness is treated as the whole
of abundance, stability looks like scarcity. But stability is the condition
under which selected coherence can persist long enough to matter. A stable
basin holds what collapse selected. It gives the system a region of return. It
lets identity accumulate history.

Yet stability also produces its own limit. Every stable form excludes other
forms. Every basin that holds also compresses. Over time, the basin carries
unresolved tensions from what it has selected, ignored, simplified, or
absorbed. If the system is alive to its own pressures, this tension can become
the condition for spark. If it is not, the same tension can become rigidity or
breakage.

The cycle is not a circle returning to the same place. Openness becomes
collapse. Collapse becomes stability. Stability gathers tension. Tension
reaches spark. Spark renews differentiation. But each passage changes the
geometry. The system does not simply regain its previous abundance. It becomes
capable of different abundance because it now carries the history of what it
selected and what it could not contain.

This is why abundance is not a state. It is a regime of becoming in which
openness, collapse, stability, tension, and spark depend on one another. A
system that refuses collapse avoids commitment. A system that refuses stability
cannot carry history. A system that refuses tension cannot renew. A system that
refuses spark hardens until renewal arrives as breakage.

Abundance is not the permanent presence of many options. It is the renewal of
differentiable possibility through the whole cycle.

## 5. The Arc's Discipline

The Arc of Becoming is not needed because structural abundance is vague. It is
needed because the embedded observer is vulnerable to misreading abundance at
each phase of the cycle.

Each movement of the Arc protects abundance from a specific distortion. Without
that protection, abundance easily turns into confirmation, inflation, proxy, or
dependence while still appearing generative from the inside.

The observer begins with orientation. It arrives with a desire, question,
hypothesis, pressure, or longing. This is not a mistake. Without orientation
there is no region of inquiry. But orientation becomes dangerous when it treats
its desired outcome as the class the system must express. Then abundance is no
longer allowed to teach. It is made to confirm the observer's first frame. The
system is called abundant only when it produces what the observer already hoped
would count.

Observation interrupts that premature closure. It asks the observer to let the
system disclose what actually appeared before calling it success, failure,
proof, threat, or breakthrough. This matters because abundance is especially
easy to overread. Newness feels important. Surplus feels like confirmation. A
fresh opening can be mistaken for a stable basin before it has shown any
capacity to hold. Observation protects against treating the first felt expansion
as the truth of the event.

Classification gives the appearance a responsible name. This is one of the
Arc's most important protections. A local event may be real without being
reusable. A felt expansion may be meaningful without implying a new capacity. A
spark may expose a boundary without proving that a new identity mode has
formed. Classification is not promotion. It is the discipline of keeping the
claim at the level the event can actually bear. It prevents every local
increase from becoming a general class.

Probing asks what made the appearance possible. If a capacity appears only
under special support, the probe asks what the support supplied. Did it create
the effect, reveal a near-capacity, stabilize a boundary, or merely improve a
visible proxy? The probe is not a demand that abundance prove itself. It is a
small question about the boundary between appearance and structure. It prevents
the system from treating emergence as self-explanatory just because it arrived
with force.

Withdrawal is where the interpretation becomes honest. If the support is
weakened and the property disappears, then the property may still matter, but it
was not native in the stronger sense. It belonged to a supported configuration.
If it persists, the claim changes. Withdrawal separates what the system can
display under held conditions from what it can generate through its own regime.
It prevents a held-open system from mistaking being held for being capable.

Naturalization asks what would let the system supply the needed function for
itself. This is different from hiding the scaffold inside the system. It is also
different from optimizing the nearest measurable proxy until the result looks
stable. Naturalization asks whether the generator of the capacity can become
endogenous. It prevents the system from cultivating the appearance of surplus
while failing to generate the function that surplus was supposed to reveal.

Integration prevents each opening from being only an episode. It asks what was
learned, what held, what dissolved, what support was necessary, and what next
question is now better than the one that began the arc. Integration turns
abundance from event into history. It prevents each spark from being consumed
as novelty and then forgotten before it can alter the next orientation.

The Arc's discipline is therefore not anti-abundance. It is what lets abundance
remain in contact with truth. It lets novelty matter without becoming proof. It
lets support matter without becoming hidden dependence. It lets openness matter
without becoming proxy. It lets the system ask, again and again, not only what
appeared, but what kind of appearance it was.

## 6. Coupling and Cost

The question after the Arc's discipline is not only whether abundance appeared.
It is whether the system that appears abundant can actually carry what appeared.
Coupling and cost matter because abundance is never carried by possibility in
the abstract. It is carried by relations, substrates, supports, and flows.

Abundance requires coupling because new basins are not formed by isolated
entities sealed from influence. Coupling means mutual dependence of dynamics:
one region, system, or basin changes the future of another, and is changed in
return. In RC, coupling appears through flux, curvature, exchange, and shared
geometry. In lived systems, it appears as relation, friction, coordination,
dependence, imitation, rhythm, and exposure.

Coupling is generative because it lets the joint system produce trajectories
that neither participant could have produced alone. An isolated basin can
deepen its existing form, but relation can change the geometry in which new
forms become possible. This is why abundance is not only internal surplus. It
is also the capacity to be altered by contact without immediately collapsing
into defense or control.

But coupling is not innocent. The same relation that makes novelty possible
also redistributes cost. If coherence is conserved or balanced across the
relevant whole, then the deepening of one basin can involve the thinning,
dissolution, or reallocation of another. Collapse does not destroy coherence,
but it can dissolve the structure by which a previous identity remained itself.
From the standpoint of the dissolved structure, redistribution is not a gentle
abstraction. It is loss of form.

This is where abundance first becomes ethically charged. The question is not
only whether more forms appear. It is where coherence goes, which basins become
more stable, which structures are asked to dissolve, and which participants
have enough coupling to remain part of the cycle at all. A system can look
abundant because it is highly active while its costs are being carried by
substrates that do not get to appear as beneficiaries of that abundance.

Optimization matters here because it often answers the wrong carrying question.
It asks which channels contribute to the present target, not which channels may
be needed for the next real distinction to form. In a system with known inputs,
known outputs, and a stable target, that may be enough. In an irreducible
self-referential system, the observer often does not yet know which interaction
will become meaningful. A channel that looks redundant from the present target
may be the path through which the next basin appears.

This does not make inefficiency sacred. It means that optimization must be
answerable to the kind of system it touches. If the future property-space is not
yet known, eliminating coupling can make the present proxy cleaner while making
the system less able to carry the future it has not yet learned to name.

There is also a subtler failure. A system can learn to perform abundance. It
can produce more variation, more activity, more novelty, more expressive range,
and more visible openness, while failing to deepen any native capacity. If the
appearance disappears under withdrawal, if no function becomes endogenous, or
if the system depends on hidden support to keep looking open, then abundance
has become proxy.

The cost question and the proxy question converge. Both ask whether what
appeared can actually be carried by the system that appears to carry it. Does
the abundance naturalize, or is it being held by an unacknowledged support? Does
the system generate surplus, or does it redistribute cost into regions that the
interpretation has not counted? Does the relation deepen the field, or does it
make one basin look abundant by thinning another?

These questions do not negate abundance. They make it interpretable.

## 7. The Question Abundance Leaves Behind

By this point, abundance has done real work. It has shown that RC does not
describe identity as fixed substance. Identity forms as basin structure inside
a conserving, self-referential field. It has shown that novelty is not merely
noise, collapse is not merely failure, stability is not merely scarcity, and
spark is not merely disruption. It has shown why coupling matters and why
proliferation has costs.

But abundance also reaches a limit.

It can explain why more forms appear. It can explain why a system differentiates
rather than settling into final uniformity. It can explain why an embedded
observer experiences surplus, uncertainty, and possibility. It can explain why
the cycle must include both openness and collapse.

It does not yet explain what persists through the cycle.

If coherence continually differentiates, collapses, stabilizes, accumulates
tension, sparks, and renews, then identity cannot be understood only as the
presence of many basins. Nor can it be understood only as the basin selected in
one collapse. Something must carry continuity through reconfiguration. At the
RC level, an identity basin gives one answer: coherence can return to a stable
mode and reinforce it. But the Arc raises the question developmentally. What
survives orientation, observation, classification, probing, withdrawal,
naturalization, and integration?

This is where the abundance interpretation opens onto agency without yet
defining it.

The first question was structural:

```text
How does a coherence-conserving system produce differentiated identity geometry?
```

The Arc changes the question:

```text
What can persist through differentiation, collapse,
redistribution, withdrawal, and renewal?
```

This is not yet a theory of agency. It is the threshold at which agency becomes
the right next question. If collapse is only one phase of the cycle, then agency
cannot be reduced to the moment of choice. If abundance continually renews the
field, then agency must have something to do with what can hold through renewal
without being identical to any one temporary form.

The earlier Arc questions now return in a changed form. They are no longer only
questions about whether an abundance event was supported or native. They become
questions about continuity across reconfiguration:

```text
What remains recognizable after collapse has rewritten the field?
What support function becomes endogenous rather than merely internalized?
What structure can cross substrate without carrying all original conditions?
What regenerates when the initiating scaffold is gone?
What carries identity through renewed abundance without freezing it?
```

The abundance interpretation prepares these questions but does not answer them.
Its value is that it moves the problem from output to persistence. It begins
with the proliferation of basins and ends with the unresolved question of what
can carry itself through their proliferation.

That unresolved question is the opening toward agency.

---

## 8. Conclusion

There are not two unrelated abundances. There is structural abundance and there
is the interpretation of structural abundance from inside becoming.

Structural abundance is native to RC. It names the way a non-uniform,
coherence-conserving, self-referential system can produce differentiated,
self-reinforcing, and renewable identity basins. It is a dynamical claim about
coherence, geometry, flux, collapse, spark, and redistribution.

Abundance interpreted through the Arc asks what this structural tendency means
for an embedded observer. It appears as novelty, surprise, uncertainty,
spaciousness, anxiety, cost, coupling, and the need to distinguish real
capacity from supported appearance. It is not simply more. It is not simply
good. It is a regime of becoming that must be read with discipline.

The Arc provides that discipline. It keeps abundance from becoming inflation by
asking what appeared, what class it deserves, what support made it possible,
what survives withdrawal, what can become native, and what history should be
integrated into the next encounter.

The central claim can be stated plainly:

```text
Structural abundance begins as a dynamical claim about basin proliferation.
Interpreted through the Arc of Becoming, it becomes a disciplined account of
novelty, cycle, cost, and surplus capacity from inside irreducible change.
Its limit is persistence: abundance explains how more forms appear, but it
opens the further question of what can hold through their appearance,
collapse, redistribution, and renewal.
```

That further question is the threshold of agency.
