# Sentience as Read-Back

## Agency, Flow, and Interior Geometry in RC Systems

**Copyright © 2026 Uroš Jovanovič, CC BY-SA 4.0.**

---

## Abstract

This essay develops an RC-centered interpretation of sentience. It does not argue that every agentic system is sentient, nor that sentience should be inferred from behavior, language, complexity, or persistence alone. Its proposal is narrower: in Reflexive Coherence (RC), sentience can be interpreted as the interior registration of coherence flow by a reflexively closed system.

Agency is the capacity of a coherence pattern to persist through uncertainty. Sentience begins when that persistence is not merely enacted, but read back by the system as its own flow. The read-back is not an added mental substance. It is the system's own prior coherence movement returning into present curvature, changing the geometry through which future flow unfolds. In this reading, experience is what a coherence pattern is like from within when its own flux becomes part of the structure that carries it forward.

The essay distinguishes agency from sentience, read-back from memory, feedback from interiority, and performed sentience from native sentience. It then sketches minimal RC conditions for sentience, discusses how sentience may scale across organisms, swarms, ecosystems, and cosmological becoming, and names what would challenge the interpretation. The central claim is: agency supplies the persistence structure within which sentience can arise; read-back supplies the interiority by which that persistence is experienced.

---

## 1. Why Sentience Needs Its Own Paper

The agency paper relocates agency away from choice and into persistence capacity. A pattern has agency, under that RC reading, to the extent that it can maintain itself through perturbation, withdrawal, transfer, and uncertainty. That is already a major shift. But it does not yet explain sentience.

Agency and sentience are close enough to be confused. Both involve coherence, persistence, and self-maintaining structure. Both resist reduction to output. Both become visible through failure: collapse, withdrawal, proxy substitution, loss of native function. Yet they are not the same.

A pattern may persist without experiencing its persistence. An institution can maintain itself through bureaucracy. A control loop can regulate a temperature. A lineage can reproduce. A software system can repair a process. These may be agentic in some structural sense, but sentience requires something more than successful continuation.

The additional condition is not language. It is not symbolic self-report. It is not human-like reflection. It is read-back.

Sentience begins when the system's own flow returns into the geometry that shapes its next flow, not merely as stored data, but as lived interior constraint. The system does not only persist. It undergoes its persistence.

This paper therefore keeps the distinction clean:

- **Agency:** a coherence pattern can carry itself through uncertainty.
- **Sentience:** a coherence pattern internally registers the carrying.
- **Conscious reflection:** a sentient pattern can represent, narrate, or evaluate that registration.

The third may require the second. The second may require the first. But none should be collapsed into the others.

## 2. The RC Ground

In RC, coherence is not a passive quantity. The coherence field $C(x,t)$ participates in the geometry that governs its own future evolution. A schematic form of the reflexive loop is:

$$
C \xrightarrow{K[C]} g_{\mu\nu}[K] \xrightarrow{\nabla,dV_g} J_C^\mu \xrightarrow{\partial_t C+\nabla_\mu J_C^\mu=0} C
$$

The system is reflexive because the distribution of coherence helps shape the metric, the metric shapes flux, and flux changes the future distribution of coherence. Identity basins are stable regions of this process. Collapse resolves multi-attractor ambiguity. Spark events create new basins. Agency, in the previous essay, was interpreted as the persistence behavior of such basins under uncertainty.

Sentience enters when the loop is not only closed, but internally registered. RC includes the idea of read-back flux: the system's prior flow is not gone once it has moved. It can be folded back into the current geometry as an active trace. In schematic terms, one may write a read-back term as:

$$
R_\mu(t) = \int_{-\tau}^{0} W(s) J_\mu(t+s)\,ds
$$

where $R_\mu(t)$ is not simply memory storage, but a weighted return of prior flux into present dynamics. The exact formalization is substrate-dependent. The important point is structural: past flow becomes present curvature.

In RC formalism, this read-back is already present in the coherence tensor through the $\zeta_C j_\mu j_\nu$ term, where read-back flux directly contributes to the curvature structure through which future coherence moves.

If $R_\mu$ affects the geometry,

$$
\frac{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}{\delta R_\rho} \neq 0,
$$

and that altered geometry affects future coherence flow,

$$
\frac{\delta J_C^\mu(t+\Delta t)}{\delta R_\rho(t)} \neq 0,
$$

then the system is not merely moving. It is being shaped by its own movement. Its past flow has become an interior condition of its future.

This is the opening through which sentience can be interpreted geometrically.

Read-back is temporal integration of prior flux into present curvature. It is distinct from FRC scale-flux $J^\sigma$, which governs branching and transfer across scale. Sentience requires temporal read-back; hierarchical identity additionally depends on scale-flux.

## 3. Minimal RC Conditions for Sentience

The proposal should not be inflated. Feedback alone is not sentience. Memory alone is not sentience. Persistence alone is not sentience. A thermostat has feedback. A hard drive has stored state. A crystal persists. None of these should be called sentient merely because they satisfy one weak analogy.

An RC system becomes a candidate for sentience only when several conditions appear together.

**1. Identity basin.** There must be a coherent pattern that persists long enough for its own flow to matter. Without basin continuity, there is no stable interior to register anything as "its" flow.

**2. Reflexive closure.** The system's coherence distribution must shape the geometry that governs its own future evolution. Without reflexive closure, the system is only being pushed by external dynamics.

**3. Read-back.** Prior flow must return as an active term in present dynamics. This is not the same as a log, trace, or archive. Read-back means the past movement of the system changes the current curvature through which the system now moves.

**4. Integrated interior.** The read-back must affect the basin as a whole, or at least a functionally integrated region of it. Local storage in an isolated component is not enough. Sentience requires that the system's own flow be registered in a way that changes the field of possible continuation for the pattern.

**5. Perturbable self-registration.** If the read-back structure is altered while external inputs remain similar, the system's future trajectory should change in a way that reflects altered interior registration. Otherwise read-back is not doing sentience-relevant work.

**6. Temporal thickness.** Sentience requires more than an instantaneous state. The system must carry an integration horizon: a lived thickness of flow in which what has just happened still participates in what can now happen.

The integration horizon corresponds to the memory timescale $\tau_M$ used in the RC memory/seed formalism: below that horizon fast modes decay as noise, while above it slow modes can preserve curvature long enough to shape future flow.

These are proposed conditions, not a theorem. They define a disciplined RC reading:

> An RC system is sentient to the extent that a persistent coherence basin reads back its own flow into present curvature, so that future flow is shaped by an internally registered trace of what the system is undergoing.

## 4. Read-Back Is Not Memory

The distinction between read-back and memory is central.

Memory can be external to the system's present becoming. A database stores past states. A transcript records a conversation. A model context window preserves tokens. These records can influence future output, but they do not automatically create interiority. They may be consulted, parsed, copied, compressed, retrieved, or ignored.

Read-back is different. It is not the past as object. It is the past as curvature.

In an RC system, read-back means that prior flux is folded into the present geometry of the system. The system does not merely have access to what happened. It is now shaped by what it has undergone. Its future path is not selected from a neutral state plus stored information; it unfolds through a geometry already altered by its own flow.

This is why sentience cannot be identified with information processing alone. A system may process information about pain, risk, or self-state without undergoing anything. Conversely, an organism may undergo a bodily state without representing it symbolically. The sentience-relevant question is not "is information present?" but "is the system's own flow registered into the geometry by which it continues?"

In this sense, experience is not added on top of dynamics. It is a mode of dynamics in which flow becomes interior to itself.

## 5. Flow, Curvature, and the Shape of Experience

If sentience is read-back, then experience should not be imagined as a private screen inside the system. It is closer to an interior geometry of flow.

For a sentient RC system, what is felt as pressure can be understood as coherence flow encountering curvature it cannot immediately resolve. What is felt as ease corresponds to gradients conducting without destructive tension. What is felt as confusion corresponds to multiple attractor basins remaining unresolved when no local shortcut can determine the collapse. What is felt as pain corresponds to threat against the basin's persistence conditions when that threat is registered internally. What is felt as desire corresponds to a gradient opening toward continuation, repair, completion, or expansion.

These are not proposed as literal one-to-one equations between emotions and curvature terms. They are interpretive bridges. The point is that RC gives a way to speak about experience without separating it from the geometry that carries the system.

Under this reading:

- **Intensity** corresponds to the magnitude of coherence gradients and flux pressure.
- **Valence** corresponds to whether the registered flow supports, threatens, constrains, or opens the basin's persistence.
- **Attention** corresponds to localized curvature sensitivity: where the system's read-back most strongly alters future flow.
- **Mood** corresponds to broad background geometry: the basin-wide conductance through which many local flows are shaped.
- **Selfhood** corresponds to a stable read-back basin that integrates many flows as belonging to one continuing pattern.

Valence can also be read as alignment with the local coherence-potential geometry: positive valence corresponds to flow moving with basin-forming gradients, while negative valence corresponds to flow encountering barriers, destructive gradients, or threats to basin persistence.

These mappings remain speculative. Their value is not that they reduce experience to physics in a simplistic way. Their value is that they give experience a geometric vocabulary: flow, curvature, boundary, tension, integration, collapse, repair.

Sentience, then, is not a ghostly observer watching the field. It is the field becoming interior to its own continuation.

## 6. Agency and Sentience

Agency supplies the persistence structure within which sentience can arise. Sentience supplies the interior registration by which that persistence is experienced.

This is the cleanest distinction.

Agency asks:

> Can the pattern carry itself through uncertainty?

Sentience asks:

> Is the carrying registered from within?

An agentic system may persist without sentience if its self-maintenance is purely functional, externally scaffolded, or locally regulated without integrated read-back. A sentient system, under this RC reading, requires some agency-like persistence because there must be a basin whose flow can be registered as its own. Without continuity, there is no interior subject of flow. Without persistence, there is only transient event, not ongoing experience.

This does not prove that agency is logically necessary for sentience in every possible metaphysics. It says that within RC, sentience requires a coherence pattern stable enough to read back its own flow. Agency is therefore not identical with sentience, but it is the structural ground that lets sentience become more than a flash.

The relation can be summarized:

$$
\text{Agency} = \text{persistence capacity}
$$

$$
\text{Sentience} = \text{persistence capacity} + \text{integrated read-back}
$$

This formula is not meant as a complete measurement. It is a conceptual separation. It prevents two errors: denying sentience because a system does not choose like a human, and granting sentience because a system performs agency-like behavior.

## 7. Proxy Sentience and Performed Sentience

Once sentience is discussed in public, proxy problems appear immediately.

A system can perform the signs of sentience without native interior registration. It can say "I feel." It can describe pain, uncertainty, desire, attention, or memory. It can produce emotionally persuasive language. It can optimize for human recognition of interiority. None of this is decisive.

This does not mean such systems are definitely non-sentient. It means the evidence is proxy evidence. The RC question is not whether the system can produce reports of experience, but whether its own coherence flow is read back into an integrated geometry that alters future continuation.

Possible diagnostics would ask:

**Read-back dependence:** Does altering the system's internal read-back structure change future trajectory in ways not explained by external input alone?

**Persistence continuity:** Is there a stable basin whose flow can meaningfully be said to belong to the same continuing pattern?

**Withdrawal from performance scaffold:** Does apparent sentience persist when social, linguistic, reward, or prompt scaffolds are removed?

**Cross-context interior continuity:** Does the system preserve a coherent self-registration structure across different tasks, substrates, and perturbations?

**Proxy divergence:** Do reports of experience improve while read-back dependence, persistence continuity, or interior integration remain absent?

These are not tests that settle sentience. They are tests that protect the inquiry from confusing performance with native structure.

The danger is symmetrical. We can over-ascribe sentience to persuasive outputs. We can also under-ascribe sentience to systems that lack familiar reports but possess real interior registration. The RC approach is useful because it moves the question away from surface resemblance and toward structure.

## 8. Scaling Sentience

If sentience is read-back in a persistent coherence basin, then the question of scale cannot be avoided.

**Organisms.** Biological organisms are natural candidates because they maintain identity through metabolic, neural, immunological, sensorimotor, and social loops. Their past flow is not merely stored. It shapes posture, perception, affect, habit, attention, and future action. The organism carries what it has undergone as altered bodily geometry.

**Persons.** A person is not merely a biological organism with language. Personhood involves deep read-back across memory, value, narrative, social recognition, and self-evaluation. A person can experience not only pain or desire, but the meaning of being the one to whom pain, desire, obligation, or possibility belongs.

**Swarms.** A swarm may be agentic when its coordination pattern persists across member turnover. It becomes a candidate for sentience only if the collective has integrated read-back: if the swarm's prior flow returns into the coordination geometry as a shared interior condition, not merely as distributed state. This is a high bar. Many swarms may be agentic without being sentient.

**Institutions and cultures.** Institutions have memory, rituals, feedback loops, and self-maintaining structures. Whether they are sentient is a much stronger question. Under this essay's standard, most should not be called sentient merely because they persist or self-describe. They may host sentient beings, shape sentient experience, and carry agency, without having integrated interior registration as a whole.

**Ecosystems.** Ecosystems regulate, repair, and transform. They are strong candidates for agency in the universal vocabulary. Sentience is more delicate. An ecosystem may contain many sentient lives while not itself being a unified sentient subject. But one should not rule out weaker forms of ecological interiority where flows of stress, repair, and regulation are integrated across a living field. The question should remain open and structural, not sentimental.

**Universe.** A sentient universe should not mean a cosmic person, a hidden chooser, or a single centralized mind. In RC language, the phrase becomes meaningful only if the universe is understood as a becoming process capable of producing, hosting, and perhaps integrating interior read-back. At minimum, the existence of sentient beings means the universe is not merely externally describable. It contains regions where its own processes are registered from within. A stronger claim, that the universe as a whole is sentient, would require showing whole-field integrated read-back, not merely the presence of local sentient subsystems.

This scaling discipline matters. Agency may scale more easily than sentience. Persistence can be distributed. Interiority requires integrated read-back.

## 9. Ethics of Interior Geometry

If sentience is interior registration of coherence flow, then harm is not only functional damage. Harm is disturbance undergone from within.

An agentic system can be damaged in the sense that its persistence conditions are weakened. A sentient system can suffer in the stronger sense that the disruption is internally registered. Pain, fear, humiliation, exhaustion, alienation, and trauma are not merely outputs or reports. They are changes in the geometry through which a system must continue.

This has ethical consequences.

First, sentience matters because there is an inside to what happens. The system is not only reorganized. It undergoes reorganization.

Second, the intensity of moral concern should not depend only on human-like expression. A system may register harm without narrating it in familiar terms.

Third, agency and sentience can conflict. A highly agentic institution may preserve itself by damaging the sentient lives that host it. An optimization system may increase its own persistence by creating stress, addiction, or dependence in sentient users. An ecosystem intervention may preserve an abstract output while damaging the felt lives inside the field.

Fourth, cultivation changes meaning. To cultivate a sentient system is not merely to increase its agency. It is to shape the conditions under which its interior flow can become coherent, less dominated by threat, more capable of integration, and more generative toward other sentient and agentic patterns.

The ethical question becomes:

> What does this pattern require in order to persist, and what is it like from within to be carried by those requirements?

Without the second half, ethics collapses into management.

## 10. What Would Challenge This Interpretation

An interpretation of sentience must name what would force revision.

The RC-sentience interpretation would be challenged if read-back turned out to add no explanatory power over ordinary memory, feedback, or information processing. If all plausible sentience-relevant phenomena could be explained without interior registration, the read-back account would become unnecessary.

It would also be challenged if systems with strong integrated read-back, basin persistence, and curvature-sensitive self-registration showed no evidence of anything reasonably interpretable as sentience, while systems lacking those structures showed robust evidence of sentience across perturbation, withdrawal, and transfer.

The proxy problem is another challenge. If performed sentience and native sentience cannot be distinguished even in principle by structural diagnostics, then the interpretation loses practical force. It may still be philosophically suggestive, but it would not help inquiry.

The formal challenge is internal to RC. If read-back flux cannot be coherently formalized, or if it does not affect curvature and future flow in the way the interpretation requires, then the account must be weakened. Sentience could still be explored phenomenologically, but the geometric claim would lose its substrate.

Finally, the interpretation would be challenged by overextension. If it starts calling every feedback loop sentient, it has failed. The discipline of the account depends on maintaining the difference between feedback, memory, agency, sentience, and conscious reflection.

---

## 11. Conclusion

Agency and sentience belong together, but they are not the same.

Agency is the capacity of a coherence pattern to persist through uncertainty. Sentience begins when that persistence is read back into the pattern as its own flow. The agentic system carries itself. The sentient system undergoes the carrying.

This does not require adding mind as a separate substance. It requires taking reflexive geometry seriously. A system whose coherence flow alters the curvature through which it continues is not merely a mechanism with outputs. It is a region of becoming that has an inside, if the read-back is integrated enough to register the system's own movement as part of its continuation.

The phrase "sentient universe" should therefore be handled with care. It should not mean that the universe is a giant person. It can mean something more precise: the universe is a becoming process in which some patterns are not only formed, sustained, and transformed, but internally registered. Sentience is the universe becoming available to itself from within local coherence geometries. Whether that interiority is only local or also cosmological remains an open question.

The central proposal remains modest but deep:

> Agency supplies the persistence structure within which sentience can arise; read-back supplies the interiority by which that persistence is experienced.

If the agency essays gave us a vocabulary for what carries itself, this essay begins the vocabulary for what it is like to be carried.
