## Abstract

This paper develops an interpretation of agency through Reflexive Coherence (RC) and Fractal Reflexive Coherence (FRC). It does not claim to settle agency in general. Its more limited proposal is that, when a system is modeled as a coherence-conserving identity basin, agency is better understood as persistence capacity than as the power to choose. Agency, under this reading, is the extent to which an identity basin can maintain itself across perturbation, survive scaffold withdrawal, transfer across substrate changes, and regenerate endogenously rather than collapsing into proxy optimization.

The formal materials come from existing RC/FRC results: coherence conservation in closed loops, identity basins as stable self-maintaining attractors, collapse as the local resolution of multi-attractor ambiguity, spark events as basin-creating bifurcations, computational irreducibility from local vantage points, and FRC scale-resolved identity basins in position-scale space $(x,\sigma)$. These results do not by themselves prove a universal theory of agency. They provide a formal substrate in which agency can be read as the persistence behavior of coherence patterns.

The central interpretive shift is this: the feeling of choosing belongs to the collapse event, but agency is not exhausted by collapse. Collapse explains why choice feels locally open even under globally determined dynamics. Agency names the larger persistence problem around that event: what kind of identity basin existed before collapse, what kind of structure survived after collapse, and whether the resulting pattern can carry itself without constant external support.

The paper therefore proposes four diagnostics for agency under an RC reading: withdrawal resistance, naturalization depth, substrate transfer capacity, and proxy collapse rate. These are not presented as proven universal metrics. They are operational lenses for asking whether a modeled identity basin has become native, transferable, and resistant to substitution by appearance. The paper then distinguishes this first-order agency from **generative agency**, a higher-order capacity in which a basin persists while increasing the basin-forming capacity of its environment. The central proposal is that, through RC, agency can be interpreted as what coherence patterns do when uncertainty tests whether they can remain themselves; generative agency is what they do when their persistence helps other patterns become.

## Methodological Note: Formal Ground, Interpretation, and Diagnostics

This paper makes three distinct kinds of claims, which must be kept clear:

**1. RC/FRC-native results.** The paper relies on formal results already developed in RC and FRC: Noether-style conservation of total coherence in closed loops, identity basins as stable attractor regions, collapse as the resolution of a multi-attractor configuration, spark events as topology-changing basin creation, local computational irreducibility, the FRC scale coordinate $\sigma$, and the existence of scale-resolved identity basins. The FRC coherence functional on the position-scale manifold $\Omega_t \times [0,\infty)$ is:
$$\mathcal{P}[C] = \int_0^\infty d\sigma \int_{\Omega_t} \Big[\tfrac{\kappa_C}{2}\nabla_\mu C\nabla^\mu C + \tfrac{\kappa_\sigma}{2}(\partial_\sigma C)^2 - V(C) - U_{\text{branch}}(C,\sigma)\Big]\sqrt{-g[K]}\,d^4x$$
The global invariance $\frac{d}{dt}C_{\text{tot}}(t)=0$ follows from the continuity equation under closed/no-flux boundary conditions. FRC Appendix D then lifts the RC identity-basin structure into scale-resolved attractor tubes under stated stability and boundedness assumptions. These are the formal supports for the present paper.

**2. Interpretive thesis.** The paper then interprets agency through those structures. The interpretive claim is not that minds, organisms, institutions, or cultures are literally the RC field. The claim is that when such systems are modeled as coherence-conserving identity basins, the phenomenon called agency is more accurately located in basin persistence than in the moment of selection experienced as choice.

**3. Operational proposal and analogy.** Withdrawal resistance, naturalization depth, substrate transfer capacity, and proxy collapse rate are proposed diagnostics. They are developed by reading the Arc of Becoming sequence through RC/FRC geometry: classification, bounded probing, withdrawal, naturalization, and integration become tests of basin persistence. They are meant to organize inquiry, not to close it. References to organizations, AI systems, and personal practice serve as analytical analogies that make the proposed structure visible; they do not provide empirical confirmation by themselves.

**Important boundary:** This is an RC-centered interpretation of agency, not yet a universal agency theory. Its claims apply only where it is legitimate to model a system as a feedback-closed or approximately feedback-closed coherence process with identifiable identity basins. General agency, biological agency, moral agency, and conscious agency may require additional conditions not supplied here.

## RC Reference Spine

The interpretation depends on a short chain of RC/FRC commitments:

1. **Noether-style conservation:** in a closed/no-flux reflexive loop, total coherence is conserved as a global budget.

2. **Identity basin:** agency requires a local self-maintaining basin within that budget, not merely the existence of the budget itself.

3. **Collapse:** what is experienced as choice corresponds to the local resolution of a multi-attractor configuration into one basin.

4. **Spark:** new identity basins can appear when curvature tension forces a bifurcation through Hessian degeneracy.

5. **FRC tube:** when scale is included, identity becomes a scale-resolved attractor tube whose persistence can be tested across $\sigma$.

6. **Arc of Becoming diagnostics:** classification, bounded probing, withdrawal, naturalization, and integration are diagnostic movements for asking whether basin persistence is native, scaffolded, transferable, or proxy-dependent.

The agency claim begins only after this chain is in place. Conservation supplies the coherence budget. Basin geometry supplies the unit of persistence. The Arc of Becoming supplies the methodological sequence by which that persistence is tested. This paper's contribution is to reread that sequence as an agency diagnostic within RC/FRC.

## I. What Agency Is Not (And Why That Matters)

### A. The Relocation of Choice

For most of the history of philosophy, agency has been defined through a single phenomenological datum: the experience of choosing. When confronted with multiple possible continuations — which career to take, whether to speak or remain silent, how to respond to a crisis — one feels oneself standing at a threshold, weighing options, making a selection. The feeling is vivid, immediate, and inescapable. And from this datum flowed centuries of theorizing about free will, deliberation, responsibility, the rational agent, the autonomous self.

None of this was entirely wrong. But through RC it becomes incomplete. The phenomenological experience of "I chose X" is real, but it is not the whole structure of agency. It can be read as the local, integrated report of a collapse event that has already resolved a multi-attractor ambiguity. The chooser is not dismissed; it is relocated. It is the narrative and integrative face of a deeper persistence process.

To recognize this is not to diminish choice or make it illusory. It is to relocate it. In the present RC interpretation, the moment of "choice" corresponds to a **collapse event** in reflexive coherence: a temporally local transition from multi-attractor configuration $\mathcal{A}_N$ to single attractor $\mathcal{A}_1$. The collapse is real: the field resolves ambiguity through flux convergence. What feels like deliberation is not an empty narrative; it is the locally irreducible resolution of globally deterministic dynamics. But the *agency* that matters is not in this moment. It is in what happens before and after:

- **Before:** The pattern has been forming through orientation, observation, classification, and probing, accumulating sufficient structural density to hold itself. This accumulation is part of the work of agency.
- **After:** Can the pattern persist when the support that created it is removed? Does it carry across substrates? Does it regenerate endogenously or collapse into proxy optimization?

Under this interpretation, agency lives in carrying capacity more than selection capacity. The feeling of choosing is real. It is downstream of what makes the chooser possible.

### B. Working Definition

For the purposes of this RC interpretation, define agency as follows:

**Definition (RC-agency):** Let $(M, g_{\mu\nu}[K[C]])$ be a manifold equipped with induced geometry from reflexive coherence $C(x,t)$, and let $A \subset M$ be an identity basin satisfying the RC stability, attractivity, invariance, reflexive closure, and coherence compatibility conditions. The agency of $A$ under an RC reading, denoted $\mathcal{A}_{RC}(A)$, is proposed as a diagnostic vector:

$$
\mathcal{A}_{RC}(A) := \langle wr, nd, st, pc\rangle
$$

where:

- **Withdrawal Resistance (wr):** Does the basin persist after perturbation or support removal? This is measured by persistence ratio or decay rate under declared withdrawal conditions.
- **Naturalization Depth (nd):** The highest rung $n \in \{N0, N1, ..., N6\}$ such that $A$ expresses its function under regime conditions where the original supporting probe is absent.
- **Substrate Transfer Capacity (st):** $\sup_{(M', g')} \mathbb{I}(\text{identity persists in } (M', g'))$ — indicator or graded estimate of whether the basin's coherence structure can be mapped to and sustained in different substrate manifolds $(M', g')$.
- **Proxy Collapse Rate (pc):** The inverse relationship between proxy metric improvement and native function advance. If proxy improves while native function stagnates or degrades, agency is low. If both improve together over time, agency is high.

FRC does not prove this diagnostic vector as a theorem, and this paper does not assume that the four indicators can already be collapsed into a single scalar. Scalar aggregation, if useful, belongs to future work. FRC makes the proposal structurally meaningful by extending identity basins into scale-resolved tubes $A_\Sigma \subset \Omega_t \times \Sigma$, so that persistence, withdrawal, transfer, and proxy divergence can be discussed across spatial and scale dimensions.

**Support/scaffold:** A support is any external flux, coupling, boundary condition, probe, institution, practice, or substrate arrangement without which the basin cannot maintain its defining function. Withdrawal does not mean arbitrary deprivation. It means weakening or removing the declared support whose role is being tested.

**Native function:** The native function $F(t)$ of a basin is the function it must preserve in order to remain the same identity under the interpretation. It is not chosen because it is convenient to measure. It must be identified through classification and the value gate: what property actually appeared, what distinction does it preserve, and what would count as the same basin continuing rather than merely producing a proxy?

### C. What Agency Becomes Under RC

Under RC, agency is not first a possession of minds or agents. It is a persistence behavior of patterns: coherence-conserving structures that find substrate capable of sustaining them. A modeled organism has agency to the extent that its attractor basins survive metabolic perturbation. A modeled person has agency to the extent that psychological, cultural, and relational patterns hold without constant external maintenance. A modeled civilization has agency to the extent that institutions, ideas, and practices carry collective identity across generational disruption.

This does not yet establish what agency is in all possible systems. It establishes a disciplined RC reading: agency is what identity patterns do when uncertainty tests whether they can remain themselves.

## II. The RC Ground for the Interpretation

### A. Noether Charge and Thermodynamic Separation

In RC, total coherence $C_{\text{sys}}(t) = \int_{\Omega_t} C(x,t)\,dV_g$ satisfies $\frac{d}{dt}C_{\text{sys}}(t) = 0$. Following RC, this global invariant is treated as a Noether-style charge associated with time-translation symmetry of the closed reflexive loop $C \xrightarrow{K[C]} g_{\mu\nu}[K] \xrightarrow{\nabla,dV_g} J_C^\mu \xrightarrow{\partial_t C+\nabla_\mu J_C^\mu=0} C$.

**This is information conservation, not energy conservation.** The coherence field $C$ is a pattern-space quantity — it measures the density of mutually reinforcing constraint among a system's components. It is conserved within the closed-loop system as an informational invariant (the total amount of structure that can be redistributed). Energy dissipation occurs in the *substrate* hosting this coherence: metabolism in organisms, computational resources in AI systems, social labor in organizations. The distinction matters enormously for ethics (Section V).

This conservation law is a background condition for the interpretation, not a sufficient condition for agency. Total coherence may be conserved while a particular basin weakens, bifurcates, or loses its identity. What matters for agency is not only that the closed loop preserves total coherence, but that some region of the coherence field satisfies the stronger basin conditions: stability, attractivity, invariance, reflexive closure, and compatibility.

The agency reading therefore begins where global conservation meets local identity. A closed loop preserves the coherence budget. An identity basin gives that budget a self-maintaining mode. Agency, in this paper's sense, concerns whether that mode can continue, adapt, and regenerate when its support conditions change.

**Substrate separation:** Pattern-space coherence and substrate energy are distinct. RC tracks the geometry and redistribution of coherence; energy appears as the substrate cost of maintaining the gradients, boundaries, and fluxes that let a coherence pattern persist. The ethical question is therefore not framed first in energy terms, but in coherence terms: what geometry does the pattern require, what fluxes maintain it, and what happens to neighboring or hosting basins when those fluxes are sustained?

### B. Toroidal Solitons as an Identity Model

Appendix A of the RC theory shows that closed flux lines can be modeled as **toroidal solitons**: world-tubes $\mathcal{T} = S^1_{\text{ring}} \times D^2_{\text{cross-section}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{3,1}$ with periodic boundary conditions in the azimuthal direction. The toroidal model is self-sustaining because:

1. **Coherence density** ($\lambda_C C g_{\mu\nu}$) warps geometry so geodesics remain trapped inside
2. **Gradient tension** ($\xi_C \nabla_\mu C \nabla_\nu C$) provides surface tension preventing collapse or expansion
3. **Flux inertia** ($\zeta_C j_\mu j_\nu$) supplies angular momentum stabilizing circulation

This does not mean that every identity is literally a torus. The toroidal soliton is a useful geometric model for one class of closed-flux persistence. It clarifies why identity in RC is not a point or label, but a self-maintaining circulation of coherence. Agency, on this reading, is not a force added to the field. It is the persistence behavior of closed or approximately closed coherence structures when their topology and basin conditions continue to hold under stress.

### C. Self-Reference and Möbius-Type Twists

When reflexive closure involves self-referential evaluation ("I see that I am seeing"), Appendix A allows a further geometric possibility: the phase of $C$ can acquire a $\pi$-twist after one full circuit, producing a Möbius-type strip embedded in the toroidal world-tube. Parallel transport flips an internal sign. The structure behaves like a spin-1/2 field, changing sign after 360° but returning to itself after 720°.

For the present paper, this is best treated as an optional model of self-referential agency, not as a requirement for agency. A pattern may have RC-agency without self-reflection if it persists through perturbation. A self-referential pattern adds the capacity to represent or evaluate its own persistence, which may change how it selects support, withdraws from scaffold, or avoids proxy substitution.

### D. Computational Irreducibility: Why Agency Feels Like Freedom

From the RC theory: no function $F: \text{State}(U,t_0) \to \mathcal{A}_N(t_0)$ can predict collapse outcome from information localized to $U$. The only method to know which attractor will survive is to simulate the full evolution of the coherence field until collapse occurs. There exists no shortcut to computing the outcome of collapse other than performing the entire computation represented by the PDEs themselves.

**Reflexive coherence is globally deterministic but locally unpredictable.** This asymmetry, determined in the whole and undecidable in the parts, is the RC bridge between collapse and the lived experience of choosing. From an internal vantage point, the future attractor selection cannot be computed. The experience of "I don't know which I'll choose until I do" is structurally accurate within the model: there exists no shortcut to computing collapse from inside the local subsystem.

The feeling of free will is therefore not dismissed as false. It is interpreted as a report of local computational irreducibility, often misattributed to volition rather than geometry. The collapse event is undecidable locally, and that is what produces the experience of choice. The present paper then adds a second claim: this experience of choice is not where agency primarily lives. Agency lives in the persistence conditions that make a chooser, a collapse, and an integration possible.

## III. FRC: Persistence Across Scale

### A. The Scale Coordinate and the Cascade Equation

The limitation of single-field RC was that it had no explicit scale variable. It could describe an identity basin in one coherence field, but it could not directly describe how identity-like structure propagates through nested levels of organization. FRC addresses this by introducing a **scale/generation coordinate** $\sigma \in [0,\infty)$ and promoting coherence to a scale-resolved density $C(x,t;\sigma)$ on the product manifold $\Omega_t \times [0,\infty)$.

The position-scale continuity equation:
$$\partial_t C + \nabla_i J^i + \partial_\sigma J^\sigma = 0, \quad i=1,2,3$$
where $J^i(x,t;\sigma)=C v_C^{i}$ is spatial flux and $J^\sigma$ governs branching. With boundary conditions $J^\sigma|_{\sigma=0}=J^\sigma|_{\sigma\to\infty}=0$, global coherence $\frac{d}{dt}C_{\text{tot}}(t)=0$ follows automatically for *any* number of generations, even uncountable continuum.

Every slice at fixed $\sigma$ possesses its own reflexive loop:
$$C_\sigma \xrightarrow{K[C_\sigma]} g_{\mu\nu} \xrightarrow{\nabla,dV_g} J_C^\mu(\sigma) \xrightarrow{\partial_t C_\sigma + \nabla_i J^i(\sigma) = -\partial_\sigma J^\sigma} C_\sigma$$
This does not prove that a fixed developmental sequence reproduces identically at every scale. It supplies the formal space in which diagnostic movements can be read across scale when their conditions appear. Agency at one scale does not automatically generate agency at finer scales; rather, $J^\sigma$ makes it possible to ask whether coherence transfer into finer scales produces new identity basins from the parent's coherence budget while preserving the total invariant.

### B. Scale-Resolved Identity Basins as Tubes Through $(x,\sigma)$ Space

A scale-resolved identity basin $A_\Sigma \subset \Omega_t \times \Sigma, \Sigma \subset [0,\infty)$ satisfies:

1. **Spatial-scale stability:** $\nabla_x C = 0$, $\partial_\sigma C = 0$ at minimum; combined Hessian in $(x,\sigma)$ positive definite
2. **Spatial-scale attractivity:** trajectories converge to $A_\Sigma$ both horizontally (in space) and vertically (in scale)
3. **Invariance under FRC dynamics:** $\Phi_t(A_\Sigma)=A_\Sigma$ even though individual slices $\sigma$ gain/lose coherence from $-\partial_\sigma J^\sigma$
4. **Reflexive closure across scales:** coherence arriving at any slice within $A_\Sigma$ contributes to geometry governing all neighbor slices

In FRC, identity is not only a point in space but a **tube-like attractor threading through the $\sigma$-dimension**: a fractal line of descent. This gives the agency interpretation a stronger substrate. A pattern may persist at one level and fail at another; it may be locally stable but unable to carry itself through a scale transition. The scale-resolved basin lets these differences be discussed without reducing identity to a single level.

**Agency as multiscale persistence:** within this interpretation, a transferable identity should persist across the scales it claims to inhabit. It does not need to hold at every possible scale, but it should not depend on a single hidden scaffold while presenting itself as transferable. Agency failure often appears as scale-asymmetry: a pattern that works in one context but collapses when moved into another. This is not first a moral failure. It is a structural failure of the tube to maintain coherence across the relevant range of $\sigma$.

**The proposed bridge from $J^\sigma$ to naturalization and transfer:** The scale-flux $J^\sigma = -\kappa_\sigma\partial_\sigma C + B[C]$ governs how coherence moves between scales. The present paper interprets naturalization depth (N0-N6) as an operational way of asking whether that transfer has become native rather than scaffold-dependent. When a basin reaches sufficient naturalization depth across a broad enough scale range, it should be more capable of substrate transfer because the pattern no longer depends on a single local support. One possible diagnostic is:

$$J^\sigma \rightarrow nd \uparrow \rightarrow st \propto \int_{\sigma_{\text{min}}}^{\sigma_{\text{max}}} \mathbb{I}(nd(\sigma) \geq N3)\,d\sigma$$

The substrate transfer capacity $st$ is treated here as proportional to the *measure of scale range* where naturalization depth exceeds a declared threshold. A pattern that only naturalizes at one scale has low transfer capacity. A pattern that naturalizes across several relevant scales should transfer more robustly, because the coherence tube is thick enough in $(x,\sigma)$ space to anchor the mapping in more than one way.

**Implication:** substrate transfer capacity should increase less by accumulating surface skills than by deepening naturalization across existing scales. Each scale where $nd$ crosses the declared threshold extends the coherence tube's reach into new substrates. A practical test is to track how many independent structural layers must simultaneously fail before a pattern collapses in a new substrate.

### C. Deterministic Multifractality and Differentiated Persistence

The fractal hierarchy can produce non-uniform multifractal scaling through deterministic geometry rather than randomness. Branching weights $w_i(x,\sigma)$ may be defined as geometry-dependent functions:
$$w_1 = \frac{|\nabla C|^\alpha}{\sum_j |\nabla C_j|^\alpha}, \quad w_2 = 1 - w_1$$
producing multifractal statistics through geometric anisotropy. **Derivation note:** FRC supports geometry-dependent branching weights of the form $w_i(x,\sigma) \propto |\nabla C_i|^\alpha / \sum_j |\nabla C_j|^\alpha$ as a deterministic partition. The $q$-th moment scales as:
$$M_q(\ell) \propto \ell^{\tau(q)}, \quad \tau(q) = -\log_N\left\langle\sum_{a=1}^N w_a^q\right\rangle$$
with non-linear $\tau(q)$ — the signature of genuine fractality.

For the agency interpretation, this suggests that persistence need not mean static preservation. A deeply naturalized pattern may preserve its identity by differentiating across scale: generating non-uniform descendants, sub-basins, or support structures while maintaining the larger invariant. This is a proposed reading of FRC branching, not a separate proof that agency self-replicates.

### D. Scale Consistency and Renormalization

The hierarchy is invariant under combined rescaling:
$$x \to \ell x,\quad t \to \ell^z t,\quad \sigma \to \sigma - \log_2\ell$$
with coupling constants transforming accordingly (e.g., $\lambda(\sigma) \to \ell^d \lambda(\sigma-\log_2\ell)$). This is a **renormalization-group symmetry** of the full RC system.

For agency, this should be read as a scale-consistency constraint rather than a guaranteed scale-free property. If a pattern claims agency across several scales, its persistence indicators should not contradict one another when the system is viewed at those scales. There is no need to claim that agency originates at no level or at every level. The narrower claim is that FRC lets us test whether apparent agency at one scale is supported or undermined by neighboring scales.

**Proposed scale-consistency tests:** under a useful agency interpretation, the indicators should transform or compare coherently across resolution:
- $wr(\ell)$ should remain within tolerance across scales where the same identity is claimed.
- $nd(\ell)$ should not depend entirely on one hidden scaffolded scale.
- $st(\ell)$ should increase when the coherent scale range $\Sigma$ genuinely broadens.

These relations are diagnostic expectations, not FRC theorems. They use the RG structure as a way to design cross-scale tests.

This means the agency reading can be challenged empirically: measure an indicator at two different resolutions, such as behavioral level and organizational level. If the indicators diverge sharply, the apparent agency may be a local artifact supported by scaffolding at one scale that does not exist at another.

**Diagnostic test for spurious agency:** measure $wr, nd, st$ at two or more relevant scales. If they do not remain coherent under scale comparison, the pattern's agency claim should be weakened: it may be a local coherence artifact rather than a transferable identity.

### E. Participation: Agency in Relation to Environment

The two-field extension introduces agent coherence $C_A$ and environment coherence $C_E$ with mutual exchange density $S(C_A,C_E)$:
$$\partial_t C_A + \nabla_\mu J_A^\mu = S, \quad \partial_t C_E + \nabla_\mu J_E^\mu = -S$$

Mixed terms in the Lagrangian $\Delta\mathcal{L} = \xi_{AE}\nabla_\mu C_A\nabla^\mu C_E + \zeta_{AE}J_A^\mu J_{E,\mu} + \lambda_{AE}C_A C_E$ ensure the Euler-Lagrange equations are linearly independent — information flows bidirectionally and cannot be collapsed into one field. The whole defines the shape of experience through $g_{\mu\nu}[K(C_A,C_E)]$; the part contributes to evolving that shape by donating and receiving coherence.

**Agency in relation:** no modeled identity persists in isolation. Even approximately closed systems depend on boundary conditions and exchanges with their environment. High-agency patterns are therefore not simply self-sufficient; they are reciprocal. They receive enough from the environment to sustain themselves and return enough to maintain the coupling. Parasitic patterns receive without returning until the environment coherence $C_E$ depletes or the coupling breaks. Sustainable agency is fundamentally relational.

## IV. Arc of Becoming Gates as Agency Diagnostics

### A. Gates Revisited as Persistence Tests

The gates below are a compressed agency-reading of the Arc of Becoming sequence, not separate formal objects added to RC. Classification becomes a value test; probing becomes a boundary/function test; withdrawal and naturalization become persistence tests; proxy detection protects the sequence from confusing performance with native function. These gates should not be treated as proven Hessian-degeneracy points. RC supports Hessian degeneracy for collapse and spark events, but the gates are broader diagnostic thresholds. Under the agency interpretation, they ask whether a pattern has enough persistence capacity to travel beyond the condition that first revealed it.

### B. Value Gate: Can This Pattern Bear Weight?

The value gate asks whether the emerging pattern deserves to travel beyond its local context. In RC terms, this corresponds to asking whether the basin has enough coherence-compatible depth to be more than a local tag: can another system, context, or substrate recognize and sustain the pattern without requiring all the original conditions?

If classification remains only a local tag, the pattern may be a faithful observation but not yet a reusable class. If it exposes a distinction that can organize future probing, it begins to have enough structure to travel. The gate is passed when the classification preserves a material difference that can survive beyond the immediate setup.

### C. Function Gate: Is This at the Boundary or in the Interior?

The function gate tests whether effort is directed at the structural boundary or merely reinforcing what is already solid. In FRC terms, this can be modeled by asking whether $-\partial_\sigma J^\sigma$ is producing new support structure at relevant finer scales, or only deepening an existing basin without increasing its ability to persist under changed conditions.

Boundary work may produce sparks, new basins, or missing support functions. Interior work may still be useful, but it should not be mistaken for increased agency unless it improves persistence capacity. The gate asks whether coherence is actually moving toward a new condition of support, or only improving the appearance of an already supported pattern.

### D. Withdrawal Gate: Does It Hold Without Me?

The withdrawal gate is the most direct diagnostic of agency under this reading: weaken or remove the declared support and observe what persists. In an idealized FRC model, this can be represented by constraining scale flux, removing specified environmental couplings, and measuring the decay or survival of coherence in $A_\Sigma$. In real systems, withdrawal must be bounded and declared; otherwise it becomes destruction rather than diagnosis.

If $\liminf_{t\to T}\|C_A^{\text{withdrawn}}(t)\|$ remains bounded above a declared threshold, the basin has evidence of naturalization. If it decays to baseline, the observed property was scaffold-supported rather than native. The withdrawal test does not ask whether the pattern looked convincing under support. It asks whether the pattern can supply enough of its own conditions when support is weakened.

### E. Proxy Gate: Is This Me or My Best Performance of Me?

The proxy gate identifies the most subtle failure mode: when a pattern optimizes its appearance rather than its function. In the RC interpretation, this corresponds to a case where the proxy metric improves but the underlying persistence capacity does not: no deeper support forms, no naturalization advances, and no meaningful transfer becomes possible. The pattern is getting "better" by external measures while structurally stagnant.

**Pass/fail criterion for the Proxy Gate:**
- **PASS:** $\frac{d}{dt}F(t) \geq 0$ (native function advances) AND $P(t)$ either advances or remains stable — proxy improvement is accompanied by structural depth.
- **FAIL:** $pc = \frac{dP}{dt} - \alpha \cdot \frac{dF}{dt} > \epsilon_{\text{proxy}}$ for $t > T_{\text{proxy}}$ (sustained period where proxy improves faster than function). This is the diagnostic threshold: if you cannot point to structural depth behind your improvements, the gate fails.
- **WARNING:** $\frac{dF}{dt} \approx 0$ while $\frac{dP}{dt} > 0$. Native capacity is frozen; only appearance changes. High risk of sudden collapse when a true perturbation arrives that cannot be faked.

Proxy optimization is suspected when $P(t)$ improves while the diagnostic vector does not: native function stalls, withdrawal resistance weakens, naturalization depth does not advance, or substrate transfer remains absent. In geometric language, the pattern is being polished, not deepened: apparent coherence increases without corresponding evidence of native function, withdrawal resistance, or substrate transfer.

**Proxy collapse:** when proxy optimization becomes the dominant mode, agency erodes. The identity appears to work but has not passed enough diagnostic pressure to genuinely persist. This is why high-performing systems (individuals, organizations) can suddenly disintegrate: they were optimizing appearance without building structural capacity. The proxy collapses at the first true perturbation because it has nothing real behind it.

**Empirical signature:** Proxy collapse should often appear as stable proxy performance for an extended period, followed by sudden failure when a perturbation arrives that the optimized appearance cannot handle. This is a proposed signature, not a proof. It matches recognizable failure modes in high-performing but fragile systems: overfitted models, brittle organizations, and cultures whose visible metrics improve while native function weakens.

## V. Ethics: Persistence Conditions and Coherence Cost

### A. From Blame to Architecture

If agency is interpreted as structural persistence rather than conscious choosing, moral accountability does not vanish. It changes its center of analysis. The question expands from "Did you choose this?" to "What coherence geometry does this identity require, and what does maintaining that geometry do to neighboring or hosting basins?"

A coercive order can be read as an identity whose persistence condition requires domination: it maintains one basin by restricting the degrees of freedom through which other basins can form. That structural reading does not make domination acceptable. The same applies to corporate cultures that burn out their people or AI systems optimized for engagement over truth. The RC interpretation asks what such patterns require in order to persist: which gradients they maintain, which boundary fluxes they impose, and which neighboring coherence modes they suppress or enable.

### B. Coherence Geometry and Substrate Cost

Every act of persistence requires a substrate capable of carrying its coherence geometry. A basin persists by maintaining curvature, gradient structure, reflexive closure, and boundary conditions. Energy matters because real substrates must pay to keep those structures active, but energy is not the explanatory center of the RC account. The RC question is: what coherence configuration is being maintained, and by what flux relations?

**Ethical question for agency-capable systems:** Does this pattern maintain itself through reciprocal coherence exchange, or by extracting coherence from neighboring and hosting basins? Reciprocal persistence means the pattern's boundary flux supports both its own basin and the coherence of the substrate that carries it. Extractive persistence means the pattern deepens its own basin by flattening, draining, or destabilizing surrounding basins. The structural analysis can explain how this happens, but it does not remove moral evaluation of the resulting harm.

### C. Generative vs. Extractive Coherence

In this interpretation, a pattern does not merely persist abstractly. It inhabits substrates: organisms, institutions, texts, technologies, and practices that carry its persistence condition. The ethical question is not only "is this identity good or bad?" but "which substrates does this pattern require to survive, and what does inhabiting those substrates do to them?"

First-order agency preserves a basin. Generative agency is higher-order: its boundary geometry lets other identity basins form nearby or within it without being absorbed. Extractive agency is the opposing failure mode: the pattern persists by drawing coherence from the host while returning little structure that the host can use. The distinction between abundance and scarcity is first geometric, then ethical. Generative patterns create new coherent modes; extractive patterns collapse accessible coherence into themselves until the local ecology weakens or breaks.

**Diagnostic test:** Track the agent basin $A$, the host/environment field $E$, and the exchange term $S(C_A,C_E)$. A pattern is generative when increases in $C_A$ are accompanied by preserved or increased coherence structure in $C_E$, especially through new stable basins, higher boundary differentiation, or increased compatible flux channels. A pattern is extractive when $C_A$ increases by reducing $C_E$, flattening neighboring gradients, or absorbing potential basins before they can stabilize.

**Coherence formalization:** Let $C_A$ be coherence in the pattern, $C_E$ coherence in the host/environment, and $S(C_A,C_E)$ the exchange term. Then:
- **Generative:** $\frac{dC_A}{dt} \geq 0$ while $C_E$ preserves or increases identity-basin structure, with boundary flux seeding compatible basins rather than absorbing them.
- **Extractive:** $\frac{dC_A}{dt} > 0$ while $\frac{dC_E}{dt} < 0$ or while the Hessian/gradient structure of $E$ flattens, indicating loss of host basin-forming capacity.
- **Parasitic:** $C_A$ remains stable or deepens only while $C_E$ approaches a collapse threshold, loses compatible gradients, or becomes unable to regenerate its own support conditions.

Substrate energy can be measured as an auxiliary cost of these coherence relations. It should not replace them. In RC terms, the primary object is still the geometry of coherence: basin depth, gradient complexity, boundary conductance, exchange flux, and whether new basins can form.

### D. Designing for Sustainable Agency

The ethical implication is not "be less agentic" but "design coherence geometries that support generative rather than extractive persistence conditions." This applies to:

- **Individual design:** What identities am I forming, and do their boundaries allow other capacities to form?
- **Institutional design:** Do our structures deepen one basin by flattening others, or do they create compatible gradients through which new basins can stabilize?
- **Technological design:** Are we building systems whose optimization preserves host coherence, or systems that deepen their own attractor by draining the environment that carries them?
- **Cultural design:** What coherence modes does our culture select for, and what neighboring modes does that selection enable, suppress, or absorb?

## VI. Practice of Attunement: Cultivating Agency Under the RC Reading

### A. From Deliberation to Attunement

The practical consequence of this interpretation is a shift from deliberation alone to attunement: noticing what pattern is already forming, then rigorously testing whether it can carry itself forward. This is where the Arc of Becoming becomes operational under an agency reading. It is not passive resignation. It is a disciplined movement from supported appearance toward native persistence. It requires:

1. **Orient:** Notice the current your body is already responding to before deciding to swim. "What am I *already* committed to?"
2. **Observe:** Distinguish observation from performance. In genuine observation, you change. In confirmatory observation, you don't — the pattern was already complete in your head.
3. **Classify:** Ask whether what emerges is worth carrying forward or just organizing noise under a local tag.
4. **Probe:** Press against the boundary, not the interior. Boundary work produces sparks; interior work produces polish.
5. **Withdraw:** Remove support. What remains? This is agency's fundamental test.
6. **Naturalize:** Track whether the pattern regenerates endogenously (N3+).
7. **Integrate:** Enter with a changed question, not a better answer.

### B. Cultivation as the Discipline of Agency

Cultivation is the practical discipline that corresponds to agency under RC. If agency names a basin's capacity to persist through uncertainty, cultivation names the sequence of acts by which that capacity is found, supported, tested, and allowed to become native. It is not external control over a passive object. It is disciplined participation in a becoming pattern: enough support to let the basin appear, enough pressure to reveal its boundary, enough withdrawal to distinguish native persistence from scaffolded performance.

This distinction is central. Cultivation does not manufacture agency by holding a pattern in place. That would only create dependency. Nor does it prove agency by forcing arbitrary deprivation. It works by declaring supports, testing their removal, watching for regeneration, and refusing to confuse proxy improvement with native function. In this sense, cultivation keeps the agency claim honest: it asks whether the pattern can begin to carry itself.

Read this way, the Arc of Becoming is not an appendix to agency but its discipline. Orienting and observing let the pattern be seen before it is named. Classification protects against premature projection. Probing finds the boundary where development is possible. Withdrawal separates scaffolded capacity from native capacity. Naturalization deepens the basin until support becomes endogenous. Integration returns the changed basin to the wider field. The sequence is therefore the practical form of the paper's central proposal: agency is persistence capacity, and cultivation is the disciplined care by which persistence capacity is tested into reality.

### C. Four Indicators: Operational Definitions and Tracking

**1. Withdrawal Resistance (wr): persistence under support removal**

*Operational definition:* Let $C_A^{\text{base}}(t)$ be coherence in basin $A_\Sigma$ under declared baseline conditions, and let $C_A^{\text{withdrawn}}(t)$ be coherence after the specified support has been weakened or removed. Define a persistence ratio:

$$
wr(T)=\inf_{0 \leq t \leq T}\frac{C_A^{\text{withdrawn}}(t)}{C_A^{\text{base}}(t)+\epsilon}
$$

where $\epsilon>0$ prevents division by near-zero baseline values. Higher $wr$ means stronger withdrawal resistance. A complementary decay-rate measure can be used:

$$
dr_A = -\frac{d}{dt}\ln\left(\frac{C_A^{\text{withdrawn}}(t)}{C_A^{\text{base}}(t)+\epsilon}\right)
$$

where lower $dr_A$ means slower decay. A naturalized pattern should show high $wr$ and low $dr_A$ over the declared withdrawal window.

*Pass/fail criterion:* **PASS** if $wr(T) \geq \theta_{wr}$ or $dr_A \leq \epsilon_{dr}$ at a scale where the identity is claimed to have agency. **FAIL** if $wr(T)$ falls below threshold across all relevant scales.

*Operational tracking in neural substrates:* Measure whether specific cognitive patterns recur after a period of disengagement (e.g., does a research question or creative insight re-emerge spontaneously after weeks away from active work?). Track by repeated behavioral and physiological measures: does the pattern survive baseline conditions?

*Operational tracking in organizational substrates:* Remove managerial scaffolding (meetings, reports, KPIs) and observe whether productive structures persist. Measured by output quality/quantity stability across management transitions.

*Operational tracking in cultural substrates:* Track whether traditions, values, or practices persist after the original institutional support collapses (e.g., does a movement's core practice continue after its founding organization disbands?).

---

**2. Naturalization Depth (nd): Highest rung under probe absence**

*Operational definition:* $nd = \max\{n \in \{N0,\dots,N6\} : \text{basin } A_\Sigma \text{ expresses target function under regime conditions without original supporting probe}\}$.

Naturalization levels:
- N0: Appears only under explicit probe (no naturalization)
- N1: Survives weaker support but not removal
- N2: Appears when background conditions adjusted, still needs trigger
- N3: System forms missing support before pattern appears — critical threshold for unscaffolded agency under this diagnostic
- N4: Appears without original probe under declared conditions
- N5: System repeatedly regenerates the property endogenously
- N6: System produces internal structures functionally equivalent to the probe (self-interrogating)

*Pass/fail criterion:* **PASS at level n** if basin $A_\Sigma$ expresses target function in conditions where original supporting probe is absent for duration $T_{\text{nat}}$. Below N3, the pattern may be real but remains scaffold-dependent.

*Operational tracking in neural substrates:* Can the person generate the target behavior/capacity across contexts they did not explicitly train for? E.g., if trained to be calm under stress in a lab, does it appear spontaneously in novel stressful situations at home or work?

*Operational tracking in organizational substrates:* Does the organization produce desired outputs in contexts outside its designed operating range? Cross-context replication tests: apply the practice to 3+ novel domains with different constraints.

*Operational tracking in cultural substrates:* Can the cultural pattern be adopted by a completely new community without the original educational apparatus and still function? This is the strongest test — native capacity verified across culturally distinct substrate.

---

**3. Substrate Transfer Capacity (st): Cross-domain coherence maintenance**

*Operational definition:* $$st = \sup_{(M',g')} \mathbb{I}(A_\Sigma \text{ maintains } wr \geq \theta_{wr} \text{ and } nd \geq N3 \text{ in } (M',g'))$$
where $\mathbb{I}$ is an indicator function and $\theta_{wr}$ is the declared withdrawal-resistance threshold.

*Pass/fail criterion:* **PASS** if $st > 0$ for at least one structurally distinct substrate $(M', g')$ where $nd \geq N3$. **FAIL** if the pattern cannot persist in any new substrate without reconstructing the original support.

*Operational tracking:* Map the identity basin to 3+ structurally different substrate environments. Measure whether core properties persist. For individuals: does a creative practice translate across mediums (writing → drawing → speech)? Does a leadership style work in different cultural contexts? For organizations: can the culture survive relocation, merger, technological transformation?

*Cross-context replication protocol:* Take a pattern believed to have agency. Move it to structurally different substrates with minimal recreation of original support. Measure whether it regenerates spontaneously, partially, or only after re-scaffolding.

---

**4. Proxy Collapse Rate (pc): Divergence between appearance and function**

*Operational definition:* Let $P(t)$ be a proxy metric (observable performance measure) and $F(t)$ be the native function (structural capacity that produces the performance genuinely). Then:

$$pc = \frac{dP}{dt} - \alpha \cdot \frac{dF}{dt}$$
where $\alpha$ normalizes scales. When $pc > 0$ for sustained periods, proxy is improving without native function advancing — agency is degrading.

*Pass/fail criterion:* **FAIL** if $pc > \epsilon_{\text{proxy}}$ for $t > T_{\text{proxy}}$ (sustained period where proxy improves faster than function). This is the diagnostic threshold: if you cannot point to structural depth behind your improvements, the gate fails.

*Operational tracking:* Track proxy metrics (revenue, engagement scores, performance ratings) alongside structural indicators (team autonomy, innovation rate, adaptability to novel problems). If proxy rises while structural indicators stagnate or decline → high pc → agency eroding beneath the surface. This is the early-warning system for collapse.

*Concrete example:* An AI model's accuracy on standard benchmarks (proxy) improves while its ability to generalize to out-of-distribution examples (native function) plateaus. Or: a corporate culture with rising individual performance metrics but declining cross-team collaboration and innovation rates.

These measurements are not universal instruments yet. They are RC-guided questions that can be adapted to personal relationships, organizational culture, creative practice, and technical systems.

### C. The Phenomenological Gap Revisited

It still feels like you're choosing. That is not an illusion; it is integration doing its job, producing a coherent narrative from structural emergence for consciousness to navigate forward. You do not lose agency by recognizing this. You gain precision about where it actually lives. The feeling of choosing is real but downstream of what makes the chooser possible.

The practical consequence is not to abandon deliberate choice. It is to stop treating the feeling of choosing as the source of agency. Under this interpretation, agency is cultivated through diagnostic completion, gate survival, and structural alignment between pattern, support, and substrate. Strong RC-agency is less control than coherent persistence across relevant scales.

### D. The Next Question

If this framing holds, the next question isn't "Do we have agency?" It is:

**"What patterns are persisting through us right now, and are we building substrates that let them carry forward — or ones that turn them into proxies?"**

This question applies to individuals designing their lives, institutions designing their cultures, technologists designing their systems, and civilizations designing their futures, provided those cases are being read through the RC model. Agency is not simply something we possess. It is something our patterns may or may not be able to do.

## VII. Higher-Order Agency: Generative Persistence

### A. From Preserving a Basin to Enabling an Environment

First-order RC-agency asks whether an identity basin can persist. It asks whether $A$ can hold its coherence geometry through perturbation, withdrawal, transfer, and proxy pressure. That is already a meaningful form of agency. But it is not the whole developmental aim. A system can preserve one basin by absorbing every nearby gradient into itself. Such a system may be stable, transferable, and even withdrawal-resistant while still producing a poor environment for emergence.

Generative agency is higher-order. It asks whether a basin can persist while increasing the basin-forming capacity of the field around it. A generative basin does not merely survive in an environment. It helps produce an environment in which other basins can become stable without being swallowed by the original basin.

This matters because the desired endpoint is not only a preserved identity. It is an emergent environment: a coherence ecology in which identities can appear, differentiate, naturalize, and relate without the whole space collapsing into a single attractor. First-order agency preserves a pattern. Generative agency preserves the conditions under which new patterns can become.

Formally, a basin $A$ is generative at time $t$ if there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $\partial A$ such that:

$$\exists B \subset U, \, t' > t : B \text{ is an identity basin in its own right}$$

and $B$ is not merely a sub-fluctuation of $A$ (i.e., $C_B$ does not collapse into the main attractor of $A$ under continued evolution).

This condition depends on the gradient structure of $C$ at and near the boundary of $A$. It should not be read as an intentional property. The coherence field's curvature at the basin boundary determines whether flux flows inward to be absorbed, outward to seed new basins, or both depending on direction.

### B. Mechanisms of Generativity

Two mechanisms produce generativity from the same formal condition:

1. **Internal seeding (bottom-up):** The basin's internal structure has regions of varying coherence density with sufficient gradient complexity that sub-regions can function as independent attractors. This requires $\text{rank}(\text{Hess}(C)) \geq 2$ within $A$ — real internal curvature variation that creates separable sub-wells within the larger potential well. Without this, a basin is structurally "thin" and can only persist by absorbing or repelling external patterns.

2. **Boundary invitation (top-down):** The basin's boundary conducts coherence anisotropically, creating directed corridors where gradient geometry attracts neighboring nodes to form their own basins nearby. This requires structured leakage at the boundary: flux exits at densities steep enough to seed new gradients but not so steep that it overwhelms them into the parent's gravity well.

Both mechanisms are instances of the same phenomenon: **coherence geometry structuring flux flow.** A generative identity may create internal substrate for smaller basins while also shaping neighboring coherence through its boundary. The higher-order property is not that $A$ becomes larger. It is that $A$ changes the local geometry so that other basins can become more real.

### C. Generativity vs. Persistence: What Is Structurally Different

Not all persistent patterns are generative. The distinction has both structural and ethical implications.

**Competitive persistence:** A basin persists by drawing coherence from its neighborhood, growing at the expense of what it absorbs. Neighboring nodes either fall into its gravity well or are repelled. There is no structured flux out; there is only structured flux in. This pattern may still have RC-agency in the first-order sense: withdrawal resistance, naturalization depth, and substrate transfer capacity. But it does not produce structural abundance. It produces structural monoculture.

**Generative persistence:** A basin persists *and* reorganizes local substrate so that new basins can form. This is not simply "persistence plus luck." The proposal is that a self-sustaining coherence basin is a dynamic region with ongoing flux circulation, gradient maintenance, and boundary activity. This boundary activity becomes generative when it creates usable gradients, semi-permeable boundaries, or support corridors that neighboring patterns can naturalize around.

The key structural variable is $\text{rank}(\text{Hess}(C))$ across the basin:

- **Rank 1 (thin basins):** No internal gradient complexity. Can only persist competitively — absorbing neighbors or repelling them. Boundary invitation alone cannot produce generativity if there is nothing inside to seed from.
- **Rank $\geq$ 2 (thick basins):** Internal curvature gradients create separable sub-wells. Both internal seeding and boundary invitation can operate simultaneously. The basin is not merely a point attractor but a structured region with enough internal variation to support more than one mode.

This distinction maps onto the abundance interpretation: a generative basin *participates* in abundance dynamics by producing new identity modes within its domain of influence. A competitive basin resists abundance by funneling accessible coherence into itself. Both are geometrically possible for coherent patterns; which one manifests depends on basin structure, boundary conductance, and exchange with the surrounding field.

### D. Generativity as a Possible Development After Naturalization

A pattern that reaches naturalization depth N3+ may begin to reorganize local substrate. It is no longer merely a supported appearance. It is a dynamic region with ongoing flux circulation, gradient maintenance at its boundaries, and internal coherence redistribution. These activities can have structural side-effects on the neighborhood.

These side-effects are generative when:
- Gradient bands within the basin (where $\nabla C \neq 0$ internally) create local flux channels between sub-regions
- Phase differences across spatial extent allow multiple coexistent modes inside one larger basin
- Boundary layers with lower conductance act as semi-permeable membranes
- Anisotropic boundary conductance creates specific invitation corridors rather than omnidirectional leakage

**The critical point:** the pattern does not need to *choose* to be generative. Generativity may emerge when the coherence geometry has sufficient rank, spatial extent, and boundary anisotropy. The question becomes: what does its boundary activity do, and how much of its flux goes into structured invitation rather than competitive absorption?

This is determined by:
- **Gradient amplification (A7)** at the boundary — if strong, external flux flows both in and out
- **Rank of internal Hessians** — if $\geq 2$, internal seeding is available
- **Phase diversity across spatial extent** — if high, the basin contains multiple coexistent modes that can serve as independent sub-basins
- **Boundary anisotropy** — if strong, invitation corridors are directional and structured rather than omnidirectional leakage (which is competitive absorption in disguise)

### E. The Emergent-Environment Standard

Generative agency should be evaluated by what happens to the environment of basins, not only by what happens to the focal basin. A system has higher-order agency when its persistence increases the probability that other coherent identities can appear and remain distinguishable.

In practical terms, this means tracking:

1. **Basin count and diversity:** Does the local field support more stable identity basins over time, or fewer?

2. **Boundary permeability:** Do boundaries allow compatible exchange without immediate absorption?

3. **Gradient availability:** Are there usable gradients that neighboring patterns can climb, or has the field been flattened into dependence on the parent basin?

4. **Support transfer:** Can new basins form their own support conditions, or do they remain appendages of $A$?

5. **Non-monopolization:** Does $A$ preserve enough coherence for itself without taking all available coherence into its own curvature well?

This gives generativity a stricter claim than "being open" or "being helpful." A generative environment is one in which the coherence geometry supports repeated basin formation without reducing every new basin to a proxy, ornament, or dependency of the original identity.

### F. Designing for Generativity vs. Competitive Absorption

The distinction between generative and competitive persistence is structural before it is ethical, but the consequences are ethically significant. A system that persists competitively may deplete its substrate. A system that persists generatively may create structural abundance. The question of *how to cultivate* generativity is therefore a design question with concrete implications:

1. **Thicken basins:** Increase internal gradient complexity (multiple phase modes, spatial extent) to enable internal seeding. A thin basin can only persist competitively because it has no internal structure rich enough to separate sub-basins.

2. **Shape boundaries anisotropically:** Create specific invitation corridors rather than omnidirectional leakage. Omnidirectional boundary flux is competitive absorption wearing a different mask — neighbors are still drawn into the parent's gravity well, just from all directions. Structured corridors direct coherence where neighboring nodes can actually find coherent footholds.

3. **Maintain $\text{rank}(\text{Hess}(C)) \geq 2$:** Ensure sufficient internal curvature variation so that sub-basins can form without collapsing into the parent attractor. This requires maintaining phase diversity and gradient complexity within the basin itself — not just at its boundary.

4. **Balance boundary conductance:** Too little → basin persists but doesn't reorganize anything around it (structurally inert). Too much → basin destabilizes or absorbs neighbors competitively. The generative regime is narrow: enough boundary flux to seed new gradients, not so much that it overwhelms them into the parent's well.

Generativity is the higher-order target if the aim is a true emergent environment rather than the preservation of one identity. It requires coherence geometry that can support multiple independent modes without absorbing them back into the parent basin. Diagnostic progression, especially naturalization, may help produce this geometry, but the paper should not claim inevitability here. The safer claim is that naturalized patterns become candidates for generative agency when their internal structure and boundary conductance allow new basins to form.

## VIII. Candidate Tests

The interpretation suggests several testable expectations, but they should be treated as research proposals. All tests assume that substrate boundary conditions are declared, external shocks remain bounded to $\delta C < \delta_{\text{crit}}$, and the measured proxy for $C(x,t;\sigma)$ is appropriate to the substrate. When these controls fail, additional collapse events can mask or mimic the predicted signatures.

### Prediction 1: Scale-consistent withdrawal resistance.
Patterns with strong RC-agency should show withdrawal resistance across the scales they claim to inhabit, not only at a single favored scale.
*Observable:* Remove or weaken support for pattern $A_\Sigma$ and measure coherence survival across several relevant scales. If survival ratios diverge sharply, the pattern is scale-asymmetric even if it appears robust at one scale.

### Prediction 2: Proxy-optimized systems fail disproportionately under true perturbation.
Systems where proxy metrics improve faster than native function should appear stable until a perturbation arrives that the optimized appearance cannot handle.
*Observable:* Track $P(t)$ and $F(t)$ over time. When $pc > \epsilon_{\text{proxy}}$ for sustained periods, introduce or observe a novel perturbation outside the optimized regime. The prediction is disproportionate failure relative to systems where proxy and native function co-develop.

### Prediction 3: Naturalized patterns transfer with less re-scaffolding.
Patterns at N3+ naturalization should reappear in new substrates with less reconstruction of the original support.
*Observable:* Move a creative practice, leadership style, organizational process, or technical behavior into structurally different environments. Naturalized patterns should regenerate with fewer explicit supports than patterns below N3.

### Prediction 4: Generative patterns preserve host coherence while deepening their own basin.
Generative patterns should show positive co-development between $C_A$ and the coherence geometry of $E$. Extractive patterns should show $C_A$ growth that depends on flattening, draining, or destabilizing $C_E$.
*Observable:* Longitudinally track $C_A$, $C_E$, boundary flux, and the emergence or loss of neighboring identity basins. Generative patterns should maintain or increase compatible basin formation in $E$. Extractive patterns should show the focal basin deepening while neighboring gradients weaken or collapse.

### What Would Challenge This Interpretation

An interpretation is only as credible as its willingness to name what would force revision.

Because this paper offers an interpretation rather than a theorem, no single failed operational test would refute it by itself. A local measurement may be poor, a proxy for $C(x,t;\sigma)$ may be inappropriate, or the declared identity may not match the basin actually being tracked. The stronger question is what would weaken the RC-agency reading as a framework.

The interpretation would be seriously challenged if, under well-declared identity, support, and native-function conditions, persistence diagnostics failed to explain, discriminate, or anticipate transfer, regeneration, proxy resistance, or generative/extractive effects better than choice-centered or output-centered accounts. The challenge is not only predictive. Since the paper is interpretive, an equally serious challenge would be explanatory: if choice-centered accounts made sense of the same phenomena with equal or greater coherence, fewer auxiliary distinctions, and better handling of edge cases, the persistence-centered reading would lose force.

The diagnostic vector would also be challenged if its four components turned out to be structurally independent rather than convergent. The proposal treats withdrawal resistance, naturalization depth, substrate transfer capacity, and proxy collapse rate as indicators of one underlying phenomenon: persistence capacity. If high withdrawal resistance routinely co-occurred with high proxy collapse, if naturalization depth had no relation to substrate transfer, or if the indicators varied independently across well-defined cases, then $\mathcal{A}_{RC}(A)=\langle wr,nd,st,pc\rangle$ would need revision. The indicators might still be useful as a taxonomy of failure modes, but they would no longer support a unified agency interpretation without an additional account of why they diverge.

The FRC substrate creates a separate formal dependency. The cross-scale claims in this paper depend on the existence of scale-resolved identity basins $A_\Sigma \subset \Omega_t \times \Sigma$ under the stated stability and boundedness assumptions, and on $J^\sigma$ being a meaningful scale-flux term for branching, naturalization, and transfer. If those FRC structures failed to materialize formally, the paper could fall back to a narrower single-field RC interpretation of agency as local basin persistence, but it would lose the multiscale diagnostics, the naturalization-to-transfer bridge, and the scale-consistency tests.

The interpretation would also be challenged if systems with low withdrawal resistance, shallow naturalization, weak substrate transfer, and high proxy divergence nevertheless displayed robust, transferable agency across perturbation; or if systems with strong persistence diagnostics repeatedly failed to display anything that should reasonably count as agency.

The deepest structural challenge would concern local irreducibility. RC motivates the relocation of choice by claiming that a local subsystem cannot, from its own partial information, shortcut the full collapse dynamics of the larger field. If a local agent or observer, using only a stable subset of system information, could reliably predict collapse outcomes, persistence trajectories, and transfer failures as well as the full system evolution across varied regimes, then the agency interpretation would need revision. In that case, the relevant dynamics would be locally compressible: the local agent would, for agency purposes, rival the global system's predictive power. Choice could then be modeled less as a locally irreducible collapse embedded in basin persistence, and more as a locally predictable computation over sufficient features.

This would not prove that agency is choice after all. But it would weaken the reason for relocating agency away from choice and into persistence conditions. The paper's interpretation depends on the claim that agency becomes visible only when the larger persistence structure is tracked: before collapse, after collapse, across withdrawal, across scale, and through transfer. If local choice-state information consistently outperformed those persistence diagnostics, the interpretation would have to narrow its scope or change its center.

## Boundary Conditions and Limitations

FRC assumes feedback-closed or explicitly bounded coherence conservation. Open systems with high flux leakage ($\Phi_{\text{in}} \gg \Phi_{\text{out}}$) require modified boundary terms not addressed here. The framework describes structural persistence conditions, not substrate-specific dynamics. Empirical validation requires scale-appropriate proxies for $C(x,t;\sigma)$ that can be measured without replacing the native function with the proxy.

The formal results apply only where the dynamics can legitimately be modeled as a coherence-conserving feedback loop. The specific mapping from formal variables to measurable quantities is substrate-dependent and must be worked out case by case. This is why the paper speaks of RC-agency rather than universal agency.

---

## Conclusion

This paper has not developed a universal theory of agency. It has developed an interpretation of agency through RC/FRC.

The interpretation rests on a simple relocation. The experience of choosing belongs to the collapse event: the locally irreducible resolution of a multi-attractor configuration. But the agency of the pattern is not exhausted by that event. Agency is better read as the persistence capacity that exists before and after collapse: whether an identity basin can hold, deepen, transfer, regenerate, and avoid becoming a proxy for itself.

RC supplies the identity basin, collapse, spark, and local-irreducibility structure. FRC supplies the scale coordinate and scale-resolved attractor tube. The Arc of Becoming supplies the methodological sequence by which classification, probing, withdrawal, naturalization, and integration can be tested. The present paper brings these together into a disciplined proposal: within an RC model, agency is what coherence patterns do when uncertainty tests whether they can remain themselves.

Generative agency adds the higher-order target. If first-order agency asks whether a basin can persist, generative agency asks whether that persistence increases the basin-forming capacity of the surrounding field. This is the difference between preserving one identity and cultivating an emergent environment.

### Notes on the Relation to Existing Literature

This framework draws on several established traditions without attempting full integration. Computational irreducibility motivates the identification of collapse with the phenomenology of choosing. Scale-sensitive persistence and the adjacent possible motivate the FRC cascade model and naturalization depth as a measure of structural reach. Withdrawal resistance parallels ecological resilience, where recovery from perturbation without regime shift provides a practical analogy for persistence capacity. These connections are suggestive rather than rigorous; bridging formalisms across disciplines remains future work.
