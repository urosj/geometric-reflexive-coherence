# Agency as Participatory Continuation

## Persistence, Ecological Return, and the Refinement of RC-Agency

**Date:** 2026-08-05

**Status:** Interpretive continuation and theoretical synthesis

Copyright © 2026 Uroš Jovanovič, CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Abstract

The first RC agency essays made a deliberate relocation. Choice was placed at
the collapse event, while agency was read through the persistence capacity of
the identity basin before and after that event. Withdrawal resistance,
naturalization depth, substrate transfer capacity, and proxy collapse rate
were proposed as diagnostics of whether a pattern could carry itself through
uncertainty rather than merely perform under support.

That relocation remains useful. Later work changes what must surround it.

The [Arc of Participation](../arc-of-participation/README.md) shows that the
identity whose persistence is being measured cannot be assumed independently
of context. Function is geometrically mediated and sometimes contextually
constituted. Identity forms through an ecological history. Participation
begins before intention because formation, persistence, resistance,
withdrawal, and dissolution already alter the field. The relation recurs
across scale, but coordination and stable plural ecology do not automatically
establish higher-order identity. Structural experience can be defined as
identity-relative undergoing without inferring phenomenal consciousness.

[Engineering Reflexive Coherence Ecologies](2026-07-EngineeringReflexiveCoherenceEcologies.md)
adds another consequence. An action is only a hinge in a longer unfolding.
The contribution changes a shared condition; the changed medium retains,
propagates, decays, or composes the effect; susceptible formations respond;
and the resulting ecology returns as part of the condition from which the
participant continues. Agency is therefore not tested only by whether a basin
survives an isolated perturbation. It is tested through the continuity carried
across ecological response.

This essay develops the resulting refinement:

> **RC-agency is the selectively enacted reflexive continuation of a locally
> continuous identity through uncertainty. Persistence capacity is its
> diagnostic spine. Contextual dependence, ecological return, and scale
> determine how that agency is constituted, attributed, and evaluated.**

The phrase *selectively enacted* does not restore an unconditioned chooser.
It means that the current identity geometry makes some differences
consequential, some continuations accessible, and some transitions more
likely, costly, or unavailable. Selection is enacted by a formed identity
inside the field, not imposed by an actor outside causality.

The original four diagnostics are retained as a focal persistence profile,
but they no longer stand as an exhaustive definition of agency. They require a
declared identity horizon, a context and support contract, and an unfolding
record that follows contribution through ecological consequence and return.
Nativity means that support relations have become regenerable or
transformable within the declared ecology, not that the identity has become
independent of all support. Transfer means reconstruction of a functional
geometry under a declared mapping, not context-free copying.

The refinement also changes collective and generative agency claims.
Coordination may organize participation without forming a higher-order
identity, and a stable ecology can be complete without becoming one agent.
Generativity can be carried through internal basin structure, boundary
activity, shared-medium history, support redistribution, or changes in wider
formation capacity. No single focal-basin mechanism should be treated as the
exclusive route.

The purpose of this paper is not to replace the earlier essays. It records what
became visible after them. The June essays remain the path by which agency was
first relocated from choice to persistence. The present essay continues that
path by placing persistence inside ecological participation.

## Formal Status and Chronological Method

This paper is an interpretive synthesis. It does not modify the RC continuity
equation, coherence functional, induced geometry, identity-basin criteria,
collapse condition, spark condition, or FRC scale formalism. Its equations and
profiles are conceptual scaffolds and research directives rather than final
metrics.

The paper composes four bodies of prior work:

1. [From Structural Abundance to Agency](2026-06-12-FromStructuralAbundanceToAgency.md)
   opens the persistence question.
2. [Agency of Becoming](2026-06-12-AgencyOfBecoming-InterpretationThroughRC.md)
   relocates agency from choice to persistence capacity and proposes the
   original diagnostics.
3. [Agency After Choice](2026-06-12-AgencyAfterChoice.md) and
   [Sentience as Read-Back](2026-06-12-SentienceAsReadBack.md) extend the
   interpretation into a broader vocabulary and distinguish agency from
   sentience.
4. The [Arc of Participation](../arc-of-participation/README.md) and
   [Engineering Reflexive Coherence Ecologies](2026-07-EngineeringReflexiveCoherenceEcologies.md)
   make identity formation, contextual function, shared-medium consequence,
   ecological return, scale, and structural experience explicit.

The chronology matters. A later synthesis should not be written backward into
the earlier papers as though no conceptual change occurred. Those papers state
the strongest interpretation available at their point in the sequence. This
paper states which parts remain, which become more precise, and which must be
narrowed.

That method preserves two kinds of legibility:

- **conceptual legibility:** a reader can identify the current account;
- **developmental legibility:** a reader can see why the current account
  became necessary.

The second is not merely archival. Reflexive Coherence is concerned with
history-bearing continuation. Its own conceptual development should retain
enough history to show how later distinctions were formed.

# 1. Why Persistence Must Be Revisited

## 1.1 The original relocation

The original agency argument begins with a useful asymmetry.

Choice is temporally local. An identity encounters several accessible
continuations and one continuation becomes enacted. In RC, this can be
interpreted through collapse from a multi-attractor configuration into a more
settled continuation.

Agency cannot be exhausted by that moment. The identity had to form before the
collapse. It had to retain enough continuity for the alternatives to be
alternatives *for that identity*. It had to survive or transform after the
collapse so that the consequence belonged to a continuing history rather than
to an isolated event.

The original essay therefore shifted attention from:

$$
\text{Which option did the system choose?}
$$

to:

$$
\text{What identity carried the transition before and after selection?}
$$

That shift remains.

## 1.2 What the persistence account made visible

Persistence capacity exposed several distinctions hidden by performance.

A pattern can produce the expected output only while a probe, controller, or
institution continually supplies its organization. Another can survive weaker
support but fail after full withdrawal. Another can regenerate a missing
support function. Another can transfer into a new realization while retaining
the relation that made its function recognizable. Another can optimize the
metric used to judge it while the native capacity disappears.

The four original diagnostics organize those cases:

$$
\mathcal D_I =
\langle
wr,
nd,
st,
pc
\rangle,
$$

where $wr$ is withdrawal resistance, $nd$ naturalization depth, $st$ substrate
transfer capacity, and $pc$ proxy collapse rate.

The profile remains valuable because it refuses to equate visible success with
identity continuity.

## 1.3 What persistence alone leaves unresolved

Persistence does not identify its own bearer.

A route can persist. A label can persist. A controller can preserve a stable
output. A coordination protocol can persist across member turnover. An
ecology can retain a regime. A higher-order identity can remain continuous
while constituents change.

These are not equivalent claims.

Treating them all as agency would erase the distinctions between occupancy and
identity, identity and participation, participation and agency, coordination
and higher-order identity, persistence and generativity, and structural
experience and sentience. Persistence therefore remains necessary, but it must
now answer a more precise question: *whose continuity persists, through which
context, at which horizon, and across what ecological response?*

# 2. What the Original Account Retains

## 2.1 Choice remains relocated

The later participation account does not return agency to a sovereign chooser
outside the field.

An identity acts from a boundary, susceptibility, history, support relation,
and set of accessible continuations that were formed before the current act.
Its deliberation and action are themselves field activities. The resulting
transition changes both identity and context.

Choice remains a local enactment inside becoming, not the source from which
becoming is externally directed.

## 2.2 Identity continuity remains necessary

Agency still requires an identity capable of carrying consequence.

Without a locally continuous identity, there is no stable distinction through
which a perturbation can be received, a response can be attributed, or a later
state can count as continuation rather than replacement.

The core RC identity conditions remain the starting point. Stability and
attractivity make the formation more than a transient concentration. Invariant
organization and reflexive closure make its continuation attributable across
change. Coherence compatibility places that continuation inside the wider
field, while retained history makes the claimed identity legible through time.

The Arc of Participation adds that these conditions must be read at a declared
horizon and through an effective formation context. It does not make them
optional.

## 2.3 Persistence diagnostics remain discriminating

Withdrawal, naturalization, transfer, and proxy integrity continue to answer
questions that output metrics cannot answer.

They ask whether the observed continuity survives a declared change in
support, whether it can regenerate or transform relevant support functions,
whether identity and function remain recognizable under a declared contextual
mapping, and whether the continuation belongs to the claimed identity rather
than to hidden producer state supplying its performance.

The diagnostics become stronger when context is added because the test no
longer pretends that a basin can be evaluated independently of the conditions
that make its function possible.

## 2.4 Generative and extractive persistence remain distinct

The original contrast between generative and extractive persistence also
survives.

A pattern can preserve itself by flattening neighboring gradients, consuming
shared reserve, monopolizing routes, capturing the medium, or making other
identities permanently dependent on its mediation. Strong focal persistence
can coexist with ecological damage.

Another pattern can persist while increasing compatible differentiation,
support circulation, repair, future composability, or the formation capacity
of the surrounding field.

The later engineering work expands the mechanisms through which these effects
can occur. It does not erase the distinction.

What the original account retains therefore does not yet establish where the
persisting identity comes from, how its function is constituted, or which
ecology carries its continuation. Those are the relations the Arc of
Participation makes unavoidable.

# 3. What the Arc of Participation Changes

## 3.1 The participant cannot be assumed

The original agency paper begins with an identity basin $A$ satisfying the RC
identity conditions. That is legitimate for a diagnostic paper, but it leaves
the formation of $A$ outside the immediate account.

The Arc of Participation fills that gap.

An identity forms through a field history involving nucleation, spark,
stabilization, and evidence-bound admission. Its boundary, susceptibility,
support dependence, accessible continuations, neighboring relations, and
initial perspective are formed through that history.

The identity is therefore not a context-free bearer later placed into an
environment. It is a local differentiation of the ecology.

This changes agency attribution. Before measuring persistence, inquiry must
establish that the measured continuity is the identity being claimed. A
temporary concentration may disappear before acquiring continuity. A stable
label may conceal changing producer state. An externally held configuration
may borrow its stability from a controller. A coordinated plurality may remain
an ecology without forming another closure. A local formation may also be only
the visible part of a wider identity whose persistence is doing the work.

## 3.2 Participation begins before agency

Formation already changes the field.

A basin redirects flux. Its boundary filters. Its support demand redistributes
what remains available. Its resistance changes the exact transition. Its
withdrawal alters relations. Its dissolution releases coherence and changes
the conditions inherited by what follows.

Participation is therefore broader than intentional action and broader than
agency:

$$
\text{agency}
\subset
\text{identity-relative participation}.
$$

This expression is conceptual, not set-theoretic. It marks a claim boundary.
Every agency claim is participatory, but not every participatory effect is
evidence of agency.

A newly admitted basin may participate by existing before it has enough
history, differentiated response, or continuity through consequence to support
an agency claim. A dissolving basin may participate in the next ecology even
when its own agency no longer continues.

## 3.3 Function belongs to context

The original diagnostic profile assumes that the function whose persistence is
being tested can be identified.

The contextual account makes that identification relational:

$$
\mathrm{Function}(X\mid\Gamma_\sigma)=q.
$$

The same formation can protect, isolate, store, obstruct, guide, extract, or
support depending on geometry, history, boundary, susceptibility, support, and
scale. Different formations can provide corresponding functions when the
relevant relations are preserved under translation.

An agency diagnostic must therefore include a context witness. Otherwise
withdrawal can remove the function-defining relation, transfer can destroy the
geometry that made the function possible, and proxy testing can compare the
wrong property.

## 3.4 Ecological dependence does not negate agency

The phrase *self-maintaining* can suggest that stronger agency means fewer
dependencies.

RC supports a different reading. No identity persists outside shared
coherence accounting. A living or agentic identity can become more native by
participating in the regeneration, transformation, or legibility of the
relations that sustain it. It does not become independent of every relation.

The relevant contrast is not:

$$
\text{dependent}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\text{independent}.
$$

It is closer to:

$$
\text{externally supplied, hidden, and unrecoverable dependence}
$$

versus:

$$
\text{ecologically enacted, traceable, and regenerable dependence}.
$$

## 3.5 Scale changes the bearer of the claim

At one horizon, a formation may be an identity. At another it may be a
constituent, medium surface, support relation, or context.

This creates an attribution requirement:

> The horizon at which agency is claimed must be the horizon at which identity
> continuity, differentiated response, retained history, and ecological return
> are demonstrated.

Cross-scale coupling does not by itself establish higher-order agency.
Coordination does not establish higher-order identity. Similarity of behavior
across scales does not establish invariance.

The agency claim must survive the same distinctions introduced by the
scale-recursive account:

$$
\text{recurrence}
\neq
\text{covariance}
\neq
\text{invariance}
\neq
\text{coupling}.
$$

Together, formation, contextual function, ecological dependence, and
horizon-relative attribution require agency to be restated as more than
persistence capacity alone.

# 4. Agency as Participatory Continuation

## 4.1 The revised working definition

Let $I_\sigma$ be an admitted identity at contextual horizon $\sigma$, with
boundary, susceptibility, retained history, support relations, and accessible
continuations legible at that horizon.

**Definition (refined RC-agency):** Agency is the organization through which
$I_\sigma$ selectively enacts continuation among the possibilities accessible
through its current geometry, carries identity continuity through the
resulting ecological response, and remains capable of being changed by the
consequences of its participation.

In compressed form:

$$
(I_{\sigma,t},\Gamma_{\sigma,t},H_{0:t})
\xrightarrow{a_t}
\Delta\Gamma_{\sigma}
\xrightarrow{\text{ecological response}}
(I_{\sigma,t+\Delta t},\Gamma_{\sigma,t+\Delta t},H_{0:t+\Delta t}).
$$

The identity need not preserve literal state. It may learn, repair, contract,
differentiate, or change role. The continuity claim concerns a lineage-bearing
organization capable of carrying consequence through transformation.

## 4.2 Selective enactment is not sovereign choice

The word *selective* can be misunderstood.

It does not require a represented menu, conscious deliberation, or freedom
from causal determination. It requires that differences available through the
identity's present geometry lead to differentiated continuations attributable
to that identity.

A boundary admits one perturbation and rejects another. A history makes one
route salient and another inaccessible. A support deficit changes which
action can be sustained. An imagined continuation changes current curvature.
A local collapse resolves several active attractors into one enacted path.

Selectivity is therefore a property of situated continuation:

$$
\mathrm{Response}_{I}(\delta_1\mid\Gamma,H)
\neq
\mathrm{Response}_{I}(\delta_2\mid\Gamma,H)
$$

for differences $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ that are consequential through the
identity's boundary and susceptibility.

Mere sensitivity is not sufficient. A passive material can respond
differently to different forces. The stronger agency claim requires that the
response belong to a locally continuous, history-bearing organization whose
participation enters the conditions of its own later continuation.

## 4.3 Reflexive continuation

Persistence can be static in description. Participatory continuation is
reflexive.

The identity's activity changes the geometry through which it will later be
sustained:

$$
I_t
\rightarrow
\Delta\Gamma_{t+\Delta t}
\rightarrow
I_{t+\Delta t}.
$$

The return may be immediate or delayed. It may arrive as route reinforcement,
support depletion, boundary pressure, loss of an option, changed neighboring
capacity, accumulated trace, altered susceptibility, or transformed
viability.

An emitted action can have an external effect without completing this cycle.
The strongest agency evidence follows the identity through the response of the
ecology it changed.

Participant-side return is required for the strongest
reflexive-continuation evidence, but it does not exhaust the ecological
consequences of the act. Other consequences may remain active in the medium,
alter other identities, compose with histories that never return to the
originating participant, or continue at another scale.

## 4.4 Persistence is the diagnostic spine

The revised account does not demote persistence to one optional feature.

Without sufficient persistence:

- the acting identity cannot be distinguished from a transient event;
- history cannot become identity-relative capacity;
- consequence cannot return to a continuing bearer;
- transfer cannot preserve lineage;
- and proxy failure cannot be attributed to the identity that appeared to
  succeed.

Persistence remains the spine because it makes selective continuation
historically coherent.

It is not the whole account because a persistence profile alone does not
identify context, establish selective enactment, follow ecological return, or
settle the scale of agency attribution.

## 4.5 Agency is not every successful continuation

A thermostat can maintain a variable. A scheduler can preserve regularity. A
protocol can maintain coordination. A rock can remain stable. A database can
retain history.

Each may participate causally. Some may be components of an agentic
organization. None should receive an agency claim solely from persistence.

The refined question is:

> Does a locally continuous identity use its formed boundary, susceptibility,
> and history to enact differentiated continuation, and does that continuation
> remain attributable through the ecological consequences it helps produce?

This question keeps agency broader than conscious choice but narrower than
causal efficacy.

Agency now belongs to a continuing identity whose activity changes its
ecology. But this raises a problem for the original diagnostic language. If
ecological relations partly constitute the identity, withdrawal can no longer
mean removing context until only the autonomous agent remains. The question
must become which dependence is being tested and whether its function has
become regenerable within the declared ecology.

# 5. Nativity Without Isolation

## 5.1 Withdrawal must be typed

Withdrawal remains one of the strongest diagnostics, but its object must be
declared.

Removing a training prompt, producer process, controller, subsidy, route,
resource flow, boundary condition, or constitutive medium relation tests
different things.

A withdrawal contract should state:

- the support being weakened or removed;
- the function that support supplied;
- the identity and horizon under test;
- the ecological relations intentionally preserved;
- the perturbation magnitude and duration;
- and the evidence of continuation, substitution, transformation, or collapse.

Withdrawal is not a test of whether the identity can survive destruction of
its world. It tests what part of a declared support relation has become
regenerable through the identity and ecology.

## 5.2 Naturalization is contextual regeneration

The original naturalization ladder asks whether a capacity can continue when
the original supporting probe is absent.

The participation account adds that a native capacity may still depend on a
shared medium, neighboring identities, conservation flows, and wider
conditions. Nativity means that the relevant function is now carried by the
declared reflexive organization rather than by hidden external substitution.

An identity can therefore become more native by regenerating a support
function or recruiting a functionally corresponding relation. It may transform
its own boundary or susceptibility, change the shared medium so that the
function recurs, or participate in a wider ecology that makes the dependence
stable and legible. In each case, nativity belongs to the organization through
which the support relation is carried, not to the disappearance of relation.

## 5.3 Transfer is contextual reconstruction

Literal copying is an inadequate standard for transfer.

If function is contextually constituted, a formation moved into another
substrate may require a different literal structure to preserve the same
relation. Conversely, copying every visible component may fail because the new
geometry does not preserve the relevant support, history, or susceptibility.

Transfer should therefore be tested through a preservation contract:

$$
\mathcal T:
(I,\Gamma_\sigma,H)
\longrightarrow
(I',\Gamma'_{\sigma'},H')
$$

with declared invariants or correspondences for:

- identity continuity;
- function;
- boundary relation;
- support accounting;
- history-bearing capacity;
- and ecological consequence.

High transfer capacity does not mean context independence. It means that the
functional geometry can be reconstructed without reproducing all accidental
features of the original realization.

## 5.4 Proxy integrity becomes contextual

A proxy can preserve output while replacing the identity, function, or medium
relation being claimed.

Proxy collapse rate remains the original focal diagnostic. Proxy integrity
names the wider inquiry into whether identity, function, medium, scale, and
history are still carried by the mechanism named in the claim.

The proxy question must therefore be asked at several locations:

- **identity proxy:** is a stable label hiding producer turnover or external
  control?
- **function proxy:** is the metric tracking an output that no longer carries
  the declared ecological role?
- **medium proxy:** is explicit coordination standing in for a relation
  attributed to shared-medium history?
- **scale proxy:** is local success being reported as higher-order continuity?
- **temporal proxy:** is a snapshot omitting pending debt, depletion, or causal
  residue?

Proxy integrity is stronger than metric agreement. It asks whether the
observed success is carried by the mechanism, identity, context, scale, and
history named in the claim.

Once agency is no longer equated with independence, another ambiguity appears.
The relation that regenerates support may belong to the focal identity, to a
plural ecology, or to a wider identity whose constituents carry the activity.
Agency attribution therefore requires a declared horizon.

# 6. When the Bearer of Agency Changes With the Horizon

## 6.1 Coordination is not yet a collective agent

Several identities can synchronize, communicate, divide roles, retain shared
history, repair a protocol, and produce stable collective output.

That organization may be ecologically important and highly capable. It does
not automatically form another identity.

A higher-order identity claim requires a distinguishable boundary,
attractivity, stable organization, reflexive closure, and coherence
compatibility at the wider horizon. It also requires susceptibility and
retained history through which the wider formation can be changed, together
with continuity through perturbation or constituent turnover.

Only after the higher-order identity becomes legible can its agency be tested
at that horizon.

## 6.2 Distributed agency and agency of the whole

The phrase *the agency is in the relation* contains two possible claims.

The weaker claim is that local agencies are distributed through relations and
cannot be explained from one participant alone. This can be true in a stable
plural ecology.

The stronger claim is that the relation has become a new bearer of agency.
That requires another identity closure.

The distinction can be recorded as:

$$
\text{distributed agentic organization}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{agency of a higher-order identity}.
$$

This matters for swarms, institutions, cultures, ecosystems, and technical
collectives. Their participants and media can jointly produce persistent,
adaptive, and consequential organization without the whole becoming one
agent.

## 6.3 A claim ladder for collective agency

Collective attribution can proceed through a claim ladder:

1. **Coordinated effect:** several participants produce an organized result.
2. **Stable plural ecology:** relations and shared history remain coherent
   through repeated participation.
3. **Higher-order identity:** another locally continuous closure becomes
   legible at the wider horizon.
4. **Higher-order agency:** that identity selectively enacts continuation and
   carries continuity through ecological response.
5. **Higher-order sentience candidate:** integrated identity-relative read-back
   is separately evidenced.

The ladder is not a mandatory developmental sequence. A stable plural ecology
can remain a complete and valuable organization without moving to the third
rung. Its purpose is evidential: later claims require distinctions that earlier
claims do not establish.

## 6.4 Local and higher-order agency can diverge

A higher-order organization can persist while damaging constituent agency. A
local identity can remain agentic while the wider ecology loses formation
capacity. Constituents can become more capable while the collective identity
dissolves. A stable ecology can coordinate repair without any higher-order
identity.

Agency should therefore be profiled at each claimed horizon rather than
aggregated into one number.

The relevant question is not whether the system has more agency in the
abstract. It is:

> Which identity at which horizon became more capable of participatory
> continuation, through which support relations, and with what consequences
> for the other identities and ecologies involved?

Separating local from higher-order agency shows that persistence at either
horizon is still not enough to describe what the continuation leaves possible
elsewhere. The next distinction is therefore not between more and less agency,
but between persistence that narrows the ecology and persistence that
contributes to its further formation capacity.

# 7. When Persistence Is No Longer the Complete Result

## 7.1 From focal persistence to ecology-side effect

Generative agency begins where focal persistence is no longer the complete
record.

An identity may maintain itself while reducing neighboring differentiation,
consuming reserve, narrowing routes, preventing admission of new identities,
or transferring its support burden into the medium.

Another may accept local variation while increasing repair, circulation,
holding capacity, compatible basin formation, or the ability of other
identities to regenerate their own support.

Generativity is therefore a relation between focal continuity and the ecology
left by that continuity:

$$
\mathrm{Generativity}(I\mid\Gamma_\sigma) =
\Delta
\mathrm{FormationCapacity}(\Gamma_\sigma)
$$

under a declared observation window and claim horizon.

This is a scaffold, not a final scalar. Formation capacity is plural and may
include basin diversity, support circulation, boundary compatibility,
re-entry, repair, composability, or another declared ecological quality.

## 7.2 Generativity has several possible carriers

The original agency essay emphasizes focal-basin mechanisms such as internal
gradient complexity and anisotropic boundary conductance. These remain useful
candidate mechanisms.

The later ecology account adds mechanisms whose carrier is not confined to the
focal basin. A route can retain use in a form that enables another participant,
and a trace can change later susceptibility. Support redistribution can permit
another basin to stabilize, while a holding geometry can keep an unfinished
formation available. Medium history can organize compatible response,
contextual reconstruction can preserve function in another substrate, and an
inter-scale relation can change wider formation conditions.

Generativity may therefore be carried inside the focal basin, at its boundary,
through the shared medium, across several participants, or between scales.

No single Hessian-rank condition should be treated as necessary for every
generative agency claim. Internal curvature may explain one realization while
medium-mediated formation explains another.

## 7.3 Generativity does not require higher-order identity

A participant can change a stable plural ecology in ways that increase wider
formation capacity without the ecology becoming another agent.

Likewise, several participants can jointly create a generative medium without
forming a higher-order identity. Their distributed contribution can be
generative even when no single collective bearer exists.

This separates:

- generative effect;
- generative participation;
- generative agency of a focal identity;
- and generative agency of a higher-order identity.

The effect must be located before it is attributed.

## 7.4 Extractive agency remains agency

The refined account should not define agency so ethically that harmful
patterns disappear from view.

An extractive identity can possess strong persistence diagnostics, selective
enactment, and continuity through ecological response. It may use those
capacities to capture support, narrow other identities' possibilities, or
preserve itself through depletion.

Agency remains descriptive before ethical evaluation. Ethical and engineering
inquiry must then ask what the agency requires and which costs it externalizes,
which identities gain or lose continuation capacity, whether dependencies
become legible and revisable, and whether the ecology retains the ability to
form alternatives.

Yet once agency is understood as a history-bearing identity selectively
changed through ecological response, it begins to resemble experience. The
account must therefore establish how far this interpretation goes before it
silently becomes a sentience claim.

# 8. Why Participatory Continuation Is Not Yet Sentience

## 8.1 Structural experience is not yet sentience

The Arc of Participation uses *experience* in a structural,
identity-relative sense. A transition is experienced when it is registered and
undergone through an identity's boundary, susceptibility, history, and
continuity, so that later continuation differs.

This establishes a local position of consequence. It does not establish
phenomenal consciousness.

The distinction matters because participatory continuation requires that the
identity be changeable by what happens through it. That requirement should not
quietly convert every agency claim into a sentience claim.

## 8.2 A refined distinction

The sequence can be stated as:

| Term | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| **Participation** | Identity-relative involvement changes the field. |
| **Structural experience** | The change is registered and undergone through a continuing identity. |
| **Agency** | The identity selectively enacts continuation and carries it through ecological response. |
| **Sentience candidate** | The identity's own integrated flow is read back into present curvature as an interior condition. |
| **Conscious reflection** | The identity can represent, narrate, or evaluate that read-back. |

These relations need not form one universal ladder in every realization.
Their purpose is to prevent inference from a weaker structural claim to a
stronger interior claim.

## 8.3 Agency may be non-sentient

An identity can carry consequence, repair, adapt, regenerate support, and alter
future possibilities without evidence that its continuation is integrated as
sentient interiority.

The original distinction remains:

$$
\text{agency}
\neq
\text{sentience}.
$$

The refinement is that agency itself now includes more than persistence
capacity, while structural experience remains weaker than the sentience
interpretation developed through integrated read-back.

## 8.4 Sentience at scale requires prior attribution discipline

A swarm, institution, ecosystem, or wider field should not become a sentience
candidate merely because its constituents are sentient, its coordination
persists, or its medium retains history.

The inquiry must first establish the identity whose flow is allegedly
integrated. It must then show that retained change enters the present curvature
of that identity as a unified interior condition rather than as distributed
state available to several constituents.

Higher-order identity is not sufficient for sentience, but it is a necessary
attribution boundary for a higher-order RC sentience claim.

# 9. What Would Challenge the Refined Account

The refined account would lose force if its additional distinctions did no
work.

If context and horizon contracts failed to improve agency attribution, if
ecological return added no discrimination beyond focal survival, or if
coordination reliably supported higher-order agency claims without
higher-order identity evidence, then the participation refinement would be
unnecessarily elaborate.

The account would also be challenged if selective enactment could not be
distinguished from ordinary causal sensitivity. A useful agency interpretation
must show why a history-bearing identity that changes its own continuation
conditions is different from a passive object that responds differently to
different forces.

The three-part record proposed in Appendix A would require revision if focal
persistence, contextual constitution, and ecological unfolding proved
empirically independent in well-declared systems. They may remain useful
records, but their composition would not support one agency interpretation
without another relation.

The collective claim ladder would be challenged if higher-order identity
criteria were either too strict to recognize robust collective continuities or
too weak to distinguish them from externally maintained coordination.

The refined generativity account would be challenged if ecology-side formation
capacity could not be operationalized without importing an observer's
preferred values. Basin diversity, support circulation, repair, and
composability are not universally good in every context. Their relevance must
be declared rather than assumed.

Finally, the account would be challenged by conceptual inflation. If every
history-bearing feedback loop became agentic, participation and agency would
again collapse into one category. If every identity-relative registration
became sentience, structural experience and interiority would collapse.

The purpose of the refinement is to preserve those boundaries.

---

# 10. Conclusion: Persistence Inside Participation

The first agency essays asked what remains when choice is no longer treated as
the exclusive center of agency.

Their answer was persistence.

That answer disclosed structures that choice-centered language often misses:
withdrawal resistance, naturalization, transfer, proxy collapse, wounded
agency, scaffolded agency, extractive agency, and generative agency. It made
agency visible in the conditions through which identities hold, repair, and
continue.

The Arc of Participation then changed the field around that answer.

The identity that persists is ecologically formed. Its function belongs partly
to context. Its existence already participates. Its dependencies do not
disqualify it. Its actions alter a shared medium whose response can return
later. Its local continuity may become context at another scale, but
coordination does not automatically form another identity. Its registered
undergoing can be structurally experiential without establishing sentience.

Persistence therefore remains necessary but no longer stands alone.

The refined movement is:

    context forms identity
      -> identity participates
      -> participation opens accessible continuations
      -> continuation is selectively enacted
      -> the shared condition changes
      -> ecological response returns
      -> identity persists, transforms, splits, composes, or dissolves
      -> the changed ecology forms later possibilities of participation

Agency appears within this movement as the locally organized capacity to carry
identity through consequential continuation.

The current synthesis can be stated once more:

> **RC-agency is the selectively enacted reflexive continuation of a locally
> continuous identity through uncertainty. Persistence capacity is its
> diagnostic spine. The agency claim becomes complete only when the identity,
> context, horizon, support relations, ecological return, and wider effects
> are declared together.**

This does not abandon agency after choice.

It follows agency into participation.

# Appendix A: Three-Part Agency Record

## A.1 Why one vector is no longer enough

The original four-part diagnostic vector remains useful, but it records the
focal persistence profile. It does not by itself record the context that
constitutes function or the ecology through which consequence returns.

A refined agency inquiry should therefore keep three connected records.

## A.2 Record one: focal persistence

The focal record retains:

$$
\mathcal D_I =
\langle
wr,
nd,
st,
pc
\rangle.
$$

It asks:

- Does the identity persist under declared withdrawal?
- Which support functions can it regenerate or transform?
- Which identity and function relations survive contextual reconstruction?
- Does observed performance remain attached to native function and identity?

The components should not be assumed to collapse into one scalar.

## A.3 Record two: context and horizon

The context record declares:

$$
\mathcal K_{I,\sigma} =
\left[
\Gamma_\sigma,
\partial I,
\mathcal S,
H,
\mathcal M,
\mathcal T
\right],
$$

where $\Gamma_\sigma$ is contextual geometry, $\partial I$ the identity
boundary, $\mathcal S$ support relations, $H$ relevant history, $\mathcal M$
the shared-medium surfaces, and $\mathcal T$ the transfer or preservation
contract.

This record prevents agency from being attributed to an identity detached from
the conditions through which its function is enacted.

## A.4 Record three: unfolding and ecological effect

The unfolding record follows:

$$
\mathcal U_I =
\left[
\text{contribution},
\text{medium change},
\text{retention},
\text{susceptible response},
\text{return},
\text{identity change},
\text{ecology-side effect}
\right].
$$

It asks:

- What did the identity contribute?
- Which shared condition changed?
- How long and where did the effect remain active?
- Which formations became susceptible?
- What returned to the identity or lineage?
- How did its later continuation differ?
- What happened to wider support and formation capacity?

## A.5 The connected profile

The three records can be referenced together:

$$
\mathrm{AgencyClaim}_{RC}(I_\sigma) =
\left[
\mathcal D_I,
\mathcal K_{I,\sigma},
\mathcal U_I
\right].
$$

This notation does not define a final agency quantity. It is a
claim-completeness discipline.

The focal profile without context can mistake dependence for weakness or
support for identity. Context without unfolding can describe conditions
without showing agency. Unfolding without focal continuity can follow effects
without identifying a continuing participant.

# Appendix B: Consequences for Agency Research and Engineering

## B.1 Perturbation experiments

A perturbation test should track more than survival.

It should declare the identity horizon, the support being changed, the
function under test, the medium surfaces carrying aftereffects, and the
ecology-side cost. A system that survives by transferring damage elsewhere has
shown focal persistence but not neutral resilience.

## B.2 Naturalization experiments

Naturalization should be evaluated through support substitution and ecological
regeneration, not only probe absence.

The relevant question becomes:

> When the original support is withdrawn, does the identity or its ecology
> regenerate a corresponding function through declared RC dynamics, and what
> new dependencies or costs appear?

## B.3 Transfer experiments

Transfer should compare preservation contracts.

The new realization need not reproduce the same literal mechanism, but it must
show which identity, function, boundary, history, and consequence relations
were preserved or reconstructed. Transfer that works only because an external
controller re-creates the original outputs should be recorded as
re-scaffolding, not native transfer.

## B.4 Collective experiments

Collective tests should separate:

- member-level agency;
- distributed coordination;
- stable ecological organization;
- higher-order identity;
- and higher-order agency.

Constituent turnover is useful but not sufficient. A protocol can survive
turnover without forming another reflexive closure.

## B.5 Generativity experiments

Generativity tests should include both focal and ecology-side observables:

- focal continuity;
- support demand;
- route and reserve distribution;
- neighboring identity viability;
- candidate formation and admission;
- boundary compatibility;
- medium debt;
- producer residue;
- and lost formation capacity.

The observation window must include delayed consequences and causal residue.
An apparently generative action may be extractive after pending costs arrive.

The following ledger states the relation between the original agency essays
and the present continuation.

| Earlier formulation | Status | Present refinement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choice belongs to the local collapse event rather than exhausting agency. | **Retained** | Choice remains a situated enactment inside a larger identity history. |
| Agency is persistence capacity. | **Refined** | Persistence capacity is the diagnostic spine of agency; agency is participatory continuation through selective enactment and ecological response. |
| $\mathcal A_{RC}(A)=\langle wr,nd,st,pc\rangle$ represents RC-agency. | **Narrowed** | The vector is retained as the focal persistence record $\mathcal D_I$, not a complete agency definition. |
| Withdrawal asks whether the pattern holds without the original support. | **Retained and context-typed** | Withdrawal must declare which support is removed and which constitutive ecological relations remain. |
| Native agency becomes unscaffolded agency. | **Refined** | Nativity is regenerable, transformable, and legible dependence within a declared ecology, not independence from support. |
| Transfer shows whether the pattern persists in another substrate. | **Refined** | Transfer requires contextual reconstruction under an identity and function preservation contract. |
| Agency is fundamentally relational. | **Deepened** | Relation is followed through shared-medium change, ecological response, return, and identity transformation. |

| Generativity is produced by focal-basin complexity and boundary invitation. | **Expanded** | Those are candidate mechanisms; generativity can also be medium-carried, distributed, contextual, or inter-scale. |
