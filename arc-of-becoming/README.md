# Arc Of Becoming

The `arc-of-becoming/` directory contains the phenomenological and
methodological branch of Geometric Reflexive Coherence. If the `core/` papers
ask what the coherence field is, these papers ask how a coherent system
experiences, describes, studies, and develops through irreducible change.

The arc moves from vocabulary to method:

```text
language -> reinforcement -> classification -> interrogation -> naturalization -> cultivation
```

Read together, the papers answer one question:

```text
How should an embedded observer understand and participate in becoming when the
next meaningful class cannot be fully known in advance?
```

## Paper Summaries

### [The Language of Becoming](2026-01-LanguageOfBecoming.md)

This paper translates Reflexive Coherence into a phenomenological vocabulary.
It asks what coherence geometry feels like from within: stability, collapse,
fragmentation, leakage, memory, abundance, communication, and understanding as
geometric phenomena rather than only psychological descriptions.

Its main contribution is the first language bridge between the formal RC loop
and lived or agentic experience. It proposes that intelligence, identity, and
communication can be understood as the dynamics of self-maintaining geometry.

### [Language of Becoming: Cultivation, Reinforcement, and the Legitimacy of Experience](2026-01-LoB-Reinforcement.md)

This paper extends the first Language of Becoming beyond crisis and collapse.
It focuses on abundance, reinforcement, basin deepening, cultivation-drive,
cooperation, and ecological intelligence. It distinguishes completion-drive
from cultivation-drive and argues that experience can be legitimate regime
evidence rather than noise.

Its main contribution is the positive regime: how coherent systems deepen,
cooperate, and explore when they are not trapped in scarcity or threat.

### [Classification of Becoming](2026-05-ClassificationOfBecoming.md)

This paper develops observation-first knowledge creation for nonlinear,
computationally irreducible systems. Instead of first asking whether a system
reached the predicted endpoint, it asks what property the system actually
expressed.

Its main contribution is methodological: failed predictions can still reveal
real classes. A non-promoting result can become useful when it exposes a
reusable, generative, coherence-preserving property.

### [Interrogation of Becoming](2026-05-InterrogationofBecoming.md)

This paper adds bounded probes after classification. A probe is not a proof and
not a broad parameter search. It is a controlled question to the system: what
boundary, support, or missing relation makes a property possible?

Its main contribution is the probe discipline: classify probe results at the
lowest valid rung, compare with controls, weaken or withdraw support, and ask
whether the property can become native.

### [Naturalization of Becoming](2026-05-NaturalizationOfBecoming.md)

This paper asks when a property first expressed under external support becomes
available through the system's own regime. It distinguishes probe-exposed
capacity from native regime expression.

Its main contribution is the bridge between interrogation and cultivation:
naturalization is not simply making a probe more precise. It asks whether the
system can generate the function of the probe for itself.

### [Cultivation of Becoming](2026-05-CultivationOfBecoming.md)

This paper synthesizes the arc into a practical method for irreducible change.
The method moves through seven movements: orient, observe, classify, probe,
withdraw, naturalize, and integrate.

Its main contribution is the full stance of cultivation: disciplined
participation without forcing premature closure, optimizing proxies, or
confusing supported expression with native development.

## Conceptual Progression

The arc can be read as a method stack:

```text
Language of Becoming:
  describe regimes and experiences.

Reinforcement:
  distinguish scarcity/completion from abundance/cultivation.

Classification:
  name what appeared before judging endpoint success.

Interrogation:
  ask bounded questions through probes.

Naturalization:
  test whether supported capacity becomes endogenous.

Cultivation:
  participate through the full cycle without forcing closure.
```
