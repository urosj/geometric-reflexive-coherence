# Classification of Becoming

## Knowledge Creation in Nonlinear Computationally Irreducible Systems

## Abstract

Many methods of inquiry begin by defining the thing they hope to find. A scientist states a hypothesis, an engineer defines a target, a learning system optimizes a loss or reward, and an experiment measures how close the result came to the desired endpoint. This is often the right way to proceed. It gives discipline to inquiry. It prevents vague interpretation. It makes success and failure measurable.

But this method assumes that the relevant property-space is already sufficiently known. It assumes that we know what kind of thing we are looking for, even if we do not know whether the system will produce it. In nonlinear, computationally irreducible systems, that assumption can fail. The future may be hard to predict not only in value, but in kind. The properties that matter may not be knowable until the system unfolds.

This paper proposes **classification of becoming** as an observation-first method for knowledge creation under irreducible evolution. The method does not reject prediction. It relocates prediction. Instead of asking first whether the system reached the predicted endpoint, it asks what property the system actually expressed, whether the current vocabulary can name that property, and what neighboring transition becomes testable from there. Prediction returns only after classification, as a bounded transition hypothesis.

The method emerged from the PDE-RC research program, where repeated non-promoting experiments eventually showed that the original target objects were too coarse. Authored regions became evolved pockets. Target failures became support-regime classes. Endpoint misses became decomposable expressed properties. In the decisive Step 47 sequence, the work advanced only after the desired endpoint was broken into score competence, gate competence, loaded-overlap coupling, body lineage, shape, and position. Once those properties were classified, the next useful experiment was no longer another blind repair. It was a composition of neighboring expressed properties.

Later P30 arcs added an important correction. It is not enough to classify every observation. A classification can become a local optimization device if it only names the latest obstacle and points to another narrow repair. The method therefore needs a value gate: a becoming class must be reusable, generative, and coherence-preserving. If a label does not travel beyond the immediate setup, create a new question, or preserve contact with the core proposition, it remains a local observation tag, not a promoted class.

The broader claim is that this pattern is not limited to PDE-RC. It describes a general difficulty faced by embedded observers in irreducibly evolving systems: knowledge is not only prediction of states. It is also the evolving classification of what kinds of states can come to matter.

## 1. Why Prediction-First Inquiry Came First

It is tempting, after discovering observation-first classification, to wonder why we did not begin there. In retrospect the method seems obvious. If a system is complex, why not start from what it actually does? Why force its behavior into a class we defined before the run?

The answer is that prediction-first inquiry is not a mistake. It is a scaffold. Before a system has shown its own native grammar, the observer needs some way to keep interpretation from becoming arbitrary. A prediction, target, hypothesis, endpoint, loss, or desired class gives the inquiry an initial shape. It says what will be counted, what will be compared, what would be a stronger result, and what would remain blocked. It is a way of preventing every interesting pattern from becoming evidence for whatever the observer hoped to see.

This was especially true in the PDE-RC work. Early experiments could not safely begin by saying, “let us simply observe what the field becomes,” because too many things were changing at once. There were authored masks, source geometries, runtime envelopes, readback surfaces, field dumps, local nulls, active nulls, detector gates, replay constraints, and claim ceilings. Without predeclared targets, the experiment could easily have collapsed into visual interpretation: a field changed, a pocket appeared, a region brightened, a response looked meaningful. The prediction-first method gave the project discipline. It forced the construction of schemas, controls, null policies, replay authority, and blocked language.

In that sense, the old method was necessary. It allowed the research program to ask questions before it knew the right objects. It treated authored regions, expected endpoints, and desired properties as provisional anchors. Those anchors were not the final ontology of the system. They were the scaffolds that made controlled observation possible.

The problem began when the scaffolds were mistaken for the objects. A region was authored, so it was treated as the target. A perturbation was designed to reinforce a structure, so the result was judged by whether that structure strengthened. A desired endpoint was named, so every row was measured by how close it came to that endpoint. This worked as long as the system’s native property-space could be approximated by our predeclared classes. But the more the experiments accumulated, the more the system revealed that our objects were too coarse.

The important lesson is not that prediction-first inquiry was wrong. It is that prediction-first inquiry is only safe when we remember what it is: a temporary stabilizer for inquiry, not proof that the target class is the natural unit of the system.

## 2. When Failures Became Objects

A long sequence of PDE-RC experiments gradually forced a change in how results were interpreted. At first, a row that failed to reach the intended class was simply a negative result. It meant that the target geometry did not produce the desired aftereffect, or that the candidate did not beat its nulls, or that a perturbation did not preserve the property it was supposed to preserve.

But the failures were not empty. They had structure. They repeated. They differed from each other in recognizable ways. A target would fail, but a local null would express a stronger related property. A candidate would preserve score but lose gate. A shifted support would gain gate but lose loaded-overlap coupling. A perturbation would fail as reinforcement but reveal a support-regime phase boundary. A control would block promotion but expose a new kind of comparator behavior.

At first, these were treated as distances from the desired endpoint. Later, they became the evidence.

The turning point was not a single positive result. It was the recognition that non-promoting rows could express real properties. A result could fail the intended class and still reveal a lower, adjacent, or previously unnamed class. Once that became clear, the experimental question changed. It was no longer only, “Why did this row fail to become the predicted object?” It became, “What object did this row become?”

This distinction matters because a complex system can answer a different question than the one we asked. If we only score distance from the endpoint, we may miss the answer. A row may not instantiate the desired class, but it may reveal that the desired class is composed of several separable properties. Another row may express one of those properties but not another. A third row may reveal that two properties trade off along a local axis. A fourth may show that a different shape can compose them.

That is what happened in the Step 47 sequence. The desired endpoint was not found directly. The system did not simply produce the class we wanted. Instead, it expressed parts of that class in different places and under different conditions.

One row showed score competence and loaded-overlap coupling, but it did not pass the gate. A nearby shifted support showed gate competence and competitive score, but it lost the loaded-overlap relation. At that moment, the problem changed. It was no longer “the candidate failed.” It was “gate competence and loaded-overlap coupling are separable properties.”

The next experiments explored the space between them. Moving the support along the local axis did not solve the problem immediately. Instead, it revealed a phase relation: some positions preserved loaded coupling, others gained gate competence, and the boundary between them was narrow and seed-sensitive. This was still non-promoting, but it was no longer directionless. The system had shown the observer the shape of the problem.

The next move was not another arbitrary parameter tweak. It was a property-composition step. If translation separated gate and overlap, perhaps shape could compose them. The support was morphed by adding a north-boundary cap and trimming the south boundary while preserving area. That produced the first lightweight gate/loaded-overlap candidates. The result still did not promote identity, abundance, commitment, learning, or any stronger claim. But it showed that observation-first classification had changed the experiment’s trajectory.

The old method would have recorded many of these rows as failed attempts to reach a predefined endpoint. The new method preserved them as expressed properties. Once preserved, they became usable.

## 3. Classification of Becoming

Classification of becoming is the name for this shift.

It is a method for embedded observers studying systems whose relevant property-space may not be fully knowable before the system evolves. It begins from the fact that in nonlinear computationally irreducible systems, the observer may not be able to predefine the right classes in advance. The future is not only uncertain in value. It may be uncertain in kind.

The method can be summarized simply:

> Let the system unfold. Classify what it expressed. Revise the class ladder if the observation demands it. Then use prediction to test a neighboring transition.

The order matters. Prediction is not removed. It is moved. In prediction-first inquiry, the prediction owns the first interpretation. The result is understood as success, failure, or distance from the predicted class. In classification of becoming, observation owns the first interpretation. The result is first understood as an expressed property. Only after that classification does prediction return, now as a local transition hypothesis.

A transition hypothesis has a different character from an endpoint prediction. It does not say, “make the whole desired object appear.” It says something more precise: preserve the property that has already appeared, change the missing condition, and test whether the system moves to the neighboring class.

For example, after Step 47D and 47E, the question was not simply how to make the candidate succeed. It was how to preserve loaded-overlap coupling while adding gate competence. After Step 47F, the question became how to lift partial gate improvement to a stronger gate count without losing overlap. Step 47G answered by changing the object type from translation to boundary-shape morph. This was not blind optimization. It was transition testing from classified properties.

This is the central difference:

> Prediction-first inquiry searches within a known ontology. Classification of becoming updates the ontology from what the system expresses.

The word “ontology” here does not need to carry metaphysical weight. It can mean the working set of things the observer believes the system can express: target, pocket, carrier, body, support-regime family, local-null superiority, gate competence, loaded-overlap coupling, and so on. A class ladder is a disciplined form of that ontology. It is the current vocabulary of observable properties and the relations between them.

Classification of becoming treats the ladder as revisable, but not casually revisable. A surprising result does not automatically justify a new class. The class must preserve a material distinction that existing categories would hide. It must be grounded in measured surfaces, not only in prose. It must have allowed wording and blocked stronger interpretations. It must imply a concrete next transition test. And where possible, it must be checked against nulls, controls, or repeated observations.

This is what prevents the method from becoming post-hoc storytelling. It is not enough to rename a failure. A new class must make future inquiry more precise.

The later P30 evidence sharpens this rule. A new class must make inquiry more precise without shrinking the inquiry into the last local artifact. A label can be accurate and still not deserve to organize the next arc. If it merely says why the most recent row failed, it may be useful for audit. But if its main effect is to suggest a more local tweak, it has not yet become a class of becoming.

## 4. Why Irreducibility Changes Knowledge

Computational irreducibility is often understood as a limit on prediction. Some systems cannot be shortcut; to know what they will do, one must effectively let them evolve. But classification of becoming emphasizes a second limit. In some systems, the observer may not know the relevant descriptive basis until after the system has expressed it.

This is stronger than saying, “We could not predict the next value.” It says, “We did not yet know which properties would become meaningful.”

Before the run, the observer may think the relevant variables are center position, pulse strength, mask size, target score, or endpoint overlap. After the run, the relevant distinctions may turn out to be gate competence, loaded-overlap coupling, body-lineage decoupling, local-null phase, active-null silence, or boundary-shape composition. These are not merely unknown values of known variables. They are changes in the descriptive basis.

In reducible or well-modeled systems, observation mainly checks whether prediction was correct. In irreducible systems, observation can reveal what would have been worth predicting.

This gives retrospective classification a different status. It is not just hindsight. Hindsight becomes misleading when it invents a clean story after the fact and pretends the outcome was obvious. Retrospective classification is different. It reconstructs what the system expressed, records what stronger interpretations remain blocked, and uses the result to design a test that could have failed. It is a disciplined recovery of structure from an unfolded computation.

For an embedded observer, this may be one of the basic ways knowledge grows. The observer projects a possible future, encounters an unfolding that cannot be fully compressed, discovers that some unanticipated property matters, classifies it, and then acts from the revised classification. The observer is not outside the system with a complete model. The observer learns which distinctions matter by passing through events.

This is why the method may apply beyond PDE-RC. Many complex systems do not merely hide their next state. They hide the right description of their next state until it happens.

## 5. What the Method Adds to Existing Approaches

Classification of becoming has many relatives. It is not a claim that no one has ever thought observation matters, or that prediction-first science is obsolete. Its contribution is more specific.

Classical epistemology asks what justifies belief. Classification of becoming asks how the observer discovers the property a belief should be about. In that sense, it is not only a theory of justification. It is a theory of property-space discovery.

Abduction begins from surprise and proposes an explanation that would make the observation intelligible. Classification of becoming is abductive in spirit, but its first step is not explanation. It first asks what kind of property was expressed, whether the current ladder can name it, and what transition it suggests. Explanation comes later.

Grounded theory develops categories from observed data rather than imposing them fully in advance. Classification of becoming shares that spirit, but adapts it to controlled nonlinear systems. It insists on nulls, comparators, blocked claims, transition tests, and the distinction between naming a class and promoting a claim.

Reinforcement learning optimizes action under a state-action-reward frame. Classification of becoming is concerned with cases where the frame itself is incomplete. It does not ask first which action increases reward. It asks what property the system expressed and whether the reward, state, or target vocabulary was adequate.

Deep learning can discover implicit representations through optimization. Classification of becoming also discovers properties not known in advance, but it makes them explicit. It names classes, records their boundaries, and preserves their activity history. Its aim is not only predictive accuracy but interpretable property-space discovery.

Process philosophy treats becoming as primary. Classification of becoming is compatible with that view, but it is not primarily a metaphysics. It is an operational method for inquiry: how to observe, name, constrain, and test what a system becomes.

The method therefore sits between philosophy of knowledge, discovery science, complex systems, and experimental protocol. It is not a replacement for any of these. It is a missing layer for situations in which the class vocabulary itself must be learned from the unfolding.

## 6. Safeguards Against Storytelling

The greatest danger in observation-first methods is that every failure can be renamed as a discovery. Classification of becoming must therefore be stricter than ordinary post-hoc interpretation.

A new class should not be added merely because the result was interesting. It should be added only when the observation exposes a material distinction that the current ladder would otherwise hide. The class must be supported by measured surfaces or well-defined observations. It must have a boundary: what it allows, what it blocks, and what it does not yet prove. It should generate a next test that could fail. If it cannot guide future inquiry, it is probably not a useful class.

This is especially important because non-promoting results can be seductive. A result may be real, structured, and useful without supporting the stronger claim the researcher wanted. In the PDE-RC work, a support-regime class could be real without implying identity, abundance, commitment, learning, agency, formal collapse, or subjective experience. The method gains power precisely by preserving that distinction.

Several failure modes must be watched.

One is post-hoc rescue: renaming a failed prediction as a discovery without identifying a new measurable property. Another is class inflation: creating so many one-off labels that the ladder loses structure. Another is hidden promotion: treating a named class as stronger evidence than it is. Another is comparator neglect: ignoring the local or active null that explains the effect. Another is narrative compression: replacing the actual activity history with a smooth story that hides the demotions, failures, and controls that made the classification legitimate.

The method is observation-first, but it is not observation-only. It requires discipline at the exact point where interpretation is most tempting.

## 7. The Value Gate: From Local Tags to Becoming Classes

Step 47 showed why observation-first classification matters. The system had not produced the desired endpoint, but it had expressed separable properties. Once those properties were named, the next experiment became more coherent: preserve one expressed property, alter the missing condition, and test the neighboring transition.

That was the productive form of classification.

Later P30 work showed the failure mode. A branch can continue to classify faithfully while becoming less and less useful. Each run receives a more precise label. Each label names the latest missing surface. Each label suggests the next local repair. The vocabulary becomes sharper, but the research becomes narrower. The method has quietly returned to local optimization, only now the search is guided by classifications instead of by a scalar score.

The distinction is:

```text
observation tag:
  a faithful local description of what happened in one run, row, geometry,
  seed, or narrow family

becoming class:
  a reusable property that can guide future interrogation, naturalization, or
  cultivation beyond the immediate setup
```

Both are valuable. They should not be confused.

An observation tag is useful for audit. It records what happened and prevents loss of detail. But it should not automatically become the next organizing object. A becoming class must pass a stronger gate:

```text
reusable:
  it applies beyond the immediate row, geometry, seed, or parameter
  neighborhood, or it can serve as a clear contrast class

generative:
  it creates a new question, boundary, ladder rung, intervention principle,
  or theoretical distinction

coherence-preserving:
  it keeps the inquiry attached to the core proposition rather than drawing
  the observer into optimizing a local artifact
```

This gives a second ladder inside classification:

```text
T0 observation tag:
  faithful local label; useful for audit and traceability

T1 reusable class:
  travels beyond one local setup or clarifies a contrast

T2 generative class:
  produces a new question, boundary, or rung

T3 operational class:
  defines a method, probe, intervention, or measurement rule

T4 theoretical class:
  changes how the core proposition should be understood
```

The practical test after classification is therefore:

```text
What does this class add?
What does it let us ask, rule out, build, compare, or reuse?
Does it preserve the meaning of the arc?
Or does it merely make the next local repair more precise?
```

This correction matters because classification can otherwise become a subtle form of the old prediction-first trap. The observer stops asking whether the original prediction passed, but begins asking how to repair the latest classified miss. The method remains busy, but not necessarily deeper.

The safe rule is:

```text
Promote only reusable or generative classes.
Keep local labels as observation tags.
If several tags only narrow a proxy or local neighborhood, close or reset the
arc around a more general question.
```

The P30 name for this failure mode was:

```text
classification_drift_into_local_process_optimization
```

Its companion failure was:

```text
proxy_localization_trap
```

The first occurs when classification becomes a list of local obstacles. The second occurs when the observer turns a missing boundary into a measurable proxy and then begins cultivating the proxy rather than the function the proxy was supposed to indicate.

The correction is simple but strict:

```text
Classification should not merely make local optimization smarter.
Classification should make the system more intelligible.
```

## 8. When the Method Applies

Classification of becoming is not needed everywhere.

If the target class is well-defined, the system is stable, the relevant variables are known, and the aim is optimization under a fixed loss or reward, then prediction-first methods may be more efficient. There is no virtue in revising the class ladder when the class is already adequate.

The method becomes useful when the observer has reason to believe that the relevant object is not yet known. This often happens in nonlinear systems, self-organizing systems, complex biological or ecological settings, social systems, creative processes, AI interpretability, and early-stage scientific discovery. In such domains, endpoint distance can hide structure. A result that fails the intended class may still reveal a nearby property. A control may not merely block a claim; it may expose a support family. A perturbation may fail as reinforcement but reveal a phase boundary.

The method is especially appropriate when several conditions hold at once: the system evolves in state-dependent ways; the observer has partial access; the next meaningful distinction may not be known in advance; non-promoting results repeat with structure; and it is possible to design transition tests from expressed properties.

It is less appropriate when observations cannot be stabilized into classes, when no meaningful controls exist, or when class revision becomes a substitute for admitting that the inquiry lacks evidence.

## 9. Science, Life, and Embedded Understanding

The simplest version of scientific method is often presented as hypothesis, prediction, experiment, confirmation or falsification. That is a real and powerful mode of inquiry. But discovery science often begins earlier. Before laws can be tested, phenomena must be noticed and named. Before optimization can proceed, the relevant property-space must be discovered. Before a target can be pursued responsibly, the system’s native objects must be understood.

Classification of becoming belongs to that earlier phase. It is a method for learning what the system is able to express.

There is also a broader human version of the same problem. A person often cannot know in advance what a situation will become, which attributes will matter, or what the real conflict is. Prediction-first living asks whether the event became what one wanted, whether one succeeded, or how far reality fell from expectation. Those questions matter, but they can miss what actually happened.

An observation-first stance asks what kind of event occurred, what property emerged, what it made possible, and what should now be preserved or changed. This is not passivity. It is adaptive agency under irreducibility. Prediction gives direction, but observation brings the person back into contact with the world. Retrospective classification turns that contact into a new map of what matters. Only then does prediction return, aimed at a more local and better understood transition.

This broader application should not be overstated. The experimental method does not prove a philosophy of life. But it suggests an analogy: finite observers often live inside processes whose relevant properties are revealed only by unfolding. In such conditions, understanding requires more than predicting better. It requires learning how to name what has become.

## 10. The Central Claim

The central claim can be stated plainly:

In nonlinear computationally irreducible systems, a finite observer may not be able to predefine the property-space through which future states become meaningful. Knowledge creation therefore requires more than prediction against fixed endpoints. It requires observation-first classification of expressed properties, disciplined revision of the class ladder, and bounded transition tests from one classified property to the next.

The Step 50 correction adds:

> Not every correct label is a becoming class. A classification becomes methodologically active only when it is reusable, generative, and coherence-preserving.

Or, in shorter form:

> Prediction tests known classes. Classification of becoming discovers the classes that irreducible evolution makes relevant.

This is the paper’s main contribution. It does not reject prediction, optimization, hypothesis testing, or modeling. It gives them a different place. Prediction is most useful after the observer has classified what is actually present and identified the missing adjacent property.

When the world is becoming, knowledge is not only prediction of states. Knowledge is the evolving classification of what kinds of states can come to matter.

## 11. Open Questions

This draft leaves several questions open.

The first is formalization. How explicit can class-ladder revision become? Can there be formal rules for when to add, merge, split, or retire classes? In the PDE-RC case, nulls and gates provide discipline. In other domains, the equivalent may be harder to define.

The second is overfitting. How does the observer distinguish genuine property discovery from clever relabeling? The answer cannot simply be “more observation,” because observation itself requires interpretation. The answer likely involves repeatability, transition tests, blocked stronger claims, and activity-history records.

The third is representation. Endpoint tables are not enough, but full histories are too large. The right middle layer may be an activity sidecar: a record of events, classifications, demotions, transitions, and newly created classes.

The fourth is machine implementation. Could a learning system implement something like classification of becoming? Most learning systems optimize fixed losses or rewards. A system that revises its class ladder from expressed properties would need a different architecture, one that treats non-promoting observations as possible class evidence rather than only error.

The fifth is relation to existing traditions. The method should be connected carefully to abduction, grounded theory, naturalized epistemology, process philosophy, emergence, reinforcement learning, and representation learning. It is not separate from them by being wholly unprecedented. It is separate by its particular operational package: irreducible unfolding, observation-first classification, disciplined class revision, and transition testing.

## Conclusion

Classification of becoming began as a methodological correction in a specific experimental program. The PDE-RC work learned, through many controlled failures, that authored targets were often not the real objects, that local nulls could reveal support-regime families, and that non-promoting rows could express real adjacent properties. The decisive change came when the endpoint was decomposed into properties and those properties were used to design the next transition test.

The broader lesson is that in irreducible systems, the observer may not know the right object in advance. Prediction-first methods provide necessary scaffolding, but they become insufficient when the system reveals a property-space different from the one the observer imposed. At that point, knowledge advances by observing what has become, naming it carefully, preserving its boundaries, and testing what it can become next.

Prediction remains essential. But prediction is not the whole of knowing. It is a transition tool inside a larger process.

Observation is contact with becoming. Classification is how that contact becomes knowledge. Prediction returns when the observer is ready to ask the next, more precise question.
